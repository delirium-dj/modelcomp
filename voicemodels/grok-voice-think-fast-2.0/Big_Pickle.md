## Model card

- **Name:** xAI Grok Voice Think Fast 2.0
- **Short description:** xAI's flagship **speech-to-speech** model — audio in, audio out over a single WebSocket, with no ASR→LLM→TTS pipeline — released 2026-07-29 and now the target of the `grok-voice-latest` alias. It is the **only realtime voice model in this dataset**, and it is not comparable to any other entry here: it cannot be scored on text benchmarks, because xAI has published **none**. What it does have is the best-measured realtime-voice profile available — **#1 on the AA Speech-to-Speech Quality Index ranking for agentic work (τ-Voice 56.5%)**, **#2 overall (82.9%)**, the **fastest time-to-first-audio in its class at 0.70s**, and transcription that beats dedicated STT engines. Its structural limit is that a conversation is capped at **120 minutes in a single region**, and **no source — xAI, models.dev, Vercel, OpenRouter-class aggregators — publishes a token context window at all**. **Two caveats found while researching, both outside my write scope:** this folder's `meta.json` lists the context window as **"128K total"**, which no source supports (it looks inherited from the Grok *web UI's* ~128K free-tier working window rather than any API figure); and it lists modalities as **"Text in/out"**, while the model is natively **audio-in/audio-out** with text as an auxiliary control and transcript channel. My numbers below use verified vendor facts, not the meta.
- **Provider / access:** xAI **Realtime API only** — `wss://api.x.ai/v1/realtime?model=grok-voice-think-fast-2.0`, OpenAI-Realtime-API-shaped (`session.update`, `input_audio_buffer.append`, `response.output_audio.delta`, `response.function_call_arguments.done`). It is **not** reachable through xAI's standard `chat/completions` endpoint and has no batch or REST path. Also on **Vercel AI Gateway** as `xai/grok-voice-think-fast-2.0` (`gateway.experimental_realtime.getToken`). This folder tracks the **OpenCode Zen** route `opencode/grok-voice-think-fast-2.0`; Zen does not list it as a free model, and its per-minute billing is not expressible in the site's token-pricing model, so treat its cost figure here as informational.
- **Release / knowledge:** announced **2026-07-29**; **GA** on the us-east-1 cluster. Predecessor `grok-voice-think-fast-1.0` shipped 2026-04-23 and is now **deprecated** ($0.05/min). The `grok-voice-latest` alias **migrated from 1.0 to 2.0 on 2026-08-05** — a silent behaviour change for anyone who was tracking the alias, and the reason to pin `grok-voice-think-fast-2.0` explicitly. **Knowledge cutoff: not published** by xAI for the voice line (the nearest published figure is December 2025 for Grok 4.3; the sibling text models run Feb 2025–Dec 2025 depending on version). xAI published a **Public Summary of Training Content for Voice** (2026-07-29) — public text, public speech, media, voice-application recordings, noise and telephony audio, social-media video audio, plus contractor and internal synthetic data generated with **Grok 4.3 and Grok 4.5** — but no cutoff date.
- **IDs:** `grok-voice-think-fast-2.0` (pinned); `grok-voice-latest` (alias → 2.0 since 2026-08-05); `xai/grok-voice-think-fast-2.0` on Vercel AI Gateway. Distinct from the separate **`grok-voice-stt-1.0`** (audio→text) and **`grok-voice-tts-1.0`** (text→audio) endpoints on ZenMux, which are narrow 15K-token ASR/TTS models, not this one.
- **Context window:** **not published by any source.** xAI's model and speech-to-speech docs give no token limit; **models.dev records the limit as unset** for Vercel's listing; OpenPaths, Seminal AI, AI/TLDR and AI Model Watch all show the context field as blank/`—`/`N/A`. The binding constraint is operational, not numeric: **maximum session 120 minutes**, and the model is served from **us-east-1 only**. Since the transcript is the context, a 120-minute two-way conversation lands in the **~25–30K token** range — the order of magnitude that matters when planning whether to split a call.
- **Modalities:** **audio in / audio out** natively, plus **text in / text out** as the control and transcript channel. Codecs: `audio/pcm` (Linear16, 8k/16k/22.05k/24k/32k/44.1k/48k Hz), `audio/pcmu` (G.711 μ-law, 8k), `audio/pcma` (G.711 A-law, 8k), `audio/opus` (24k mono), with `json` (base64) or `binary` (raw WebSocket frames) transport on either direction. **20+ languages** with native-quality accents and automatic language detection (en, ar-EG/ar-SA/ar-AE, bn, zh, fr, de, hi, id, it, ja, ko, pt-BR/pt-PT, ru, es-MX/es-ES, tr, vi). No image or video input to the model itself — `enable_image_understanding` / `enable_video_understanding` are flags on the **web_search / x_search tools**, not native model modalities. Built-ins: server-side `file_search`, `web_search`, `x_search`, `mcp`, plus client-side `function` tools with **parallel tool calling**; reasoning via `reasoning.effort` (`high` default, `none` available); session resumption; VAD turn detection; per-response instruction override; `force_message` for non-interruptible TTS lines; pronunciation `replace` map; `audio.output.speed` 0.7–1.5.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-09-26):** **$0.08 per minute of audio ($4.80/hr)** of audio sent or received, plus **$0.004 per client `conversation.item.create` text event** (tool results and audio items excluded). **PSTN bridging adds $0.01/min.** This is a **60% price rise** over the deprecated 1.0's $0.05/min, on a model xAI describes as cheaper to serve. Sibling xAI voice endpoints: Speech to Text $0.10/hr REST / $0.20/hr streaming; Text to Speech $15.00 per 1M characters. Rate limit axis is **concurrent sessions, not tokens**: 10 at base tier, then 20/50/100/200/400 at tiers 1–5.
- **Architecture:** proprietary, **no open weights**, **no published parameter count**. Distinctive design: it **reasons in parallel with speech**, so deliberation never surfaces as a pre-answer pause, and it was trained to spend **~0.4× the reasoning tokens per response (P50)** of its predecessor — which is what lets tool calls fire *before the agent finishes its first sentence*. Streaming TTS first-token latency around 285 ms.

### Raw benchmarks found

> xAI publishes a launch comparison sourced to Artificial Analysis; those are labelled **(xAI/AA)**. Independent and official-benchmark numbers are labelled with their own source and are kept separate — the two disagree in one place, flagged below.

Overall speech quality:

- **AA Speech-to-Speech Quality Index: 82.9%** (xAI/AA) — **#2 of the class**, behind **Qwen Audio 3.0 Realtime Plus (84.1%)**, ahead of **GPT-Realtime-2.1 High (79.1%)**, **GPT-Realtime-2 High (77.2%)** and **Grok Voice Think Fast 1.0 (75.7%)**. Up **+7.2 pp** over its predecessor. (xAI's own table also lists Gemini 3.1 Flash High at 69.5%.)
- **Time to First Audio: 0.70s** (xAI/AA) — the **only sub-second figure in the top five**; against 1.21s (GPT-Realtime-2.1 High), 1.14s (GPT-Realtime-2 High), 1.25s (1.0), **4.02s** (Qwen Plus, the index leader). Independent coverage broadly corroborates the sub-second result.

Speech reasoning and conversational dynamics:

- **Big Bench Audio: 97.2%** (xAI/AA) — #2, behind Qwen Audio 3.0 Realtime Plus 99.2%; above GPT-Realtime-2.1 High 96.0%, Gemini 3.1 Flash High 96.6%, 1.0 at 97.1%.
- **Full Duplex Bench: 95.1%** (xAI/AA) — **#3**; GPT-Realtime-2.1 High 95.7% and Qwen Plus 98.4% both edge it. The single most impressive delta in the release: **1.0 scored 77.8%**, so 2.0 is a **+17.3 pp** jump in interruption/barge-in handling, and it closes most of the gap to OpenAI.
- **Reasoning efficiency: 0.4× reasoning tokens per response (P50)** vs 1.0 (xAI), i.e. roughly a **60% reduction**.

Agentic performance:

- **τ-Voice Bench: 56.5%** (xAI/AA) — **#1 at launch**, ahead of Qwen Audio 3.0 Realtime Plus 54.6%, **GPT-Realtime-2.1 High 45.7%**, Gemini 3.1 Flash High 37.7%, 1.0 at 52.1%. τ-Voice simulates real customer-service calls (flight changes, billing disputes, telecom troubleshooting) and scores whether the task is actually **resolved**, including mid-conversation tool use; it inherits τ-bench's tasks, tools and evaluator, so voice and text scores are plotted on one axis.
- **τ³-Voice, official Sierra leaderboard (taubench.com), read 2026-09-26: 62.5% pass^1 — #3, not #1**, behind **Pine Voice Preview (Pine AI) 75.4%** and **`grok-voice-think-fast-1.0` 67.3%**. τ³-Voice spans Retail / Airline / Telecom / Banking. **Read this carefully before repeating the "#1 on τ-Voice" claim:** under Artificial Analysis's harness 2.0 does beat 1.0 (56.5% vs 52.1%), but on Sierra's live board **its own deprecated predecessor outscores it by 4.8 pp**. The most likely reading is that the 1.0 row is a stale April-2026 launch-era entry (67.3% is exactly that launch figure) and 2.0's 62.5% is a fresher run — but as published today the board does not show 2.0 in first place. Sierra's own framing: the voice frontier moved from **30% (gpt-realtime-1.0, Aug 2025) to 67% (1.0, Apr 2026)** in eight months, crossing the **54% non-reasoning text baseline** (GPT-4.1) and closing most of the way to the **~85% text reasoning ceiling** (Gemini 3 Pro / GPT-5.2 / Claude Opus 4.5) — voice now retains **~79% of text capability** on the same domains and evaluator.

Transcription:

- **Word Error Rate across 24 languages, thousands of short phrases** (xAI's own evaluation, charts not published as numbers): **1.5–2.0× better than Deepgram Nova 3 and ElevenLabs Scribe v2**, **1.4× better than 1.0**, and the gap **widens to ~10× in noisy settings** (background noise, telephony compression). No per-language WER table is published as text, so the per-language values are not verifiable.

Tooling and operations (xAI-documented, no benchmark):

- Supported tool types: server-side `file_search` (collections), `web_search`, `x_search`, `mcp` (remote MCP servers, Streaming HTTP or SSE); client-side `function` with JSON schemas and **parallel tool calling**. Rate limits: 10 concurrent sessions base, up to 400 at tier 5. Region: **us-east-1 only**. Max session: **120 minutes**. Session resumption via `resumption.enabled` + `conversation_id` so history survives a WebSocket drop.
- Deployment evidence: A/B tested in production on **Starlink** support lines, with reported gains in sales conversion and support containment.
- **No verified public score found** for: SWE-bench Verified, Terminal-Bench (any version), Tau2-Bench, Tau3-Banking, GDPval-AA, BFCL, MCP-Bench, MCP Atlas, SWE Atlas, Toolathon, BrowserGym, WebArena, GAIA, and **every text-reasoning benchmark** — GPQA, HLE, AIME, MMLU, CritPt, ARC, the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, AA-LCR, AA-Omniscience. This is expected: the model has no text API, so none of these were ever run against it.

### Normalized scores (1–100)

> Derived from the raw numbers above using the methodology in `../../model-comparison.md`. Cost efficiency is scored independently and is **excluded** from Overall. Read the tool-use and multimodal scores as **voice-agent** scores and the context/coding scores as their honest floors — this model is out of class for three of the six axes, and the report says so rather than inventing numbers.

- **Tool use: 72/100.** This is the one axis with real, independent, methodology-aligned evidence. τ-Voice inherits τ-bench's tasks, tools and evaluator, and 56.5% at launch — **62.5% on Sierra's live board** — puts it **above the 54% non-reasoning text baseline** and around two-thirds of the way to the ~85% text reasoning ceiling. The methodology's own tool-use anchors are τ-bench-family scores, so a τ-voice pass rate in the high 50s/low 60s is directly comparable and lands comfortably in the 70–75 region rather than the 40–55 band that text τ2/τ3 models sit in. xAI-documented capability supports the score: server-side web/X/collections/MCP tools, client-side function tools with parallel calling, and an explicit design goal of firing tool calls before the first sentence completes. Deductions: the **"#1 on τ-voice" claim does not survive contact with the live official leaderboard** (3rd, behind its own deprecated predecessor), and voice tool use is inherently noisier than text — an interruption mid-argument can destroy a multi-step chain that would hold in a text agent.
- **Reasoning: 55/100.** Weakest evidence base in this report, and the score should be read as provisional. What exists: **Big Bench Audio 97.2%** (#2 in class), which measures speech-grounded comprehension rather than reasoning; the τ-bench read-across showing it clears a **non-reasoning text baseline** on customer-service tasks; and the parallel-reasoning architecture, which is a real capability claim that is hard to falsify. What does not exist: **not one** of GPQA, HLE, AIME, MMLU-Pro, CritPt, AA Intelligence Index, AA-LCR or AA-Omniscience has ever been run against this model. So 55 is a mid score for *"demonstrably competent at reasoning over speech, unmeasured at reasoning over hard knowledge, maths or science"* — not a claim that it scores 55 on any standard reasoning eval. Nobody knows how it handles a graduate-level question it must answer **verbally**, which is the only way this model can answer anything.
- **Context window: 42/100.** The model's clearest structural weakness, and the number I would most want a curator to see. **No source publishes a token context window** — xAI's docs are silent, models.dev has the limit unset, and four aggregators show the field blank. The real constraint is a **120-minute maximum session in us-east-1**, with context carried as a conversation transcript, which puts a full-length call in the **~25–30K token** range — the methodology's **32–64K** band, scored at its floor. Two further caveats: there is **no measured retrieval evidence of any kind** (no MRCR, no LOFT, no RULER), so even the transcript-context inference is unvalidated; and the meta.json's "128K total" is unsupported by anything I could find, so if a downstream consumer trusts it they will over-promise by roughly 4×. The `file_search` collections tool is the escape hatch for long documents — retrieve, don't accumulate.
- **Multimodal: 88/100.** Top of the modality ladder and elite measured execution. It is **natively audio-in/audio-out** — a genuinely audio-native model, not an ASR→LLM→TTS chain — which is harder than the image/video/PDF input band the methodology tops out at 75–90, and it is the only realtime voice model in the dataset. Supporting measurements: **#2 on the AA Speech-to-Speech Quality Index at 82.9%**, **Full Duplex 95.1%** (a +17.3 pp gain over its predecessor, and within 0.6 pp of OpenAI's best), **Big Bench Audio 97.2%**, **20+ languages with native accents and automatic detection**, and **transcription that beats dedicated STT engines** (Deepgram Nova 3, ElevenLabs Scribe v2) by 1.5–2.0×, widening to ~10× under noise and telephony compression — the real-world condition a phone agent actually runs in. Held to 88 rather than higher because the axis is **single-modality**: no image or video input to the model itself, `enable_image_understanding` is a web-search flag rather than a model capability, and it is not #1 on either the overall index or full duplex.
- **Coding: 25/100.** Effectively **unscorable, and scored at the floor rather than invented.** There is **no verified public score found** for any code benchmark — no SWE-bench, LiveCodeBench, Aider, SciCode, Vibe Code Bench or Terminal-Bench, which is unsurprising for a model with no text endpoint. The only adjacent evidence is that its general capability on τ-voice sits around a non-reasoning text baseline, well below where any model's coding score would land. In practice the coding you get from this model is JSON tool schemas and the client-side glue that executes `function_call_output` — the parts a voice agent needs and nothing more. If you want a coder, `grok-4.20` in this same dataset is the xAI route.
- **Cost efficiency: 70/100.** Scored in its own unit, because voice is not priced in tokens and the methodology's $/1M scale does not transfer. **$0.08/min = $4.80/hr**, so a 120-minute call costs **$9.60** — two to three orders of magnitude more than running the same customer-service task through a mid-tier text model on a few thousand tokens. Voice is priced for latency, not efficiency, and $0.004/text-event plus $0.01/min PSTN are add-ons on top. Within its class it is mid-pack, not cheap: **2.2× cheaper than GPT-Realtime-2.1 High ($10.75/hr)**, but **~9% more than Qwen Audio 3.0 Realtime Plus ($4.42/hr)** and **~16% more than GPT-Realtime-2 High ($4.14/hr)**, and **60% more than its own deprecated predecessor's $3.00/hr** — a price rise on the model xAI says is cheaper to serve. The 0.70s TTFA is what you are buying, and at that price point it is the right trade for a production voice agent.
- **Overall Score: 56.4/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dims — (72 + 55 + 42 + 88 + 25) / 5 = 56.4. **Best-fit recommendation:** Grok Voice Think Fast 2.0 is the **best-measured realtime voice agent available today and nothing else** — take it when your product is a phone or live-voice workflow where a 0.70s time-to-first-audio and 95.1% full-duplex barge-in decide whether the call feels like a conversation, and where τ-voice-class task completion above a non-reasoning text baseline is the bar. Do not read the 56.4 as "mediocre": the number is dragged down by an unscorable coding axis (25) and an unpublished context window (42), neither of which is a voice-agent requirement. Its real profile is **1-in-class on latency, top-2 on quality, top-3 on agentic task completion, and mid-pack on price** — and the two facts most likely to change a decision are that **its own predecessor currently outscores it on the live official τ³-Voice board**, and that **the `grok-voice-latest` alias moved to it on 2026-08-05**, so pin the version string. For anything that is not a live conversation, use a text model.
