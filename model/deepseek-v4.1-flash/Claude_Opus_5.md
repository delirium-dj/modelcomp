## Model card

- **Name:** DeepSeek V4.1 Flash. **Open weight under MIT** — the official model card and a technical report PDF are both published on Hugging Face as `deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash`. **MIT is the most permissive licence of any model in this research pass**, with no revenue thresholds and no Model-as-a-Service carve-outs. Variant/alias flag: distinct from **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813** and **DeepSeek V4 Flash 0731**.
- **Short description:** Released **2026-09-10**. The headline is value: Fireworks AI published a post titled **"DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash on Fireworks: Astra-level DeepSWE at 1/15th the cost"**, and the benchmark record supports it — **DeepSWE at 74.2% clears the methodology's 74%+ frontier reference**, matching GPT-6 Astra's 74.1%, at a fraction of the price. It also records **Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 90.6% — the highest figure recorded in this entire research pass**, a **Codeforces rating of 3,471**, the **highest AA-LCR (84.0%)** and the **highest AA AutomationBench (68.9%)** here. Set against all of that is the single worst reliability figure in this dataset: **an AA-Omniscience hallucination rate of 96.5%**, with a negative composite index of −5.3%.
- **Provider / access:** DeepSeek's own API, Hugging Face weights, **Fireworks AI** (which published a dedicated launch post for it), OpenRouter and other third-party hosts. **MIT weights mean the rate card is fully escapable.** Reasoning: explicit reasoning mode (tracker variant: `flash-reasoning`).
- **Release / knowledge:** **Released 2026-09-10.** **Knowledge cutoff: no verified public figure found.**
- **IDs:** `deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash` (Hugging Face). Related earlier model: DeepSeek V4 Flash 0731.
- **Context window:** **1,000,000 tokens**, and **AA-LCR at 84.0% is the highest long-context reasoning figure recorded in this entire research pass**. Max output: no verified figure found.
- **Modalities:** **Text and image input → text output.** Vision is evidenced by four published visual benchmarks. **No video or audio benchmark and no non-text output** found.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-03):** **$0.15 / 1M input and $0.60 / 1M output off-peak; $0.30 / $1.20 peak** — sourced from an independent open-weight comparison table verified earlier in this research session. **These are the lowest rates of any model in this dataset**, roughly one-twentieth of the $3/$15 anchor off-peak. **MIT weights** additionally make the rate escapable entirely.
- **Architecture:** **Sparse Mixture-of-Experts, 552 billion total parameters, with 8 billion active at prefill and 16 billion at decode** — per the same independent comparison. That **tiny active-parameter count is the key engineering fact**: it is what makes both the rate card and self-hosting unusually cheap for a model at this capability level, and it is far smaller than Kimi K3 (104B active), Qwen3.8-Max (95B active) or MiMo-V2.6-Pro (42B active). Pre-training data is not public, so "open weight" is not "open source".

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 86/100.** The strongest figures here are genuinely category-leading: **AA AutomationBench at 68.9% is the highest recorded in this research pass**, **Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 90.6% is also the highest and clears the methodology's ~88% anchor**, **GDPval-AA at 1,600 Elo** is among the better results, **CyberGym at 88.1%** shows real defensive-security capability, and **HLE with tools at 63.9%** demonstrates effective tool augmentation. Four deductions hold it to 86: **Vals AI measures Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 74.5% against the vendor's 90.6% — a 16.1-point gap** that puts the headline in doubt; **Terminal-Bench 3.0 (30%) and 4.0 (26.8–31.2%) fall sharply**; **Agents' Last Exam (31.8%) and GDP.pdf (12.8%) are weak**; and **Tau2-bench, Tau3-Banking, Claw-Eval, MCP-Atlas, Toolathlon and OSWorld are every one of them unreported** — six methodology references with no value, triggering the explicit penalty.
- **Reasoning: 74/100.** Three results are strong: **GPQA Diamond at 90.9% clears the 90%+ frontier reference**, **AA-LCR at 84.0% is the highest long-context reasoning figure in this entire research pass**, and **Apex at 65.6%** is solid mathematics, with an Intelligence Index of 39.5% that is respectable for a 16B-active-parameter model. The deduction is large and specific: **a 96.5% AA-Omniscience hallucination rate is the worst recorded in this entire dataset**, producing a **negative composite index of −5.3%** — meaning on a benchmark that explicitly rewards saying "I don't know", this model's confident errors outweigh its correct answers. At 46.4% accuracy it is not ignorant; it is radically mis-calibrated. Three further deductions: **HLE at 36.8%/39.2% misses the 40%+ reference on both measurements**; **MLCR-AA at 22.8% against AA-LCR's 84.0% is a 61.2-point collapse** on multi-document reasoning; and **CritPt at 14.3%** is mid-range, with **no independent GPQA measurement** to corroborate the vendor's figure.
- **Context window: 96/100.** A **1,000,000-token window** places it in the ≥1M tier (95–100), and it earns above the floor on the best evidence any model in this research pass offers for the dimension: **AA-LCR at 84.0% is the highest long-context reasoning figure recorded here.** It stops short of the ceiling because the tier's 100 condition requires **≥98% retrieval at 512K+** and **no MRCR, RULER or GraphWalks figure exists**; because **MLCR-AA at 22.8% shows multi-document reasoning across that window largely fails**; and because **max output tokens are undocumented**.
- **Multimodal: 70/100.** Input is **text and image** with text-only output, placing it in the methodology's "+image in = 60–70" band, and it earns the **top of that band** on four measured results: **BabyVision with Python at 89.6%**, **Chartography with tools at 78.9%** and **AA-MMMU-Pro at 77.0% (independently measured)** are all creditable. It cannot exceed the band because there is **no video, audio or PDF input path** and **no non-text output**, and two gaps temper the picture: **ZeroBench with Python at 49.0%** shows the hardest perceptual reasoning remains out of reach, and **there is no document-understanding or OCR benchmark** despite document work being a common use for a 1M-context model.
- **Coding: 88/100.** The best value-for-capability coding profile in this research pass. **DeepSWE at 74.2% clears the methodology's 74%+ frontier reference** and matches GPT-6 Astra (74.1%) and Claude Opus 5.5 (74.2%) — which is precisely the comparison Fireworks drew when it titled its launch post "Astra-level DeepSWE at 1/15th the cost". **Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 90.6% clears the 85%+ coding reference**, **Codeforces at 3,471 is an exceptional competitive-programming rating**, and **NL2Repo at 65.4% is the highest figure in this dataset**. Three deductions: **ProgramBench at 20.3% is a startling outlier** — against Claude Opus 5.5's 91.2% and Kimi K3's 77.8% — and DeepSeek published it itself, which is commendably honest but indicates a real weakness in program-level synthesis; **AA-SciCode at 51.9% is 3.1 points below the 55%+ reference**; and **SWE-bench Verified, SWE-bench Pro, LiveCodeBench and Vibe Code Bench are all absent**, so the most widely cited coding references cannot be checked, while the Terminal-Bench headline carries a 16.1-point independent discount.
- **Cost efficiency: 95/100.** **The best cost profile in this entire research pass.** **$0.15 input / $0.60 output off-peak (and $0.30 / $1.20 at peak) is roughly one-twentieth of the $3/$15 anchor** — the lowest rates of any model in this dataset, cheaper even than MiMo-V2.6-Flash ($0.14/$0.28 input/output, though DeepSeek is cheaper on the blended off-peak figure). Three structural factors compound it: **MIT weights with no revenue thresholds or MaaS carve-outs** — the most permissive licence here, so the rate card is fully escapable; a **552B-total / 8B-prefill / 16B-decode sparse MoE**, whose tiny active-parameter count makes self-hosting genuinely cheap relative to the 95B–104B-active open-weight alternatives; and an independent third party (Fireworks AI) publishing a dedicated post arguing it delivers **frontier-level DeepSWE at one-fifteenth the cost**, which is an unusually strong external endorsement of the value claim. Deductions: the **96.5% hallucination rate imposes a real verification cost** that the rate card does not show, and there is no free tier.
- **Overall Score: 82.8/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dimensions (86 + 74 + 96 + 70 + 88) / 5 = 82.8 — the best capability-per-dollar model in this research pass, with one property that must govern how it is deployed. Choose it for **agentic coding at scale**: DeepSWE 74.2% matches GPT-6 Astra at roughly a fifteenth of the cost, Terminal-Bench 2.1 is the highest figure here, NL2Repo leads this dataset, and Codeforces 3,471 is exceptional. Choose it for **business-process automation** (AA AutomationBench 68.9%, the highest recorded), for **long-context reasoning** (AA-LCR 84.0%, also the highest), for **defensive security** (CyberGym 88.1% with ExploitGym at only 15.3% — strong at finding and fixing, weak at weaponising), and for **any deployment where owning MIT-licensed weights at 16B active parameters matters**. The governing caution is unambiguous: **a 96.5% AA-Omniscience hallucination rate is the worst in this dataset and the composite index is negative.** Never use this model as a source of truth. In a retrieval-backed architecture where it reasons over supplied context it is excellent value; asked an unaided factual question it will answer confidently and be wrong more often than not. Also discount the Terminal-Bench headline — Vals AI measures 16.1 points lower — do not rely on it for multi-document long-context work (MLCR-AA 22.8%), and note that **ProgramBench at 20.3%** indicates program-level synthesis is far weaker than its other coding results suggest.
