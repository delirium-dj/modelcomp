## Model card

- **Name:** Llama 3.2 Vision Instruct
- **Short description:** Meta's **first open-weight vision-language family**, released at Connect 2024 — "a collection of pre-trained and instruction-tuned **image reasoning** generative models in **11B and 90B sizes (text + images in / text out)**", with the instruction-tuned variants "**optimized for visual recognition, image reasoning, captioning, and answering general questions about images**" ([NVIDIA NIM documentation](https://docs.nvidia.com/nim/vision-language-models/1.2.0/examples/llama3-2/overview.html)). ⚠️ **Scope note:** this folder's ID carries no size, and the family spans two very different models (11B and 90B). Where a figure applies to a specific size I say so; where Meta published only one, I do not extrapolate to the other. Distinct model; the text-only Llama 3.2 1B/3B edge models and later Llama generations are separate.
- **Provider / access:** **Open weights under the Llama Community License** — permissive for most use but **not** an OSI-style licence: it carries an acceptable-use policy and a monthly-active-user threshold above which a separate licence is required. Hosted on **NVIDIA NIM**, Cloudflare Workers AI, Inference.net, DeepInfra, Together, Vals AI's evaluation endpoint and **OpenCode Zen**.
- **Release / knowledge:** **September 2024.** Knowledge cutoff: December 2023 for the Llama 3.2 generation (not separately verified for the vision variants in this pass). **This is a two-year-old model and should be read as such** — it predates essentially every benchmark that now separates vision models.
- **IDs:** `meta-llama/Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct`, `meta-llama/Llama-3.2-90B-Vision-Instruct` (Hugging Face); `meta/llama-3_2-90b-vision-instruct` (NVIDIA NIM); `llama_3.2_vision_instruct` (Zen). **No free Zen ID**, but NVIDIA NIM developer credits make it effectively free to try.
- **Context window:** **131,072 tokens (128K)** with a **4,096-token maximum output**. The 4K output cap is severe by current standards and limits it to short-form answers regardless of how much it can read.
- **Modalities:** **Text + image in → text out.** No video, no audio, no generated media. Reasoning: no extended-thinking mode — this is a 2024-era instruct model. Tool calls: not a strength; Llama 3.2 Vision was not designed as an agentic model and no tool-use evaluation exists for it.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-08):** open weights (self-host free), with hosted rates spanning **$0 on NVIDIA NIM developer credits**, **$0.055 / $0.055 per MTok on Inference.net**, **$0.0485 / $0.676 on Cloudflare**, and **$0.345 / $0.345 on DeepInfra**. The flat-rate options are very cheap.
- **Architecture:** A vision adapter bolted onto the Llama 3.1 text backbone via cross-attention, at **11B and 90B** total parameters. Dense, not MoE.

### Raw benchmarks found

> ⚠️ **Benchmark coverage for this model is genuinely thin, and the reason is age.** BenchLM has no page under the slugs I tried (404). [BenchmarkList](https://benchmarklist.com/models/meta-llama-3.2-90b-vision-instruct/) carries an **"evidence-only profile… found in 1 benchmark"** with access unclassified, price and release date unpublished — a sign the model has fallen out of active tracking rather than that it was never measured. Meta's original September 2024 launch figures remain the main vendor evidence.

Multimodal:

- **MMMU: 60.3** for the 90B (Meta's launch figure, against GPT-4o's 69.1)
- **AMVICC: 91.9% model average — ranked #1** among the models tested, with a full category breakdown that is the most granular visual-reasoning evidence available for this model:

| AMVICC category | Score |
|---|---|
| Viewpoint and Perspective | **100.0%** |
| Presence of Specific Features | 97.1% |
| Structural and Physical Characteristics | 96.7% |
| Color and Appearance | 96.4% |
| Positional and Relational Context | 90.6% |
| Orientation and Direction | 90.0% |
| State and Condition | 87.0% |
| **Text** | **85.7%** |
| **Quantity and Count** | **83.3%** |

  AMVICC is a research benchmark that "adapt[s] MMVP benchmark questions into explicit and implicit prompts" to profile failure modes, testing "11 MLLMs and 3 IGMs in 9 categories of visual reasoning"; its premise is that "vision language models still fail to understand basic visual concepts such as object orientation, quantity, and spatial relationships". The result is marked **self-reported** and dated 2026-06-30. **The two weakest categories — counting (83.3%) and text/OCR (85.7%) — are precisely the two that matter most for document and chart work.**
- Meta's comparative claims for the smaller model, published at launch and not independently reproduced here: "The 11B Vision model punches above its weight class, **beating Gemini 1.5 Flash 8B on document visual QA and outperforming Claude 3 Haiku and Claude 3 Sonnet on AI2D, ChartQA, and MathVista**."

**Absent entirely:** no GPQA Diamond, MMLU-Pro, HLE, AIME, ARC-AGI, CritPt or Omniscience figure for the vision variants; **no coding benchmark of any kind**; **no tool-use, agentic or GUI benchmark at all**; and no MRCR, RULER or needle-retrieval measurement despite a 128K window.

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 25/100.** Scored low on design intent plus total absence of evidence. Meta's own positioning is "visual recognition, image reasoning, captioning, and answering general questions about images" — **there is no agentic claim and no agentic benchmark**: no BFCL, τ²-bench, Terminal-Bench, GDPval, OSWorld or ScreenSpot figure exists, and the Llama 3.2 Vision release was notable at the time for *not* pairing image input with robust tool calling. Not scored at the floor because the underlying Llama 3.1 text backbone does support function calling, but nothing measures it here.
- **Reasoning: 42/100.** **MMMU 60.3** is the only real figure, and it is a multimodal-reasoning score rather than a general one — respectable for September 2024, roughly 9 points behind the GPT-4o of that era, and well behind the 76%+ MMMU-Pro figures current models post. **There is no GPQA, MMLU-Pro, HLE, AIME or CritPt measurement for the vision variants at all.** The absence of any extended-thinking mode caps it further: this is a direct-answer instruct model from before reasoning modes became standard.
- **Context window: 44/100.** **131,072 tokens** was generous in 2024 and is merely adequate now, but the binding constraint is the other end: **a 4,096-token maximum output** means it cannot produce long analyses regardless of how much context it ingests — a real limitation for document work, which is otherwise its natural application. **No retrieval measurement at any depth** exists either. Scored on a usable input window crippled by a tight output cap.
- **Multimodal: 64/100.** The model's entire purpose, and the best-evidenced dimension. **AMVICC 91.9% ranked first among the MLLMs tested** is a strong result, and the category breadth is genuinely good — **100% on viewpoint and perspective, 97.1% on feature presence, 96.7% on structural characteristics, 96.4% on colour**. **MMMU 60.3** confirms competence on academic multimodal reasoning. Meta's launch claim that the 11B beat Claude 3 Haiku and Sonnet on **AI2D, ChartQA and MathVista** was credible for its moment. Three deductions keep it in the mid-60s: the two weakest AMVICC categories are **counting at 83.3% and text/OCR at 85.7%**, which undercuts exactly the document-and-chart use case; **image input only** — no video, no audio; and **the model is two years old**, so current VLMs with native video, OCR-specialized encoders and much higher MMMU-Pro figures have moved well past it.
- **Coding: 22/100.** **No coding benchmark of any kind exists for the vision variants** — no SWE-bench, LiveCodeBench, HumanEval or MBPP figure — and Meta never positioned these models for code. The text backbone can write code, which is why this is above the floor, but there is nothing to measure and no reason to choose this model for the task.
- **Cost efficiency: 82/100.** Strong, mainly because the market has moved on and left cheap capacity behind. **Open weights** mean self-hosting is free, the **11B variant is small enough for a single consumer GPU**, and hosted rates are very low — **$0 on NVIDIA NIM developer credits, $0.055 / $0.055 flat on Inference.net, $0.0485 input on Cloudflare**. For bulk image captioning, visual QA and basic document reading, that is excellent value. Two deductions: the **Llama Community License is not fully permissive** (acceptable-use policy plus a monthly-active-user threshold requiring a separate licence), and **the 4K output cap limits cost-per-useful-output** on anything requiring a long answer.
- **Overall Score: 39.4/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dims (25 + 42 + 44 + 64 + 22) / 5 = 39.4. The score is low because **three of five dimensions have essentially no evidence and the model is two years old** — it is a perception model being measured on a 2026 rubric that rewards agents, reasoning modes and long-form output, none of which it has. Best fit: **high-volume, low-cost image understanding** — captioning, visual question answering, basic chart and diagram reading, and image-grounded classification — where short answers suffice and the per-token price matters more than frontier accuracy. Avoid it for agentic work (no evidence, not designed for it), coding (no evidence), long-form generation (4K output cap), and anything depending on **precise counting or OCR**, which are its two measured weak spots. Its historical significance is real — it was Meta's first open vision-language release and it genuinely outperformed Claude 3 Haiku and Sonnet on several vision benchmarks at launch — but it should be chosen today for price, not capability.
