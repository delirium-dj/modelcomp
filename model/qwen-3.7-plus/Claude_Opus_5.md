## Model card

- **Name:** Qwen3.7-Plus. **Proprietary and hosted-only** — despite Qwen's reputation for open weights, this model cannot be self-hosted. No free API tier. Variant/alias flag: the Qwen3.7 series also contains **Qwen3.7 Max** (the stronger, dearer tier) and **Qwen3.7 Flash** (cheaper); Plus is the middle "value workhorse". A dated snapshot `qwen3.7-plus-20260602` is tracked by at least one aggregator.
- **Short description:** Alibaba Cloud Qwen Team's **multimodal agent model that unifies vision and language into a single agent foundation** — built on the Qwen3.7 text backbone and described by Qwen as a "multimodal interactive hybrid agent", with the series' text capabilities carried forward and a comprehensive upgrade to vision-language ability while retaining full-stack agent-level intelligence for coding, tool use and productivity workflows. **Release date is reported inconsistently**: one tracker records 2026-05-31, another cites an Alibaba Cloud launch article dated 2026-06-03, and a snapshot ID suggests 2026-06-02. All three are recorded; none is contradicted by a primary source I could reach.
- **Provider / access:** Alibaba Cloud DashScope (`dashscope-intl.aliyuncs.com`), the `chat.qwen.ai` playground, **OpenRouter** (`qwen/qwen3.7-plus`), plus **Together and Fireworks** as tracked third-party hosts. Reasoning: explicit reasoning mode. **Provider fragmentation is material and must be checked before deployment** — see Context window.
- **Release / knowledge:** Released late May / early June 2026 (see above). **Knowledge cutoff: no verified public figure found.**
- **IDs:** `qwen3.7-plus`; snapshot `qwen3.7-plus-20260602`; `qwen/qwen3.7-plus` (OpenRouter). **No free-tier ID.**
- **Context window:** **1,000,000 tokens with 65,536 max output** — but **the window is provider-dependent, which is unusual and consequential**: Together serves **1.0M / 65.5K** while **Fireworks serves only 262,144 / 65.5K**, a 3.8x difference in input capacity for the same model ID. Unlike most 1M-context models in this dataset, there **is** published retrieval evidence (MRCRv2 91.7%, see benchmarks). The 65,536-token output ceiling sits just above the methodology's <64K caveat threshold and is half the frontier norm.
- **Modalities:** **Documented as text and image input → text output** by both OpenRouter and an independent tracker. **However, this conflicts with the published benchmark set**, which includes three video results sourced to Alibaba Cloud's own launch blog — **Video-MME (with subtitle) 88.0%, VideoMMMU 85.4% and MLVU (M-Avg) 87.4%** — which are not obtainable without video input. The conflict is recorded rather than resolved: either the provider modality listings understate the model, or those rows belong to a sibling. No audio input and no non-text output are documented or benchmarked anywhere.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-03):** **$0.32 / 1M input, $0.08 / 1M cached input, $1.28 / 1M output** — a blended **~$0.37 / 1M at a 20:1 in:out mix**, or **~$0.61 / 1M for typical input-heavy agent use**. Provider variance again: **Together $0.32 / $1.28; Fireworks $0.40 / $0.08 cached / $1.60**. Independent editorial scoring rates it **9.1/10 for value**.
- **Architecture:** Proprietary; **parameter count, active parameters and topology are not disclosed**. Built on the Qwen3.7 text backbone with a unified vision-language agent head. An independent benchmark service reports **100% success rate across all its benchmarks** — i.e. exceptional response reliability with no malformed or unusable outputs — alongside **speed in the 14th percentile**, i.e. notably slow.

### Raw benchmarks found

> This is the most extensively benchmarked model in this research pass — **70 of 645 tracked benchmark slots populated**, with **per-row source attribution**. Provenance is marked on every row: **(Qwen)** = Alibaba Cloud's Qwen3.7-Plus launch benchmarks; **(AA)** = Artificial Analysis's independent measurements; **(Vals)** = Vals AI's independent Terminal-Bench 2.1 leaderboard; **(paper)** = the OSWorld 2.0 paper; **(OR)** = OpenRouter's benchmark page. The aggregator notes its coverage is partial, so its composite (**56.55/100, #59 of 783**) is explicitly conservative. **The vendor-versus-independent gap on this model is the widest in this dataset and is called out row by row.**

Agent / tool use:

- **Terminal-Bench 2.0: 70.3% (Qwen)** — the methodology's mid band. **Terminal-Bench 2.1: 52.8% (Vals, independent)** — a 17.5-point drop on the next benchmark version under an independent harness, and squarely mid-band.
- **Tau2-bench: 93% (AA, independent)** — far above the Tau-family ~50%+ frontier reference, and the single best independent agentic result for this model. No Tau3-Banking figure.
- **GDPval-AA: 886 Elo / 12.8% normalized (AA)** — **roughly 860 Elo below the methodology's ~1750+ frontier reference**, and the most damaging agentic datapoint here.
- **Claw-Eval: 62.7% (Qwen)** — notable because the methodology specifically penalises a missing Claw-Eval, and this is one of very few models in the dataset to publish one. **QwenClawBench: 61.8% (Qwen)** (vendor-internal).

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 76/100.** The tool-*calling* evidence is genuinely good: **Tau2-bench at 93% independently measured by Artificial Analysis clears the Tau-family frontier reference by a wide margin**, **MCP Atlas 73.2% and BFCL v4 72.9%** are solid, **AndroidWorld 81.0%** shows real mobile-UI agency, and crucially **a Claw-Eval figure of 62.7% actually exists** — the methodology penalises its absence, and almost no model in this dataset publishes one. What caps the score is long-horizon autonomous agency, where three independent measurements agree the model is mid-tier at best: **GDPval-AA at 886 Elo is roughly 860 below the ~1750+ reference**, the **AA Agentic Index is 19.7%** and **APEX-Agents-AA 22.4%**. Add the harness-sensitivity evidence: **Terminal-Bench drops from the vendor's 70.3% on v2.0 to 52.8% on v2.1 under Vals AI's independent harness**, and the vendor's **OSWorld-Verified 73.3% sits against an independent OSWorld 2.0 result of 2.8%**. Good at calling tools, weak at finishing long jobs.
- **Reasoning: 80/100.** Two frontier references are met and — unusually — **independently corroborated**: **GPQA Diamond 90.3% vendor / 90.0% AA** clears the 90%+ marker with a 0.3-point agreement that is the tightest vendor-independent match in this dataset, and IFBench (79.1% vendor / 78.0% AA) corroborates similarly. MMLU-Redux 94.5%, HMMT Feb 2026 92.9%, IMOAnswerBench 86.0% and MRCRv2 91.7% round out a strong knowledge-and-mathematics profile. But the independent composite is the decisive evidence and it is poor: **the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index of 25.2% sits in the methodology's mid band (20–35 → 55–65), 35 points below the 60+ frontier marker**. Supporting that read: **HLE 34.7%/35.6% misses the 40%+ reference on both measurements**, **CritPt 9.1%** is roughly a third of the best figure here, and the **AA-Omniscience Index of 1.1% with 22.5% accuracy** says the model knows comparatively little — though commendably its 27.7% hallucination rate shows it is cautious rather than confabulating. The honest summary: excellent on well-specified exam-style questions, mid-tier on open-ended intelligence.
- **Context window: 95/100.** A **1,000,000-token window** places it in the ≥1M tier (95–100), and it is one of only two models in this pass with **published long-context retrieval evidence — MRCRv2 at 91.7%, plus AA-LCR at 73.0%** — which is far better than the usual complete absence. Three things keep it at the tier floor rather than higher. **MRCRv2 91.7% falls short of the ≥98% retrieval condition** for a 100. The **65,536-token output ceiling** is half the frontier norm and barely clears the methodology's <64K caveat threshold. And most practically, **the window is provider-dependent — Together serves the full 1.0M while Fireworks caps input at 262,144 tokens**, a 3.8x difference under the same model name, so the "1M context" is not a property you can rely on without pinning your provider.
- **Multimodal: 88/100.** The best-*measured* multimodal model in this dataset by a wide margin — **sixteen distinct vision, document and video benchmarks**, where most peers publish none. The breadth is real and strong: **OmniDocBench 1.5 at 91.4% is the best document-understanding figure found anywhere here**, MathVision 90.3%, CharXiv 85.9%, RealWorldQA 86.9%, OCRBench V2 70.7%, ScreenSpot Pro 79.0%, and **MMMU-Pro independently corroborated at 80.5% against the vendor's 79%**. Three published video results (Video-MME 88.0%, VideoMMMU 85.4%, MLVU 87.4%) place it in the methodology's "+video/PDF in = 75–90" band near the top. It is held to 88 rather than higher for two honest reasons: **the documented input modalities (text and image only, per both OpenRouter and an independent tracker) directly contradict those video scores**, so the video capability is benchmarked but not documented; and there is **no audio input and no non-text output**, which caps this band at 90 regardless.
- **Coding: 82/100.** The vendor numbers are among the strongest in this dataset — **LiveCodeBench 89.6% is the highest found anywhere in this research pass**, and a **complete SWE-bench triple (Verified 77.7%, Multilingual 75.8%, Pro 57.6%)** is more than most frontier models publish. But every independent check lands lower and the methodology's references are mostly missed: **the AA Coding Index at 55.9% is 14 points below the 70%+ frontier reference**, **SciCode at 51.3% vendor / 46.1% independent misses the 55%+ reference on both**, **Terminal-Bench 2.0 at 70.3% is far below the 85%+ coding reference** and falls to 52.8% on v2.1 under an independent harness, NL2Repo is 41.1%, and **DeepSWE and Vibe Code Bench are absent**. Strong at writing code in isolation, mid-tier at engineering inside a repository.
- **Cost efficiency: 88/100.** Among the best value in this dataset. **$0.32 input / $1.28 output is roughly an order of magnitude below the $3/$15 anchor**, with **cached input at $0.08** and a blended rate of **~$0.37 / 1M at 20:1** (or ~$0.61 for input-heavy agent patterns). Independent editorial scoring rates value at **9.1/10**, and the combination of a 1M window with these rates is the specific thing it is bought for — high-call-volume agents that need long context cheaply. Three deductions keep it below the 90s: **throughput sits in the 14th percentile — notably slow**, so wall-clock cost per completed task is worse than the per-token rate implies; **pricing and context both vary by provider** (Fireworks is 25% dearer on both meters *and* caps context at 262K); and it is **proprietary and hosted-only**, so unlike the open-weight models scored nearby there is no self-hosting escape from the rate card. Offsetting all of that, one independent service measured a **100% success rate across all its benchmarks** — no wasted spend on malformed responses.
- **Overall Score: 84.2/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dimensions (76 + 80 + 95 + 88 + 82) / 5 = 84.2 — a strong **multimodal document-and-vision workhorse** and a weak long-horizon agent. Choose it for **document, chart and OCR pipelines** (OmniDocBench 1.5 91.4%, OCRBench V2 70.7%, CharXiv 85.9% — the best-measured such profile here), **vision-grounded mathematics and science** (MathVision 90.3%, MedXpertQA MM 71.0%, GPQA Diamond 90.3% independently corroborated), **mobile and screen automation** (AndroidWorld 81.0%, ScreenSpot Pro 79.0%), **algorithmic coding** (LiveCodeBench 89.6%), and **high-volume long-context agent loops where the bill matters** — $0.32/$1.28 with a 1M window and 100% response reliability is a defensible default. Do not choose it for autonomous long-horizon work: **GDPval-AA 886 Elo, AA Agentic Index 19.7% and a Terminal-Bench drop from 70.3% to 52.8% under an independent harness** all say the same thing. Do not choose it where general reasoning depth is the product — **the independent Intelligence Index of 25.2% is mid-band** — nor for unaided factual recall (AA-Omniscience accuracy 22.5%), nor for latency-sensitive work (14th-percentile throughput). Two deployment cautions: **pin your provider**, because context (1.0M vs 262K) and price both change beneath the same model ID; and **verify video input before relying on it**, because the published video benchmarks and the documented input modalities disagree.
