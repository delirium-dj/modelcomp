## Model card

- **Name:** Ling 3.0 Flash Sante
- **Short description:** Ant Group's **health-and-medicine domain variant of Ling-3.0-flash** — "an MoE model enhanced for health and medicine, built on Ling-3.0-flash", with the name "Inspired by the French word 'santé,' meaning 'health'" (@AntLingAGI on X, 2026-09-04 19:14 UTC, as transcribed by [AI Wiki](https://aiwiki.ai/wiki/ling_3_0_flash_sante)). Ant positions it for "real-world healthcare tasks spanning **medical reasoning, professional healthcare tasks, deep research, and evidence-based retrieval**". ⚠️ **The regulatory framing must travel with this model:** its own host states it "is best treated as a developer API for research, retrieval, summarization, and workflow assistance, **not as a medical device or a replacement for qualified clinical judgment**", and "**Do not use a generated response as a diagnosis, treatment instruction, or independent clinical decision**" (Novita AI). Distinct model; `ling-3.0-flash` (the base), `ling-3.1-flash`, `ling-2.6-flash`, `ling-2.6.1t`, `ling-3.0-tiny` and `ring-2.6.1t` all have their own folders, and Ling-3.0-flash-VL and -Fin are separate variants.
- **Provider / access:** **Hosted API only — no open weights.** Three routes (OpenRouter, Vercel AI Gateway, Novita AI) that **all resolve to Novita as the sole underlying provider**, plus **OpenCode Zen**. As of the fact-checked 2026-09-05 snapshot there was **no Hugging Face or ModelScope repository**, and **Ant's own developer documentation did not list the model** — its changelog recorded the VL variant but not Sante.
- **Release / knowledge:** **2026-09-04** (X announcement at 19:14 UTC; Novita added it to its catalogue on 2026-09-03, a day before the public post). Knowledge cutoff: not published.
- **IDs:** `inclusionai/ling-3.0-flash-sante` and `:free` (OpenRouter), canonical slug `inclusionai/ling-3.0-flash-sante-20260904`, plus `-free` on Vercel. **Free promotional access has lapsed** — Vercel's offer ended **2026-10-04**; OpenRouter retains a rate-limited free tier.
- **Context window:** **262,144 tokens (256K)** with **32,768 max output** on hosted endpoints. The 32K output cap is worth flagging against the "deep research" and "long-horizon medical tasks" positioning.
- **Modalities:** **Text in → text out only.** AI Wiki is explicit: "**Medical images are out of scope**" — Ant's separate VL variant is the one claiming "medical report interpretation". Reasoning: **yes, on by default**, switchable per request. Tool calls: **yes (tools and tool_choice)**, with logprobs and seed. ⚠️ **But `response_format` is not supported — "JSON output is not enforced"**, which matters for any structured clinical pipeline.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-08):** **$0.042 / MTok input and $0.123 / MTok output** (EnterpriseDNA, 2026-10-09) — *cheaper* than the base model, and very cheap in absolute terms. ⚠️ **This corrects this repo's metadata**, which recorded the list price as unpublished after the 2026-10-04 promo ended; a list price now exists.
- **Architecture:** **124B total / ~5.1B active**, unchanged from the base — "a domain-adapted version of Ling-3.0-flash rather than a new architecture". The base design (documented on the base model's page, not Sante's) is **42 transformer layers alternating Kimi Delta Attention with gated multi-head latent attention at a 5:1 ratio, and 512 routed experts plus one shared with 8 active per token — a 1/64 activation ratio**. ⚠️ **How the adaptation was done is not published at all**: "Nothing published so far says how the adaptation was done." Ant's finance variant was made "through continued training on high-quality financial data… with leading financial institutions and domain experts"; **whether Sante followed the same recipe with medical data has not been stated**, and there is **no model card, no technical report, no training-data description and no safety evaluation**.

**AI Wiki's analysis of Ant's own claim is the most important finding here, and I adopt it.** Ant's post claimed "leading performance among open-source models". In fact: "Among the seven open-weight models in the legend, Sante has the highest bar on **three of the twelve panels**… On the other nine it is behind at least one open-weight model, most often Kimi K3, which the legend lists at **2.8T total parameters**." And more pointedly: "**The medical panels on which Sante leads the open-weight group are the ones with the lowest scores overall** (MedXpertQA-Text and DiagnosisArena-MCQ), while on the licensing-exam sets (MedQA-USMLE, MedMCQA) it sits below the larger open models by one to about five points."

The genuine mitigating fact: **Sante has the smallest active-parameter count of every model in the legend whose size is given** — 5.1B against Kimi K3's 104B active.

**Evaluation settings Ant disclosed** (which change how the numbers read): temperature 0.6 / top_p 0.95 for Sante across all benchmarks; "the reasoning effort was set to the highest available tier for each model"; BrowseComp at temperature 1.0, top_p 0.95, 256K window, **maximum 1,000 turns**, with a "discard-all context-management strategy, triggered when context usage reaches 40% of the maximum window size", and a multi-agent harness "capable of **dispatching up to 64 subagents**"; HLE-with-tools and DeepSearchQA at 256K with "no context-management strategy".

**Four structural caveats, all from the fact-checked source:**
1. **Two of twelve panels are Ant's own unpublished internal benchmarks** (AFUMED-Drug, AFUSAFE-MedSCE) — "nobody outside Ant can check them", and one of the three panels Sante leads is one of these.
2. **Three panels omit comparators without explanation.**
3. **The general-search panels ran in Ant's own harness**, and "the chart does not say which API versions or dates were used" for the competitors.
4. **The BrowseComp setup differs from the base model's**, so "the two models' BrowseComp figures are not like-for-like".

**Absent entirely:** no GPQA Diamond, MMLU-Pro, AIME, Omniscience or hallucination figure; **no coding benchmark of any kind**; no retrieval, MRCR, RULER or AA-LCR measurement; no red-teaming, refusal or clinical-harm evaluation.

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 66/100.** The agentic-search results are the model's second-best story: **BrowseComp multi-agent 86.9 with a harness dispatching up to 64 subagents**, **DeepSearchQA 86.8**, and 1,000-turn runs at 256K — that is real long-horizon tool orchestration, and it matters for the "evidence-based retrieval" use case the model is sold for. Four deductions hold it in the mid-60s: **single-agent BrowseComp at 73.9 trails Kimi K3's 91.2 by 17 points** and DeepSearchQA trails by 8.2; both panels ran **in Ant's own harness** against competitors whose API versions are undisclosed; nothing is independently reproduced; and **`response_format` is unsupported with "JSON output is not enforced"**, which is a concrete limitation for exactly the structured clinical workflows this model targets.
- **Reasoning: 68/100.** The strongest dimension on the available evidence. **HLE with tools at 53.2** is high in absolute terms and only 4.7 points behind Claude Opus 4.8 — from a 5.1B-active model. **MedXpertQA-Text 53.9 leads every open-weight comparator**, and **DiagnosisArena-MCQ 83.8 leads all ten models including GPT-5.6 Sol** — expert-level medical reasoning and diagnostic reasoning are genuinely where it wins. Capped by three things: **HealthBench Professional at 45.7 is its weakest medical panel** and trails the field leader by 15.1 points; it loses the licensing-exam sets (MedQA-USMLE, MedMCQA) to larger open models; and **there is no GPQA, MMLU-Pro, AIME or hallucination measurement at all**, so general reasoning and factual calibration are unmeasured. Base-model figures are deliberately not imported.
- **Context window: 64/100.** **262,144 tokens native** on a documented hybrid-attention stack (Kimi Delta Attention plus gated MLA at 5:1), and the BrowseComp runs demonstrate the window being *used* at 256K across up to 1,000 turns. Two real deductions: the **32,768-token output cap is restrictive** for a model marketed on "deep research" and "long-horizon medical tasks" — you can read a great deal and write comparatively little; and **there is no retrieval measurement whatsoever** (no MRCR, RULER, needle or AA-LCR), so usable recall at depth is entirely unverified for this variant.
- **Multimodal: 15/100.** Text in, text out, and explicitly so — **"medical images are out of scope"**. For a medical model this is a genuine functional gap rather than a neutral scope choice: radiology, pathology and chart interpretation are all unreachable. Ant routes those to the separate VL variant, which has its own identity. Template floor.
- **Coding: 38/100.** Scored almost entirely on absence. OpenRouter and Novita both claim the model "retain[s] strong general capabilities in reasoning, coding, and agentic tasks" — but **not one coding benchmark has been published for this variant**, and I will not import the base model's figures, which belong to a different folder and a different checkpoint. A continued-training domain adaptation can plausibly preserve coding ability, and the HLE-with-tools and BrowseComp results imply competent general capability, so this is not scored at the floor. But an unverified host-page claim about a derivative is weak evidence, and it is scored as such.
- **Cost efficiency: 70/100.** The price is excellent — **$0.042 in / $0.123 out per MTok is below the base model's own rate** and among the cheapest in this dataset, with **5.1B active of 124B** making that sustainable, and OpenRouter still offering a rate-limited free tier. Against that sit four procurement problems that a medical deployment cannot ignore: **no open weights and no stated licence** (the base model and the Fin variant are both MIT; Sante's licence is simply not stated); **a single underlying provider** — all three routes resolve to Novita, so there is no redundancy; **no model card, no technical report and no published training method**; and most seriously for this domain, **no safety evaluation, red-teaming, refusal or clinical-harm assessment of any kind**, with **no medical-use disclaimer on the OpenRouter or Vercel pages** — only Novita's blog carries one. Cheap tokens do not offset an unauditable medical model.
- **Overall Score: 50.2/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dims (66 + 68 + 64 + 15 + 38) / 5 = 50.2. Best fit: **cheap, high-volume medical text work with a clinician in the loop** — literature retrieval and synthesis, evidence-based search over 256K of source material, diagnostic-reasoning support, medication-contraindication checking, and documentation drafting — where the output is reviewed and the 32K generation cap is not binding. The honest summary of this model is that **Ant's marketing claim overreaches its own chart**: "leading performance among open-source models" is true on three of twelve panels, two of which are Ant's internal benchmarks, and false on the other nine. What is genuinely impressive is doing that at **5.1B active parameters against a 104B-active rival**. What should stop a clinical deployment is not the benchmark picture but the paperwork: **no weights, no licence, no model card, no training description, no safety evaluation, no medical images, and a single provider** — and the vendor's own host saying it is "not as a medical device or a replacement for qualified clinical judgment."
