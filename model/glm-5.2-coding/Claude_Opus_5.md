## Model card

- **Name:** GLM 5.2 Coding. **Open weights, MIT licence.** **Variant/alias finding that governs this report: this is not a distinct model — it is Zhipu's June 2026 open-weights MoE flagship GLM-5.2 packaged as a coding-plane SKU**, described in the repository's curated metadata as a "long-horizon agentic coding and Claude Code-compatible drop-in at ~1/6 closed-frontier cost". The weights are GLM-5.2's. **The SKU's defining constraint is context: 128K on this coding tier against 1M on base GLM-5.2 — an eightfold reduction.**
- **Short description:** A composite tracker places the underlying GLM-5.2 at **#45 of 784 with 61.47/100** across 43 benchmarks. Its genuine distinctions are narrow and sharp: **τ²-bench at 99.1% is the highest τ² figure found anywhere in this research pass**, **MCP Atlas at 76.8% is also the highest in this pass**, and the mathematics is exceptional — **AIME 2026 at 99.2%, HMMT Nov 2025 at 94.4%, HMMT Feb 2026 at 92.5%**. It also has an unusually clean reliability profile: **a positive AA-Omniscience Index of +4.4% on a 26.3% hallucination rate**, the second-lowest confabulation rate in this pass. Against that, three findings undercut the coding-SKU positioning specifically: **Terminal-Bench 3.0 at 4.6% is near-floor**, **the AA Coding Index at 68.8% misses the methodology's 70%+ reference**, and **Z.AI's own successor GLM-5.3 — available in this repository at $0 on a free tier — beats it on every coding measure that matters.**
- **Provider / access:** OpenCode coding-plane SKU; **Claude Code-compatible drop-in** per the curated metadata. Underlying weights public at `zai-org/GLM-5.2` on Hugging Face under the **MIT licence**.
- **Release / knowledge:** **June 2026** (repository metadata). **No verified knowledge cutoff found.**
- **IDs:** `opencode/glm-5.2-coding`; underlying weights `zai-org/GLM-5.2`. **No free-tier ID for this SKU.**
- **Context window:** **128,000 tokens on this coding tier**, against **1,000,000 on base GLM-5.2** — an **eightfold reduction**, and the single most consequential difference between this SKU and the model behind it. **AA-LCR at 78.3%** shows the underlying model reasons well at length, which makes the truncation a deliberate product decision rather than a capability limit. Max output: no verified figure found.
- **Modalities:** **Text in → text out. Text-only**, per the curated metadata ("Text in/out") and corroborated by the benchmark record: **no MMMU, MMMU-Pro, CharXiv, document, OCR, video or audio figure exists** for GLM-5.2, and Z.AI ships vision as separate models (GLM-5V-Turbo, and the natively multimodal GLM-5.3-Flash). **For any image, video or document workload, GLM-5.3-Flash is the correct choice in this family.**
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-03):** **$1.40 / 1M input, $4.40 / 1M output, with cached input at $0.26** (repository metadata). That is roughly **2.1× cheaper on input and 3.4× cheaper on output than the methodology's $3/$15 anchor**, with the cached rate about 5.4× below the uncached one. The curated note of "~1/6 closed-frontier cost" is directionally consistent. **No primary Z.AI rate card was reached; re-verify before budgeting.**
- **Architecture:** **Open weights, MIT licence. 744B total parameters with 40B active** — a sparse MoE (repository metadata). Note for continuity: **GLM-5.3 uses the same base model as GLM-5.2, with all gains from post-training**, per Z.AI's own GLM-5.3 card — so the successor is not a new pre-train but a better-tuned version of these same weights.

### Raw benchmarks found

> **43 of 649 tracked benchmark slots are populated** for the underlying GLM-5.2 (tracker snapshot 2026-10-05). **No benchmark figures exist for the `glm-5.2-coding` SKU specifically** — every row below is GLM-5.2, which is the correct basis given identical weights, but it is a stated inference. **Tier caveat applying to the whole table: these figures were produced on the base model with its 1M window; this SKU exposes 128K.** **(Z)** = Z.AI's own GLM-5.2 model card; **(AA)** = Artificial Analysis; **(Vals)** = Vals AI; **(Cursor)** = Cursor's own evals; plus the independent Terminal-Bench 3.0, ResearchClawBench, OpenHarmony Bench and PostTrainBench v1.1 leaderboards and OpenRouter. Composite: **61.47/100, #45 of 784**, flagged conservative on partial coverage.

- **MCP Atlas: 76.8% (Z)** — **the highest MCP-Atlas figure in this research pass**, ahead of DeepSeek V4 Pro (73.6%) and GPT-5.4 (70.6%). MCP-Atlas measurements are rare, so this is a meaningful credential for Model-Context-Protocol tool orchestration.
- **Terminal-Bench 2.1: 81.0% (Z)** against **67.8% (Vals)** — a 13.2-point vendor-to-independent gap; the vendor figure is 7 points below the ~88% anchor and the independent figure 20.2 points below.
- **Terminal-Bench 3.0: 4.6%** (independent leaderboard) — **near-floor, and the worst Terminal-Bench 3.0 figure in this research pass** against GLM-5.3's 28.3%, GPT-5.6 Sol's 34.6% and Claude Fable 5's 33.7%. Z.AI's own GLM-5.3 card confirms this 4.6% and presents the successor's 28.3% as a six-fold improvement.

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 78/100.** Two pass-leading credentials, both meaningful and one independently measured. **τ²-bench at 99.1% is the highest figure found anywhere in this research pass**, which is real evidence of excellent schema-driven tool calling, and **MCP Atlas at 76.8% is also the highest here** — MCP measurements are scarce, so this is a genuine differentiator for Model-Context-Protocol orchestration. **Toolathlon at 48.2%, the AA Agentic Index at 39.4% and AA ITBench at 42.7%** fill out a mid-field picture. Five deductions. **Terminal-Bench 3.0 at 4.6% is the worst in this research pass** — Z.AI's own successor card presents GLM-5.3's 28.3% as a six-fold improvement over exactly this figure, so the vendor concedes the point. **Terminal-Bench 2.1 carries a 13.2-point vendor-to-independent gap** (81.0% against 67.8%) and neither figure reaches the ~88% anchor. **GDPval-AA at 1,418 Elo is 332 below the reference.** **APEX-Agents (33.7%) and ResearchClawBench (20.7%) both fall away** on long-horizon work. And **Claw-Eval, OSWorld, CyberGym, BrowseComp and Toolathlon-Verified are unreported**, with the methodology explicitly penalising the Claw-Eval absence.
- **Reasoning: 82/100.** The mathematics is the standout and it is exceptional: **AIME 2026 at 99.2% is effectively saturated**, with **HMMT Nov 2025 at 94.4%, HMMT Feb 2026 at 92.5% and MMAnswerBench at 91.0%** — four independent competition-mathematics results above 90%. **Both tool-free HLE figures clear the 40%+ reference and agree within 0.6 points (40.5% vendor, 41.1% independent)**, which is unusually clean corroboration. **AA-LCR at 78.3%, AA-IFBench at 73.3% and MMLU-Pro at 86.7%** are all solid, and **the AA-Omniscience Index is positive at +4.4% on a 26.3% hallucination rate — the second-lowest confabulation rate in this research pass.** Four deductions. **GPQA only clears the 90%+ reference on the vendor's own number**: Artificial Analysis measures 89.5%, missing it by 0.5 points, and Vals 85.6%, a 5.6-point spread. **Raw Omniscience accuracy is 24.3% — the narrowest knowledge base of any model assessed in this pass** — so the good calibration is substantially a consequence of knowing little, which is a different and less valuable virtue than Grok 4.3's or GLM-5.3's. **The Intelligence Index of 33.7% is 26.3 points below the 60+ marker.** And **neither ARC-AGI nor FrontierMath has any figure**, so abstract reasoning and research mathematics are unmeasured despite the competition-maths strength.
- **Context window: 62/100. This is the SKU's defining weakness and the score reflects the tier, not the model.** **128,000 tokens** places it in the methodology's 100K–200K band (50–64), and it is an **eightfold reduction from base GLM-5.2's 1,000,000**. It is set near the top of that band because **AA-LCR at 78.3% shows the underlying model reasons well at length** — the truncation is a packaging decision, not a capability limit — and because the weights are MIT-licensed, so self-hosting restores the full window. Three reasons it is not higher: **128K is simply small for long-horizon agentic coding**, which is the exact use case this SKU is sold for, and repository work routinely exceeds it; **no MRCR, RULER, CorpusQA or GraphWalks figure exists**; and **max output tokens are undocumented**. Note the internal tension in the product positioning — a SKU marketed for "long-horizon agentic coding" with a 128K ceiling is working against itself.
- **Multimodal: 17/100.** **Text-only**, capped in the methodology's "text-only = 10–20" band, confirmed by the curated metadata ("Text in/out") and by the complete absence of any multimodal benchmark for GLM-5.2 — **no MMMU, MMMU-Pro, CharXiv, document, OCR, video or audio figure exists** — with Z.AI shipping vision as separate models. Placed near the top of the band solely because **Design Arena Website at 1,294 Elo** shows competent visual-artefact generation *through code*; that figure is **explicitly excluded** from the input-modality judgement. **For image, video or document work, GLM-5.3-Flash is the correct choice in this family** — it has verified video input (MMVU 80.5%) and excellent chart reasoning (CharXiv 89.4%).
- **Coding: 76/100. For a SKU sold specifically as a coding model, this is the most important finding in the report: none of the methodology's coding reference thresholds is cleared.** The best figures are respectable rather than strong. **SWE-bench at 82.8% from Vals AI is solid** but **12.6 points below GLM-5.3's 95.4% on the identical harness**; **ProgramBench at 63.7%** and **SWE-bench Pro at 62.1%** are mid-field; **Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 81.0% misses the 85%+ coding reference by 4 points** and drops to 67.8% independently. Then the misses accumulate: **the AA Coding Index at 68.8% misses the 70%+ reference by 1.2 points**; **AA-SciCode at 51.2% misses the 55%+ reference by 3.8**; **LiveCodeBench (69.5%), OpenHarmony Bench (58.4%), CursorBench 3.2 (55.0%) and NL2Repo (48.9%)** are all mid-band; **PostTrainBench v1.1 is 31.7%**; and **Terminal-Bench 3.0 at 4.6% is near-floor**. **SWE-bench Verified, DeepSWE and Codeforces are unreported.** One anomaly is recorded rather than resolved: ProgramBench at 63.7% here against 19.0% for the successor GLM-5.3, which almost certainly reflects different subsets rather than regression.
- **Cost efficiency: 58/100.** The rate card itself is good — **$1.40 / $4.40 is roughly 2.1× cheaper on input and 3.4× cheaper on output than the $3/$15 anchor**, **cached input at $0.26 is about 5.4× below the uncached rate**, the weights are **MIT-licensed** so the price is escapable entirely, and **Claude Code compatibility means near-zero migration cost**. What drives the score below the midpoint is that **it is dominated inside its own vendor family, and decisively so.** **GLM-5.3 is the same base model with better post-training** — Z.AI says so explicitly — and scores **68.61 against 61.47**, with **the AA Coding Index at 74.8% against 68.8%** (clearing the reference where this misses it), **SWE-bench at 95.4% against 82.8%**, **Terminal-Bench 3.0 at 28.3% against 4.6%**, and **GDPval-AA at 1,769 against 1,418**. More pointedly still, **this repository carries GLM-5.3 on a $0 free tier** with a 204K window — larger than this SKU's 128K. Paying $1.40/$4.40 for strictly worse coding performance and a smaller window than a free sibling is not defensible on cost grounds. Two further deductions: **the 128K ceiling undercuts the long-horizon use case the SKU is sold for**, and **no primary Z.AI rate card was reached**.
- **Overall Score: 63.0/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dimensions (78 + 82 + 62 + 17 + 76) / 5 = 63.0. Three things about this model are genuinely excellent and worth knowing: **τ²-bench at 99.1% and MCP Atlas at 76.8% are both the highest figures found anywhere in this research pass**, making it a strong choice for schema-driven and Model-Context-Protocol tool orchestration; **the competition mathematics is exceptional** — AIME 2026 at 99.2%, HMMT at 94.4% and 92.5%, MMAnswerBench at 91.0%; and **the reliability profile is clean**, with a positive +4.4% Omniscience Index on a 26.3% hallucination rate, second-lowest in this pass. Add **both tool-free HLE figures clearing 40% in close agreement**, **AA-LCR 78.3%**, **AA-IFBench 73.3%** and **MMLU-Pro 86.7%**. But the coding-SKU premise does not survive examination, for four reasons. **No coding reference threshold is cleared** — the AA Coding Index misses 70% by 1.2 points, Terminal-Bench 2.1 misses 85% by 4, AA-SciCode misses 55% by 3.8 — and **Terminal-Bench 3.0 at 4.6% is the worst in this pass**, a figure Z.AI itself uses as the baseline its successor improves six-fold upon. **The 128K window contradicts the "long-horizon agentic coding" positioning.** **Knowledge breadth is the narrowest measured here at 24.3% raw accuracy**, so the good calibration largely reflects ignorance rather than judgement. And **it is dominated by a free sibling**: GLM-5.3 is the same base weights better tuned, beats it on every coding measure, and is available in this repository at $0 with a larger window. Choose GLM-5.2 Coding only if you specifically need its τ²-bench or MCP-Atlas strengths, or its mathematics, at a priced tier.
