## Model card

- **Name:** Gemini 4 Argon — Google's official announced name. **No API identifier has been published**, so any `gemini-4-argon`-style ID is an assumption, not a verified fact. No free tier; broader access is slated to begin with paid API customers and Google AI Ultra subscribers.
- **Short description:** Google DeepMind's new frontier model, announced **2026-09-30**, aimed at sustained professional work — software engineering, enterprise research, legal and financial workflows, and defensive cybersecurity. Its headline technical disclosure is a **one-million-token output limit** (up from Google's stated previous 64K), i.e. a generation ceiling rather than an input-context claim. Variant/alias flag: Google's Gemini page also links **Gemini Omni, Nano Banana, Gemini Audio and Gemini Robotics** — these are separate product and model surfaces and their features must not be attributed to Argon.
- **Provider / access:** **Restricted at launch.** Day-one access goes to trusted cyber defenders through Google's **Fairwind Program** (the programme lists 650+ partners globally, but Argon went to a selected subset) plus Google's own internal teams — and those defenders receive it **without cyber guardrails** to support authorised defensive work. Google says broader access will follow for developers, enterprises and consumers, beginning with paid API customers and Google AI Ultra subscribers, but **gave no firm public rollout date**. Google is also participating in the U.S. government's voluntary pre-release model access process. Practical consequence: a published price establishes an intended commercial rate, not that any developer can call this model today.
- **Release / knowledge:** Announced 2026-09-30. **Knowledge cutoff: not disclosed.** Google's own evaluation methodology PDF contains a date inconsistency — introductory text discusses September 2026 capabilities while the results page labels the figures as of October 2026.
- **IDs:** **None published.** Parameter count, architecture details, training compute, public model ID, latency and throughput are all explicitly absent from the launch materials.
- **Context window:** **This is the key ambiguity.** The verified disclosure is a **1,000,000-token output limit**. **No definitive commercial input-context limit is stated anywhere in the launch announcement.** Google reports long-context *evaluation* results through 1M tokens (GraphWalks at 256K–1M), which demonstrates the model operates on inputs at that scale, but benchmark coverage does not settle the eventual API limit. For reference, the peers in Google's own comparison document 1,050,000-token contexts with 128,000-token normal output (GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol) or 1M context with 128K output and a 300K batch beta (Claude Opus 5.5) — so Argon's **output** ceiling is roughly 8x the frontier norm while its **input** contract is undocumented. Also unresolved: how reasoning tokens, visible output, tool activity and the max-generation setting map onto billing.
- **Modalities:** **Not enumerated in the launch announcement.** What is verified is *understanding* capability — published chart-understanding (Chartography) and long-video (LVBench) results imply image and video input. **Understanding does not establish image, video or audio generation endpoints**, and none are documented for Argon. No audio-input evidence found. Reasoning: yes — Google ran the launch evaluations at the model's highest thinking settings, and Zapier's leaderboard lists separate "Argon High" and "Argon Medium" configurations, so effort levels exist. Tool calls: yes (LABBench 2 was run with terminal, bioinformatics software, Python, R and internet access; CWE-bench used the Antigravity harness).
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-03):** **Introductory $2.00 / 1M input and $10.00 / 1M output**, with cached input discounted **95%** — which computes to **$0.10 / 1M cached input** at the introductory rate. **After the introductory period both headline rates double to $4.00 / $20.00**, and Google **does not specify the end date**. No complete cache-fee schedule (writes, storage) has been published. Context for the value claim: at introductory rates this is 80% below GPT-6 Astra's and Claude Fable 5.1's $10/$50; after the introduction it is 60% below and exactly matches Claude Opus 5.5's $4/$20. **GPT-6.1 Sol already matches Argon's introductory $2/$10 with the same $0.10 cached read**, and Google's own Gemini 3.8 Flash is cheaper still at $0.75/$3.75 — so Argon is not uniquely inexpensive even within its own vendor's lineup.
- **Architecture:** Proprietary, closed weights. Parameter count, topology, training compute: **all undisclosed**.

### Raw benchmarks found

> All 19 rows below come from **Google DeepMind's launch comparison table** (Argon vs GPT-6 Astra, Claude Fable 5.1, Claude Opus 5.5). Google's five-page evaluation methodology makes clear this is a *compilation* of documented results with benchmark-specific methods — not one lab running every model under one configuration. Argon runs at highest thinking settings with **single-attempt evaluation** (no majority voting, no parallel test-time compute). The specific comparability caveats are recorded inline because several materially change what the numbers support. A dash means Google published no figure. Across the table Argon holds the highest number in **13 rows, ties in 1 and trails in 5**.

Agent / tool use and enterprise knowledge work:

- Terminal-Bench 2.1: no verified public score found. **Terminal-bench 4.0: 57.4%** — **trails all three rivals** (Astra 58.2%, Fable 5.1 57.9%, Opus 5.5 66.4%, a nine-point deficit).
- Tau3-Banking / Tau2-Bench: no verified public score found
- GDPval-AA: **no verified public score found** — Google published no GDPval row at all
- Claw-Eval / ClawProBench: no verified public score found
- Toolathon / MCP-Atlas / SWE Atlas Codebase QnA: no verified public score found
- **AutomationBench: 51.3%** (Astra 41.4%, Opus 5.5 42.5%, Fable 5.1 31.4%) — an 8.8-point lead over the next best. Zapier's benchmark uses **strict completion: every assertion for a task must pass**, which makes this the most demanding row in the table and the lead the most meaningful. Fable's 31.4% includes Opus 5 fallback on roughly 40% of tasks.
- **Vals Index: 68.9%** (Opus 5.5 67.0%, Fable 5.1 65.8%, Astra 63.1%) — a GDP-weighted measure across finance, coding, legal and tax; a narrow 1.9-point lead.
- **Vals Finance Agent v2: 65.4%** (Fable 5.1 58.9%, Opus 5.5 58.6%, Astra 53.5%) — scored with **weighted partial credit gated by dealbreaker requirements**, so this is not "65.4% of financial tasks fully correct".
- **Harvey's Legal Agent Benchmark: 19.6%** (Fable 5.1 6.7%, Astra 5.4%, Opus 5.5 3.8%) — roughly 2.9x the next best, from a very low base.
- **Agent's Last Exam (pass rate): 39.5%** (Opus 5.5 38.2%, Astra 34.2%, Fable —) — leads, but below half.
- **OSWorld-2.0 (offline subset, partial score): 69.2%** — **loses to Astra's 72.6%**. Argon's figure is the maximum over three runs, each single-attempt, 1080p screenshot observations, 500-step limit. Claude results are absent because comparable offline-only figures were unavailable, so **those blanks are not zeros**.
- **Gray Swan indirect prompt injection (attack success at k=15, lower is better): 0.7%** — **the best displayed result**, against Claude Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1 at 1.0%, Gemini 3.8 Flash 5.5%, 3.8 Flash Cyber 6.0% and **GPT-6 Astra 8.5%**. The 0.3-point margin over the Claude models comes with no uncertainty information, so it should not be treated as a settled statistical advantage.

Argon ran with a **six-times verifier timeout** to address verification timeout issues while rivals' figures came from the official leaderboard — so Argon lost this row *despite* a methodology concession in its favour.

Coding:

- SWE-bench Verified / SWE-Pro: **no verified public score found** for either
- LiveCodeBench: no verified public score found
- SciCode / AA-SciCode: no verified public score found
- **Vibe Code Bench: 91.9%** (Fable 5.1 and Opus 5.5 both 90.3%, Astra 89.6%) — leads Google's selected field by 1.6 points, **but Vals' own benchmark page reports Claude Sonnet 5.5 at 92.39%, above Argon**, and Sonnet 5.5 was not in Google's comparison.
- **DeepSWE v1.1: 77.9%** (Opus 5.5 74.2%, Astra 74.1%, Fable 5.1 67.4%) — **clears the methodology's 74%+ frontier reference** and leads by 3.7 points. Mixed sources: Google computed Argon's result with a **mini-swe-agent harness** while rival numbers came from a public leaderboard and model reports.
- **FrontierSWE v2: 55.0%** — **loses badly**: Astra 65.5% (−10.5), Opus 5.5 62.3% (−7.3), Fable 5.1 56.3% (−1.3). The clearest counterweight to the DeepSWE lead, and the reason model choice here should follow the actual repository workload.
- **CWE-bench v1 (vulnerability remediation): 68.0%** — **ties GPT-6 Astra** (Opus 5.5 67.0%, Fable 5.1 58.0%); **Grok 4.7 also reaches 68% pass@1** on the public leaderboard. Cost asymmetry is severe: reported average rollout costs are **Argon $6.63, Astra $2.85, Grok 4.7 $2.75, Opus 5.5 $0.79** — materially different harnesses (Antigravity / Codex / OpenCode / Claude Code).

Long context:

- **GraphWalks, up to 128K, breadth-first search F1: 99.7%** (Astra 98.7%, Fable 5.1 91.4%, Opus 5.5 90.6%) — 650 items.
- **GraphWalks, 256K to 1M, breadth-first search F1: 84.2%** (Astra 71.8%, Opus 5.5 66.8%, Fable 5.1 65.0%) — 200 problems; **+12.4 points over Astra, +17.4 over Opus, +19.2 over Fable.** This is the model's clearest displayed advantage and is more informative than a context-size claim, because it measures structured reasoning quality as input length grows. It is an F1 on requested graph reasoning, not a guarantee of complete correctness over arbitrary long documents.
- No MRCR or RULER figure found.

Vision / documents:

- **Chartography: 71.6%** (Astra 71.0%, Opus 5.5 66.3%, Fable 5.1 46.2%) — a narrow 0.6-point lead.
- **LVBench (long video): 91.7%** (Astra 87.5%, Opus 5.5 83.7%, Fable 5.1 79.7%) — a 4.2-point lead over Astra, **but the frame budgets were unequal**: 1 frame per second for Gemini against fixed budgets of 800 frames for Astra, 600 for Opus and 300 for Fable, reflecting API limits. Models did not receive identical visual evidence.

Cybersecurity (Google-internal, measured against Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber):

- **Source-code vulnerability discovery across 20 programming languages: 85.8% vs 71.0%** (+14.8) — recall of recent confirmed vulnerabilities in source code.
- **Wiz black-box penetration-test evaluation: 70.9% vs 58.2%** (+12.7) — web systems without source-code access.
- Google reports work with Wiz's Scan for Good initiative including discovery of a **critical exposure in healthcare software that earlier frontier models missed**; the launch article provides insufficient public technical detail to reproduce it.

Measured per-task cost (evaluator-reported, more useful than token price):

- **Zapier AutomationBench live results:** Argon High **51.29% at $1.70/task list, $0.85 promotional**; Argon Medium 50.08% at $1.54 / $0.77; Opus 5.5 Max with default fallbacks 42.47% at $1.44; Astra Max 41.4% at $1.73; Claude Sonnet 5.5 Max with fallbacks 44.75% at $1.14.

Google-internal deployment claims (provider-reported, not reproducible head-to-head):

- Quantum computing: improved a **published baseline for the spacetime resources of a subroutine by 40% in minutes** (qubits and gates combined) — one optimisation problem, not all quantum workloads.
- Data-centre memory: agents inspected fleet profiling telemetry and applied optimisations that **freed more than 300 TiB once deployed**, with estimated total potential of 500 TiB–1 PiB (the deployed and projected figures are different claims).
- C/C++ to Rust migrations across core libraries including re2 and libgav1, up to **more than 800,000 lines for Fuchsia's Zircon kernel**, with automated and manual audits, emulation testing and review before production.
- **libgav1:** Argon agents replaced **32,000 lines of SIMD code** through repeated profile-guided experiments and compiler inspection, producing an output-equivalent decoder **2.7x faster than the earlier Rust port** — the comparator is that Rust port, not the optimised C++ decoder.

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 90/100.** The best enterprise-agent evidence in this dataset: **AutomationBench 51.3% under Zapier's strict all-assertions-must-pass grading, 8.8 points clear of the next model**, plus outright leads on the Vals Index (68.9%), Vals Finance Agent v2 (65.4%), Harvey's Legal Agent (19.6%, ~2.9x the next best) and Agent's Last Exam (39.5%) — and the **best indirect-prompt-injection hardening on record here at 0.7% attack success**, against GPT-6 Astra's 8.5%. Four deductions hold it to 90: **Terminal-bench 4.0 at 57.4% trails all three rivals**, **OSWorld-2.0 at 69.2% loses to Astra**, **GDPval-AA, Tau3-Banking, Claw-Eval, MCP-Atlas and Terminal-Bench 2.1 are all entirely unreported** so most of the methodology's tool-use references cannot be checked, and on CWE-bench it **ties Astra and Grok 4.7 at 68% while costing $6.63 per rollout against Opus 5.5's $0.79** — an 8x cost for a one-point score difference.
- **Reasoning: 88/100.** The published reasoning evidence is genuinely strong where it exists — **GraphWalks 84.2% F1 at 256K–1M input, 12.4 points above GPT-6 Astra**, GraphWalks 99.7% at 128K, **LABBench 2 88.8%** and **RiemannBench 76.0%**, all leading their field. But the evidence gap is the largest of any model scored so far: **GPQA Diamond, HLE, MRCR/LCR, CritPt and the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index are every one of them absent** — not a single one of the methodology's five named reasoning references has a published value for this model. Two further qualifications: LABBench 2 is tool-assisted (terminal, bioinformatics software, Python, R, internet), so it measures the system rather than unaided reasoning; and **Terminal-Bench Science 0.1 at 57.6% lost to Astra by 10.5 points despite running with a six-times verifier timeout in Argon's favour**, while PostTrainBench — the single best-controlled row in Google's table — **lost to Claude Opus 5.5**.
- **Context window: 96/100.** Argon has the best *demonstrated* long-context reasoning in this dataset by a clear margin — **84.2% breadth-first-search F1 across 256K–1M-token inputs, +12.4 over Astra and +17.4 over Opus 5.5**, which is far more informative than a nominal window size because it shows reasoning quality holding up as length grows — plus a **1,000,000-token output ceiling roughly eight times the 128K frontier norm**, which materially changes what a long agent trajectory can attempt. Two constraints keep it below 98: **Google never states a commercial input-context limit**, so the input side of the contract is formally undocumented and the 1M figure describes generation, not ingestion; and the tier's ≥98%-retrieval-at-512K+ condition is unmet — GraphWalks at 84.2% is a structured-reasoning F1, not a retrieval rate, and no MRCR or RULER figure exists. The undocumented billing treatment of reasoning versus visible output tokens is a further planning gap.
- **Multimodal: 86/100.** Measured results are the best in Google's comparison on both published visual rows — **LVBench 91.7% on long video** (4.2 points over Astra, 8.0 over Opus 5.5) and **Chartography 71.6%** — which places it in the methodology's "+video/PDF in = 75–90" band on demonstrated video and chart understanding. Three things prevent the band ceiling: the **LVBench frame budgets were unequal** (1 fps for Gemini against fixed 300–800-frame budgets for rivals), so the models did not see the same evidence and the lead is partly a harness artefact; the **input modality list is never enumerated** in the launch materials, so audio input and PDF handling are unverified rather than confirmed; and there is **no non-text output** — Gemini Omni, Nano Banana and Gemini Audio are separate surfaces whose generation features do not belong to Argon.
- **Coding: 88/100.** **DeepSWE v1.1 at 77.9% clears the methodology's 74%+ frontier reference and leads the field by 3.7 points**, Vibe Code Bench 91.9% leads Google's selected comparison, and unusually for a launch claim there is concrete production evidence — 32,000 lines of libgav1 SIMD replaced to yield an output-equivalent decoder 2.7x faster than the prior Rust port, and Rust migrations up to 800,000+ lines of the Zircon kernel under audit and review. Three hard deductions: **FrontierSWE v2 at 55.0% loses to Astra by 10.5 points and Opus 5.5 by 7.3** — a lead on one software-engineering suite does not erase a deficit on another; **Terminal-bench 4.0 at 57.4% trails every rival**; and **SWE-bench Verified, SWE-bench Pro, LiveCodeBench, SciCode and Terminal-Bench 2.1 are all unreported**, while the Vibe Code Bench lead evaporates against Claude Sonnet 5.5's 92.39% on Vals' own page — a model Google omitted from the comparison.
- **Cost efficiency: 62/100.** The introductory **$2 / $10 with a 95% cached-input discount ($0.10 / 1M)** is better than the $3/$15 anchor that maps to ~60, and Zapier's measured per-task figures support it at that rate: **Argon High delivers 51.29% completion at $0.85 promotional per task, cheaper and more capable than Opus 5.5 Max ($1.44, 42.47%) and Astra Max ($1.73, 41.4%)**. Five deductions keep it near the anchor. **Both rates double to $4/$20 after the introduction with no disclosed end date** — at which point the same Zapier workload costs $1.70, above Opus 5.5 and level with Astra. **No complete cache-fee schedule exists** (writes, storage undocumented), and **reasoning-token billing against the 1M output ceiling is undocumented** — a ceiling that invites expensive unsuccessful trajectories. On CWE-bench its measured rollout cost of **$6.63 is 8.4x Opus 5.5's $0.79** for a one-point score advantage. And it is not uniquely cheap: **GPT-6.1 Sol matches $2/$10/$0.10 exactly today**, and Google's own Gemini 3.8 Flash runs at $0.75/$3.75.
- **Overall Score: 89.6/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dimensions (90 + 88 + 96 + 86 + 88) / 5 = 89.6 — the best candidate in this dataset for **enterprise knowledge work and long-input structured reasoning**: Zapier-strict business automation (AutomationBench 51.3%), finance and legal agents (Vals Finance 65.4%, Harvey 19.6%), reasoning over 256K–1M-token inputs (GraphWalks 84.2% F1), long-video analysis (LVBench 91.7%), large-scale repository migration (DeepSWE 77.9% plus real Rust-migration evidence), and defensive security research where it needs no guardrail exemption to be useful (85.8% source-code vulnerability discovery, 0.7% prompt-injection success). Keep GPT-6 Astra in contention for FrontierSWE-shaped repositories, agentic science and the OSWorld offline subset, and Claude Opus 5.5 for terminal work and post-training engineering. Three caveats dominate any adoption decision: **you probably cannot call it yet** (trusted cyber defenders and Google internal teams only, no public rollout date), **no public model ID, input-context limit, rate limits or cache terms exist to budget against**, and **the price doubles at an unannounced date**. Not a single one of the methodology's standard reasoning benchmarks (GPQA, HLE, MRCR, CritPt, Intelligence Index) has been published for this model — treat the knowledge-work and long-context leads as the verified story and everything else as open.
