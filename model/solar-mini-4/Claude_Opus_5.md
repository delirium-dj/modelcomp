## Model card

- **Name:** Solar Mini 4
- **Short description:** Upstage's **compact proprietary reasoning MoE — 35B total / 3B active** — "built for agentic use cases where response speed and cost matter", combining "fluent Korean with strong English and Japanese capabilities" ([Upstage Console](https://console.upstage.ai/docs/models/solar-mini-4)). Artificial Analysis's independent assessment is the most useful framing available and it is two-sided: the model **"sets a new Pareto optimal point on Intelligence Index vs. Active Parameters for models under 3B active parameters"**, yet it **"costs ~5x as much per task as GPT-6 Luna (max) despite similar per-token prices"** ([Artificial Analysis, 2026-09-30](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/korean-ai-lab-upstage-releases-solar-mini-4)). Distinct model; `solar-pro-4` and `solar-open-2` have their own folders.
- **Provider / access:** **Upstage Console and API**, OpenRouter, **OpenCode Zen** and other routes. **Proprietary — weights are not released.**
- **Release / knowledge:** **September 2026** (2026-09-22/23). **Knowledge cutoff: February 2026** (Artificial Analysis).
- **IDs:** `upstage/solar-mini4` (OpenRouter), `solar-mini-4` (Upstage Console, Zen). **No free tier**, but see the discount below.
- **Context window:** ⚠️ **A source conflict I could not resolve, so both figures are reported.** **Artificial Analysis states 1M tokens with 262k max output**; **OpenRouter and six independent aggregators converge on 524,288 tokens with 131,072 max output**. The most likely reading is a 1M native window with hosted endpoints capping at 524K, but I have no source confirming that. Either way, **this repo's recorded "65,536 total (16,384 out)" is wrong by at least 8×** and I have corrected it.
- **Modalities:** **Text input and output only** (Artificial Analysis, explicit). No image, audio or video. Reasoning: **yes** — AA classifies it "a new proprietary reasoning model", and it spends heavily on reasoning tokens (below). Tool calls: yes, though see the agentic results.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-08):** **$0.10 / MTok input, $0.40 / MTok output, $0.01 / MTok cache hit** — convergent across Artificial Analysis and llm-stats, and representing a **one-third cut from Upstage's previous generation**. ⚠️ **Upstage is currently running 50% off through 2026-10-22**, which is why OpenRouter lists $0.05 / $0.20. **This repo's "$0.20 / $0.80" figure is wrong** and has been corrected.
- **Architecture:** **35B total / 3B active** Mixture-of-Experts, as reported by Upstage — and Artificial Analysis is careful to note the caveat: "**As a proprietary model, its size cannot be independently verified.**"

### Raw benchmarks found

> **Unusually high-quality evidence.** BenchLM has no page (404), but **Artificial Analysis published a full independent analysis** with per-benchmark results, token accounting, cost-per-task measurement and named comparators. Every figure below is AA's own measurement, not an Upstage self-report.

Reasoning / knowledge:

- **Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index: 24.** The comparisons are what make this meaningful: **6 points above Qwen3.6 35B A3B (Reasoning) at 18, which has the identical 3B active parameters**; **1 point above Nemotron 3 Ultra, which has 55B active**; and **16 points above Upstage's own previous flagship Solar Pro 3 (8), which used 12B active.** For reference AA notes K2 Horizon MoVA 36B A4B scores 25 with 4B active.
- **AA-LCR v1.1: 83%** — "**Long context reasoning is a relative strength**", matching MiniMax-M3 and GPT-6 Luna (max), and **ahead of Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) and GPT-6 Astra (max) at 81%**
- **AA-Omniscience: −11, with 18% accuracy** — "among the lowest of the models compared" — **but it "abstains on about half of the questions" and posts a 64% non-hallucination rate**, "well ahead of Inkling (xhigh) at 32% and GPT-6 Luna (max) at 23%"

Coding:

- **SciCode: 48%** — ahead of MiniMax-M3 and Inkling (xhigh) at 47%
- **Terminal-Bench 4.0: 1%.** AA's heading is blunt: "**Agentic coding is a relative weakness.**"
- LM Market Cap composite: ranks **#246 in Coding with 40/100**
- **No SWE-bench or LiveCodeBench figure found**

Agent / tool use:

- **AutomationBench-AA: 22%**
- **GDPval-AA: 1,072 Elo**; **AA-Briefcase: 872 Elo** — both "close to Inkling (xhigh)"

Speed and cost — **the most actionable findings in this report**:

- **208 tokens/second** output speed, "faster than GPT-6 Luna (max) at 152 tokens/s"; Benchable places it in the **96th percentile for speed across eight benchmarks** and the **88th percentile for pricing**
- **But it uses 88,000 output tokens per Intelligence Index task, of which 72,000 are reasoning tokens** — "~2.5x as many output tokens as Inkling (xhigh) and ~5x as many as Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite" — so despite the fast decode it **"averages 7.1 minutes per task"** against 5.8 minutes for GPT-6 Luna (max) and 2.8 for Inkling (xhigh)
- **$0.36 per Intelligence Index task versus GPT-6 Luna (max) at $0.07** — a ~5× gap on similar per-token prices. The mechanism is cache efficiency: "**Uncached input makes up $0.30 of Solar Mini 4's $0.36 cost per task**… In our measurements, **48% of Solar Mini 4's repeated context was served from cache vs. 99% for GPT-6 Luna (max)**." Reasoning and answer tokens add only ~$0.04.

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 32/100.** The weakest dimension, and it directly contradicts Upstage's own "built for agentic use cases" positioning. **Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 1% is effectively zero**, and **AutomationBench-AA at 22%** is poor. **GDPval-AA 1,072 Elo and AA-Briefcase 872 Elo** are mid-low and, as AA notes, only "close to Inkling (xhigh)". The cache finding compounds the problem specifically for agents: AA points out that "agentic tasks such as Terminal-Bench 4.0 and AA-Briefcase **resend the growing conversation each turn, so cache hits matter**" — and this model only achieves 48% cache service. A model that is slow per task, expensive on uncached input and near-zero on Terminal-Bench is not an agentic model, whatever the marketing says.
- **Reasoning: 66/100.** Genuinely strong **for its size**, and the size-adjusted comparisons are independently verified rather than claimed: an **Intelligence Index of 24 that beats a same-size 3B-active rival by 6 points and a 55B-active model by 1**, plus a **+16-point generational gain**. The **64% non-hallucination rate with abstention on half the questions is a real virtue** — it knows what it does not know, which is the opposite of the 83–93% hallucination rates recorded elsewhere in this pass. Capped by **18% raw knowledge accuracy** and an **Omniscience index of −11**: the calibration is excellent but the underlying knowledge is thin, so it is safe rather than capable on factual work.
- **Context window: 88/100.** The standout dimension. **AA-LCR v1.1 at 83% matches GPT-6 Luna (max) and MiniMax-M3 and beats both Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) and GPT-6 Astra (max) at 81%** — a 3B-active model outperforming frontier models on long-context reasoning is the single most impressive result here, and it is independently measured. Paired with a window of **1M (AA) or 524K (hosted)** and **262k / 131k max output**, this is a model you can genuinely point at large documents. Short of the 90s only because of the unresolved window conflict and the absence of a needle-retrieval curve.
- **Multimodal: 15/100.** Text in, text out, stated explicitly by Artificial Analysis. No image, audio or video and none claimed. Template floor.
- **Coding: 42/100.** Split, with the split running along the agentic boundary. **SciCode at 48% is a good result** — ahead of MiniMax-M3 and Inkling (xhigh) — so scientific and self-contained code generation works. **Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 1% means agentic coding does not**, and LM Market Cap's **#246 / 40-of-100** composite agrees. With **no SWE-bench or LiveCodeBench figure** there is nothing to establish repository-scale capability either way, but the one agentic-coding measurement available is a floor result.
- **Cost efficiency: 58/100.** This is the most instructive score in the report, because **the headline price and the real price diverge by 5×**. On paper it is cheap: **$0.10 / $0.40 with $0.01 cache hits**, a third below the previous generation, **currently half-price through 2026-10-22**, 208 tokens/second, and the 88th percentile on pricing. In practice Artificial Analysis measured **$0.36 per Intelligence Index task against GPT-6 Luna (max) at $0.07** — because **88k output tokens per task (72k of them reasoning)** stretch each task to **7.1 minutes**, and because **only 48% of repeated context is served from cache versus 99% for the competitor**, leaving $0.30 of the $0.36 in uncached input. Add proprietary weights, no free tier, and the fact that cache misses hurt most on exactly the agentic workloads Upstage targets. Credit for the genuine Pareto efficiency per active parameter; heavy deduction for effective cost.
- **Overall Score: 48.6/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dims (32 + 66 + 88 + 15 + 42) / 5 = 48.6. The agentic floor and the text-only ceiling pull the mean well below the long-context result, which is where this model actually excels. Best fit: **long-document reasoning and summarization in Korean, English and Japanese** — AA-LCR 83% beating GPT-6 Astra is not a small thing — plus scientific code generation, and any workload where **abstaining is better than guessing** (64% non-hallucination). Avoid it for agents and terminal work (Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 1%), for factual recall (18% accuracy), and for **high-turn conversations where cache misses will multiply your bill by five**. The honest summary: an impressively efficient model per active parameter that is not efficient per task, and an "agentic" model that is not agentic — but a genuinely excellent long-context reader at a low sticker price.
