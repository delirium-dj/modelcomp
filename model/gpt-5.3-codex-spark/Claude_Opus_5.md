## Model card

- **Name:** GPT-5.3-Codex-Spark
- **Short description:** OpenAI's **first model designed for real-time coding** — "a smaller version of GPT-5.3-Codex… optimized to feel near-instant when served on ultra-low latency hardware—**delivering more than 1000 tokens per second** while remaining highly capable for real-world coding tasks" ([OpenAI, 2026-02-12](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-5-3-codex-spark/)). It is also a strategic artefact: **the first OpenAI production model deployed outside NVIDIA infrastructure**, running on **Cerebras Wafer Scale Engine 3**, and "the first milestone in our partnership with Cerebras, which we announced in January". The design intent is a different interaction model, not a cheaper one: "Codex-Spark is our first model designed specifically for working with Codex in real-time—making targeted edits, reshaping logic, or refining interfaces and seeing results immediately." Distinct model; `gpt-5-nano`, `gpt-5.4-nano`, `gpt-5.4-mini` and `gpt-oss-120b` have their own folders, and the full GPT-5.3-Codex is a separate model.
- **Provider / access:** **Research preview for ChatGPT Pro users only**, through the Codex app, CLI and VS Code extension. The API is restricted: "we are making Codex-Spark available in the API for **a small set of design partners**". Served exclusively on Cerebras WSE-3, on "the same production serving stack as the rest of our fleet". **Usage is governed by a separate rate limit** and "will not count towards standard rate limits", but "when demand is high, you may see limited access or temporary queuing as we balance reliability across users." No GA date or API timeline has been announced.
- **Release / knowledge:** **2026-02-12**, as a research preview. Knowledge cutoff: no verified public date found.
- **IDs:** `gpt-5.3-codex-spark` (Codex app/CLI/VS Code, Zen). **No free tier, no public API, no enterprise deployment option during the preview.**
- **Context window:** **128,000 tokens.** ⚠️ **Source conflict, resolved in favour of the vendor:** [BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/models/gpt-5-3-codex-spark) lists 256K, but OpenAI states directly and twice that "**At launch, Codex-Spark has a 128k context window and is text-only**" and "Codex-Spark is currently text-only at a 128k context window". OpenAI's own statement is authoritative; BenchLM's figure is an error. Max output: no verified public figure found.
- **Modalities:** **Text in → text out only**, stated explicitly by OpenAI. No image, audio or video. OpenAI names **multimodal input as future work**: "we'll introduce even more capabilities–including larger models, longer context lengths, and multimodal input." Reasoning: BenchLM classifies it as a Reasoning model; OpenAI positions it explicitly *against* deep reasoning, routing that to the full Codex. Tool calls: yes, but with an important behavioural caveat — "**Because it's tuned for speed, Codex-Spark keeps its default working style lightweight: it makes minimal, targeted edits and doesn't automatically run tests unless you ask it to.**"
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-08):** **no per-token price exists.** Access is bundled with a ChatGPT Pro subscription; the API is design-partner-only. Notably, OpenAI is candid that this is not a cost play: "**GPUs remain foundational across our training and inference pipelines and deliver the most cost effective tokens for broad usage.** Cerebras complements that foundation by excelling at workflows that demand extremely low latency."
- **Architecture:** A **smaller, distilled derivative of GPT-5.3-Codex**; parameter count undisclosed. The hardware is the substantive architectural fact. **Cerebras Wafer Scale Engine 3** is, per Digital Applied's specification summary, a **single 46,255 mm² wafer carrying 4 trillion transistors and 900,000 AI-optimized cores at 125 petaflops peak — roughly 19× the transistor count of an NVIDIA B200**, with on-chip memory that removes the data-movement bottleneck and a single-chip design that avoids multi-GPU communication overhead. Alongside the model, OpenAI shipped **pipeline latency work that benefits every model**: "Through the introduction of a persistent WebSocket connection and targeted optimizations inside of Responses API, we **reduced overhead per client/server roundtrip by 80%, per-token overhead by 30%, and time-to-first-token by 50%**." The WebSocket path is default for Codex-Spark and "will become the default for all models soon."

### Raw benchmarks found

> **Two provenance caveats, stated up front.** First, [BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/models/gpt-5-3-codex-spark) has **0 of 626 benchmarks** for this model ("Benchmark data is coming soon"), and I found **no Artificial Analysis, Vals AI or other independent measurement** — unsurprising for a preview with no public API. Second, OpenAI's launch post **describes** its benchmark results in prose but presents the actual figures only as charts: "On SWE-Bench Pro and Terminal-Bench 2.0, two benchmarks evaluating agentic software engineering capability, GPT-5.3-Codex-Spark demonstrates strong performance while accomplishing the tasks in a fraction of the time compared to GPT-5.3-Codex." **I do not read numbers off vendor charts.** The figures below are as transcribed and published by [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/gpt-5-3-codex-spark-real-time-coding-cerebras-guide), which labels them explicitly: "Benchmarks are reported by OpenAI based on internal evaluations. Results may vary with different prompting strategies and hardware configurations."

| Benchmark | **Codex-Spark** | GPT-5.3-Codex | GPT-5.2-Codex |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Terminal-Bench 2.0** | **77.3%** | 77.3% | 68.1% |
| **SWE-bench Pro** | **72.8%** | 75.1% | 65.4% |
| Tokens/second | **1,000+** | ~67 | ~85 |
| Time-to-first-token | **~120 ms** | ~240 ms | ~200 ms |
| Context window | 128K | 128K | 128K |

The headline result is the pairing: **Terminal-Bench 2.0 at 77.3% is identical to the full GPT-5.3-Codex**, and SWE-bench Pro at 72.8% is only **2.3 points behind** it — while running at roughly **15× the generation speed**. Both figures are also well clear of GPT-5.2-Codex (68.1% and 65.4%). The ~67 and ~85 tok/s comparators and the "15×" framing are Digital Applied's; OpenAI's own text claims only ">1000 tokens per second".

**Benchmark methodology OpenAI does disclose**, which is worth recording because duration claims are easy to game: "Duration is estimated as the sum of (1) output generation time (output tokens ÷ sampling speed), (2) prefill time (prefill tokens ÷ prefill speed), (3) total tool execution time, and (4) total network overhead."

**Safety:** "Codex-Spark includes the same safety training as our mainline models, including cyber-relevant training. We evaluated Codex-Spark as part of our standard deployment process… and determined that it **does not have a plausible chance of reaching our Preparedness Framework threshold for high capability in cybersecurity or biology**." No numbers published.

**Absent entirely:** no GPQA Diamond, MMLU-Pro, AIME, HLE, ARC-AGI, CritPt, LiveCodeBench, SWE-bench Verified, DeepSWE, SciCode, GDPval, Toolathlon, τ²-bench, BFCL, MCP-Atlas, MRCR, AA-LCR or any multimodal benchmark. The entire public evidence base for this model is **two coding numbers and a throughput figure**.

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 68/100.** **Terminal-Bench 2.0 at 77.3% is a strong agentic-terminal result** — near the top of this dataset, and notable for matching its much larger parent exactly. The real-time interaction model is a genuine tool-use capability of a different kind: "You can collaborate with the model in real time, **interrupting or redirecting it as it works**." But OpenAI itself scopes the model away from autonomous operation, and the two most important sentences for this score are the vendor's own: it "**makes minimal, targeted edits and doesn't automatically run tests unless you ask it to**", and the full Codex remains the recommendation for "long-running agentic coding loops". With **no GDPval, Toolathlon, τ²-bench, BFCL or MCP figure**, one benchmark is carrying this dimension.
- **Reasoning: 48/100.** This dimension is **essentially unmeasured, and the vendor positions the model against it.** BenchLM classifies it as a Reasoning model but holds zero benchmarks; there is **no GPQA, MMLU-Pro, AIME, HLE, ARC-AGI or CritPt figure of any kind**. The only inferential evidence is favourable — it is a distillation of GPT-5.3-Codex and holds Terminal-Bench parity with it, which is hard to do without real reasoning — but OpenAI explicitly routes "multi-file feature implementation" and "large-scale refactor" to the full model because those "need deeper reasoning". Scored below the midpoint on genuine absence of evidence plus the vendor's own framing, not on demonstrated weakness.
- **Context window: 46/100.** **128K, confirmed directly by OpenAI** — and I am scoring the vendor's figure rather than BenchLM's uncorroborated 256K. In late 2026 that is a small window for a coding model, it is "smaller than the 1M token windows offered by some competitors", and **there is no retrieval measurement at any depth**. OpenAI treats it as a known limitation rather than a feature, naming "longer context lengths" as explicit future work. Credit for a clearly stated, honestly scoped number and for the fact that 128K is adequate for the single-file interactive work the model actually targets; no credit for breadth or demonstrated recall.
- **Multimodal: 15/100.** Text in, text out, stated twice by OpenAI and listed as a current limitation with multimodal input promised later. Template floor; the score reflects scope at preview stage, not failure.
- **Coding: 82/100.** The model exists for this and delivers. **Terminal-Bench 2.0 at 77.3% exactly matches the full GPT-5.3-Codex** and beats GPT-5.2-Codex by 9.2 points; **SWE-bench Pro at 72.8% is a high absolute score** on the harder SWE variant and sits just 2.3 points behind its parent. Achieving that at **~120 ms time-to-first-token and 1,000+ tokens/second** is the actual achievement — it is not a cheaper model that scores well, it is a fast model that did not lose accuracy. Capped below the high 80s for three reasons: the scope is deliberately narrow (single-file edits, minimal diffs, no automatic test execution), **no LiveCodeBench, SWE-bench Verified, DeepSWE or SciCode figure exists**, and the two numbers that do exist are OpenAI's internal evaluations read off charts by a third party, with no independent reproduction anywhere.
- **Cost efficiency: 38/100.** The weakest dimension, and it is about access rather than waste. **There is no per-token price at all** — you reach this model through a ChatGPT Pro subscription or not at all, since the API is limited to "a small set of design partners", with **no free tier and no enterprise deployment option**. Capacity is constrained by wafer-scale manufacturing and Cerebras datacenter geography, with explicit queuing under load. And OpenAI states plainly that GPUs "deliver the most cost effective tokens for broad usage" while Cerebras serves latency — so by the vendor's own account this is not the economical path. Against that: the throughput is extraordinary, and the accompanying infrastructure work (**−80% roundtrip overhead, −30% per-token overhead, −50% time-to-first-token**) is real value that accrues to every OpenAI model, not just this one. Scored low for unpriceability and gated access, not for poor value-per-token, which cannot be computed.
- **Overall Score: 51.8/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dims (68 + 48 + 46 + 15 + 82) / 5 = 51.8. The number understates what the model is good at and fairly represents how little of it has been measured: **two coding benchmarks, a text-only 128K window, and no reasoning, long-context or multimodal evidence whatsoever.** Best fit: exactly what OpenAI says — **interactive pair-programming inside an IDE** — inline completions, single-file bug fixes, targeted refactors, plan revision and contextual Q&A, where a sub-200-millisecond round trip changes how you work rather than just how long you wait. Route multi-file refactors, deep debugging and architecture-level work to the full Codex. Two things to know before relying on it: **it will not run your tests unless you ask**, and **access is a ChatGPT Pro subscription away with no API and no price**. The broader significance is strategic rather than numeric — this is OpenAI's first production model off NVIDIA silicon, and the first member of what it calls "a family of ultra-fast models".
