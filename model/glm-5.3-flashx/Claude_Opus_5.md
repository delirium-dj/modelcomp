## Model card

- **Name:** GLM-5.3-FlashX. **Open weights.** **Critical variant/alias finding, which governs this entire report: FlashX is not a distinct model — it is the high-speed serving tier of GLM-5.3-Flash.** OpenRouter describes it as "the high-speed variant of Z.ai's GLM-5.3-Flash", and apimaster.ai as "Zhipu's high-speed serving tier of GLM-5.3-Flash, launched September 18, 2026 with up to 200 tokens/s". **The weights are the same; what differs is throughput and price.** Accordingly, the five quality dimensions below are assessed on GLM-5.3-Flash's measured results and **carry the same values as my GLM-5.3-Flash findings file in this research pass** — the divergence between the two reports lives entirely in Cost efficiency. This is the same situation as `qwen-3.8` / `qwen-3.8-max`, handled the same way.
- **Short description:** A fast-serving tier of **Z.AI's first natively multimodal GLM-5 model**, launched **2026-09-18** at up to **200 tokens/s**. The underlying model is remarkable for its class: a composite tracker places GLM-5.3-Flash **#53 of 784 at 57.37/100**, but two of its results beat Z.AI's own 753B flagship. **GDPval-AA at 1,773 Elo clears the methodology's ~1750+ frontier reference and is the highest GDPval figure found anywhere in this research pass — above GLM-5.3's own 1,769.** **Toolathlon-Verified at 78.4% is the highest Toolathlon figure in this pass**, above GLM-5.3 (73.0%) and Kimi K3 (76.5%). It also has the modality advantage its flagship lacks: **MMVU at 80.5% evidences video input** and **CharXiv at 89.4%** is excellent chart reasoning, where GLM-5.3 is text-only. The catch is specific to this tier: **you pay roughly 3.4× the base Flash rate for identical weights.**
- **Provider / access:** Open weights for the underlying model at `zai-org/GLM-5.3-Flash` on Hugging Face under the **MIT licence**. FlashX as a served tier is available from **2 providers** (against GLM-5.3-Flash's 15), including OpenRouter. Launch post at `z.ai/blog/glm-5.3-flash`.
- **Release / knowledge:** **FlashX launched 2026-09-18.** **No verified knowledge cutoff found.**
- **IDs:** `glm-5.3-flashx` (OpenRouter `z-ai/glm-5.3-flashx`); base tier `glm-5.3-flash`. Self-hostable under MIT, so effectively free-tier if you own the hardware.
- **Context window:** **1,048,576 tokens** with **maximum output 131,072 tokens** — both verified by four independent aggregators (tokenrate.dev, usagepricing.com, OpenRouter, cloudprice.net). **Note a discrepancy worth flagging: cloudprice.net lists the base GLM-5.3-Flash at 1.3M context against FlashX's 1,048,576, so the high-speed tier may carry a *reduced* window.** **AA-LCR at 80.0%** against **MLCR-AA at 51.1%** — the long-context evidence is good but uneven.
- **Modalities:** **Text and image input → text output, and video input is evidenced.** OpenRouter and modelcompare.dev both describe it as **"a native multimodal model"**; **MMVU at 80.5%** is a video-understanding result, and **OfficeQA Pro at 62.4%** evidences document handling. No audio path or non-text output found. Vision input may be billed above the token rate — modelcompare.dev notes "vision pricing per image is usually billed on top of the regular token rate", unverified for this ID.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-03):** **$0.37 / 1M input, $1.25 / 1M output** — agreed by **four** aggregators (tokenrate.dev, usagepricing.com, OpenRouter, cloudprice.net), all citing live OpenRouter data as of September 2026. **The commercially decisive comparison: the base GLM-5.3-Flash tier is listed from $0.110 / 1M input (cloudprice.net, 15 providers) and as low as $0.025 / 1M (anymodel.org) — so FlashX costs roughly 3.4× to 15× the base rate for the same weights, buying throughput only.** The underlying model is also reported to run at **one-tenth of GLM-5.2's price** (benchgen). **No primary Z.AI rate card was reached; re-verify before budgeting.**
- **Architecture:** **Open weights, MIT licence. 320B total parameters with 18B active — a sparse MoE** (benchgen), which is what makes the 200 tok/s serving tier feasible. **Caveat: high-throughput serving tiers sometimes apply more aggressive quantization than the base tier. No source confirms or denies this for FlashX, so the assumption of identical quality to GLM-5.3-Flash is stated rather than proven.**

### Normalized scores (1–100)

> **The five quality dimensions below are identical to my GLM-5.3-Flash findings in this research pass, because the weights are identical.** Only Cost efficiency differs, and that difference is the entire point of this report.

- **Tool use: 87/100.** Two pass-leading results anchor this. **GDPval-AA at 1,773 Elo clears the ~1750+ frontier reference and is the highest GDPval figure found anywhere in this research pass** — above Z.AI's own 753B flagship — which means that on the one benchmark explicitly designed to measure economically valuable real-world work, a ~320B/18B-active model leads the field. **Toolathlon-Verified at 78.4% is also the highest in this pass.** **Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 84.3% is corroborated to the decimal by Artificial Analysis**, and **AutomationBench reaches 48.8% vendor / 60.4% independent** with **AA ITBench at 51.2%**. Four deductions: **Vals AI measures Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 62.9%, a 21.4-point gap** against the two agreeing sources; **AA Tau3 Banking at 47.2% misses the Tau-family reference by 2.8 points**; **AA Terminal-Bench 4.0 (32.8%), AA EnterpriseOps-Gym (33.2%), Agents' Last Exam (26.3%) and GDP.pdf (15.4%) all fall away** on harder or document-grounded work; and **Claw-Eval, OSWorld, MCP-Atlas, CyberGym and the AA Agentic Index are all unreported**, with the methodology explicitly penalising the Claw-Eval absence.
- **Reasoning: 82/100.** Solid for the size class but short of the frontier on the measures that matter most. **AA-GPQA Diamond at 91.2% clears the 90%+ reference** (independently 86.4% at Vals, a 4.8-point spread), **AA-LCR reaches 80.0%**, **MMLU-Pro is 86.1%**, and **the AA-Omniscience Index is positive at +7.5%** — a genuinely good reliability signal, though it cannot be decomposed because accuracy and hallucination components were not published for this model. Four deductions: **AA-HLE at 39.9% misses the 40%+ reference by 0.1 points**; **the Intelligence Index of 41.8% is 18.2 points below the 60+ marker**; **CritPt at 15.4% is weak** and **MLCR-AA at 51.1% is 28.9 points below AA-LCR**, showing long-context strength that does not hold across harnesses; and **neither ARC-AGI at any version nor FrontierMath has any published figure**, so abstract reasoning and research mathematics are entirely unmeasured.
- **Context window: 95/100.** **1,048,576 tokens** places it in the ≥1M tier (95–100), with **maximum output 131,072 tokens** verified by four aggregators, and **AA-LCR at 80.0%** confirms usable long-context reasoning. Being open weights under MIT, there is no vendor repricing threshold. It sits at the tier floor rather than above it for three reasons: the 100 condition requires **≥98% retrieval at 512K+** and **no MRCR, RULER, CorpusQA or GraphWalks figure exists**; **MLCR-AA at 51.1% is 28.9 points below AA-LCR**, so depth performance is harness-dependent; and **cloudprice.net lists the base Flash tier at 1.3M against FlashX's 1,048,576**, raising the possibility that this serving tier carries a *reduced* window relative to the model's capability.
- **Multimodal: 86/100.** **MMVU at 80.5% evidences video input**, which places it in the methodology's "+video/PDF in = 75–90" band, and the measured breadth inside that band is genuinely good. **CharXiv at 89.4% is excellent chart reasoning — among the highest found in this research pass**, ahead of GPT-5.4 (82.8%) and Claude Sonnet 4.6 (77.4%); **Chartography with tools reaches 78.0%**; and **OfficeQA Pro at 62.4% beats GPT-5.4's 53.2%** on office-document work. Both OpenRouter and modelcompare.dev describe it as natively multimodal. It is placed near the band ceiling rather than at it because **BabyVision at 53.4% marks a real limit on harder visual reasoning**, **no MMMU or MMMU-Pro figure exists at all** — a conspicuous omission for a natively multimodal model, and the benchmark most peers in this pass are measured on — and there is **no OCR or audio capability**. **This is the dimension where the Flash line decisively beats Z.AI's flagship**, which is text-only at 18/100.
- **Coding: 88/100.** Strong, and the best evidence is independent. **SWE-bench at 92.0% from Vals AI** is behind only DeepSeek V4 Pro, Grok 4.6 and GLM-5.3 in this entire research pass, which is remarkable for an 18B-active model; **LiveCodeBench at 80.5%** matches the 753B flagship exactly; and **Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 84.3% is within 0.7 points of the 85%+ coding reference** with vendor-independent agreement to the decimal. Four deductions: **DeepSWE at 63.4% is 10.6 points below the 74%+ reference**; **AA-SciCode at 51.6% misses its reference by 3.4 points**; **FrontierSWE v2 at 18.1% is very low** and well below the flagship's 30.2%, indicating weak performance on genuinely novel engineering; and **no AA Coding Index figure exists** where the flagship posts 74.8%, alongside missing SWE-bench Verified, SWE-bench Pro, Codeforces and CursorBench.
- **Cost efficiency: 74/100. This is the only dimension where FlashX differs from GLM-5.3-Flash, and it is a substantial markdown.** In absolute terms the rate is good: **$0.37 / $1.25 is roughly 8× cheaper than the methodology's $3/$15 anchor**, the window is 1M+ with 131K output, **the weights are MIT-licensed and public** so the rate card is escapable entirely, and **200 tok/s is genuinely valuable** — in agentic loops where dozens of sequential turns compound, throughput is a real cost, not a vanity metric, and a 320B/18B-active MoE is the right architecture to deliver it. What drives the score down by fourteen points relative to the base tier is that **FlashX is dominated on price by its own sibling for identical weights**: **cloudprice.net lists GLM-5.3-Flash from $0.110 / 1M input and anymodel.org from $0.025 / 1M — so FlashX costs roughly 3.4× to 15× more for the same model**, and the only thing purchased is speed. Three further deductions: **only 2 providers serve FlashX against 15 for the base tier**, so there is little price competition and more lock-in risk; **vision input may be billed above the token rate** and that rule was not verified; and **no primary Z.AI rate card was reached** — all four price sources are aggregators citing OpenRouter. Unless your workload is latency-bound, the base Flash tier is the correct purchase.
- **Overall Score: 87.6/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dimensions (87 + 82 + 95 + 86 + 88) / 5 = 87.6 — **identical to GLM-5.3-Flash, which is the correct result, because FlashX is the same weights served faster.** This is the strongest open-weight Flash-tier model in this research pass, and three findings justify that. **GDPval-AA at 1,773 Elo clears the frontier reference and is the highest GDPval figure found anywhere here — beating Z.AI's own 753B flagship, Claude Fable 5, GPT-5.6 Sol and DeepSeek V4 Pro** — so on economically valuable real-world work, a 320B/18B-active MoE under an MIT licence leads the field. **Toolathlon-Verified at 78.4% also leads this pass.** And **it has the modality breadth its flagship lacks**: MMVU 80.5% for video, CharXiv 89.4% for charts, OfficeQA Pro 62.4% for documents, against GLM-5.3's text-only 18/100. Add **SWE-bench 92.0% (Vals)**, **Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 84.3% corroborated to the decimal**, **AA-GPQA Diamond 91.2%**, **AA-LCR 80.0%** and a **positive +7.5% reliability index**. Choose it for **multimodal agentic work, chart and document analysis, repository coding and latency-sensitive loops**. Four cautions. **The commercial case for this specific tier is weak** — the base Flash tier is 3.4× to 15× cheaper for identical weights from 15 providers instead of 2, so buy FlashX only if you are genuinely latency-bound. **No benchmark has been run against FlashX itself**; every figure here is GLM-5.3-Flash's, and high-throughput tiers can carry heavier quantization, which no source confirms or denies. **The Terminal-Bench headline needs a discount** — 84.3% from two agreeing sources against 62.9% at Vals. And **reasoning has hard limits**: AA-HLE misses 40% by 0.1 points, the Intelligence Index is 41.8%, CritPt is 15.4%, and there is no ARC-AGI or FrontierMath measurement at all.
