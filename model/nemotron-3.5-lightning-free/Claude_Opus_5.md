## Model card

- **Name:** Nemotron 3.5 Lightning 30B-A3B
- **Short description:** NVIDIA's **open 30B-total / 3B-active hybrid Mamba-2 + MoE + attention model**, built for a specific and unusually well-defined role: "**the execution layer of always-on agents**. It is designed for harnesses like **OpenClaw and Hermes Agent** — all supported by the **NVIDIA NemoClaw open source security and management stack** for running always-on AI agents" ([NVIDIA Developer](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-nemotron-3-5-lightning-delivers-fast-accurate-specialized-task-execution-for-long-running-agents/)). The intended pattern is explicit: this is the fast executor you pair with a frontier planner, not the planner itself. Distinct model; `nemotron-3-ultra-free` has its own folder, and Nemotron 3 Super and Nemotron 3 Nano Omni are separate models.
- **Provider / access:** **Open weights under the commercial-use OpenMDW licence**, with **near-lossless NVFP4 quantization** and optimized local deployment on **NVIDIA DGX Spark**. Hosted via NVIDIA's own endpoints and **OpenCode Zen**, where a **free tier** is available, plus an NVIDIA trial.
- **Release / knowledge:** **2026-08-11.** Knowledge cutoff: no verified public date found.
- **IDs:** `nemotron-3.5-lightning-30b-a3b` and an `-nvfp4` quantized variant; `nemotron-3.5-lightning-free` (Zen). **Free Zen tier and NVIDIA trial access.**
- **Context window:** ⚠️ **Source conflict, reported rather than resolved.** llm-stats records **262,144 tokens (262K)**; Benchgen and an independent spec review both state **1M**. This repo's metadata says 262,144 native. The likeliest reading is a 262K native window with a 1M extended mode, but no source I found confirms that. Max output: no verified public figure found.
- **Modalities:** **Text in → text out only.** No image, audio or video. Reasoning: yes — NVIDIA lists "reasoning, chat, RAG, coding, and agentic workflows" as target uses. Tool calls: yes, and central to the design.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-08):** **$0.080 / MTok input, $0.040 / MTok cached input, $0.200 / MTok output** (llm-stats), plus **free access on OpenCode Zen and via NVIDIA trial**, and free self-hosting under OpenMDW.
- **Architecture:** **30B total / 3B active**, and the composition is the interesting part — a **hybrid stack combining Mamba-2 state-space layers, mixture-of-experts routing, and conventional attention**. It ships with **DSpark speculative decoding** for lower latency and **NVFP4 quantization** described as near-lossless, both of which exist to serve the always-on-agent use case where the model is called constantly.

### Raw benchmarks found

> **Four NVIDIA-published headline figures, plus one independent composite for the quantized variant.** The four scores below are "NVIDIA's published numbers for the **BF16 checkpoint**" (Layer3Labs). Separately, [BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/models/nemotron-3-5-lightning-30b-a3b-nvfp4) profiles the **NVFP4** variant with 13 benchmark rows and a much less flattering composite — the two should not be conflated, since one is the full-precision checkpoint and the other a quantization.

NVIDIA's published results (BF16):

- **PinchBench: 85.37** — the most significant figure here. PinchBench is a real-world **OpenClaw agent** benchmark, which makes it the benchmark that matches this model's stated purpose most directly, and 85.37 is a high score on it.
- **MMLU-Pro: 81.94** — strong for 3B active parameters
- **GPQA Diamond: 75.44**
- **SWE-bench Verified: 51.56**

Independent composite, for the quantized variant:

- **BenchLM (NVFP4): 26.9 / 100, rank #200 of 220**, across 13 benchmark rows, with "its **strongest eligible category** [being] **Instruction Following at #67**". BenchLM also notes the general caveat for open weights: "Published weights can be self-hosted, but infrastructure cost varies and is not a comparable API token rate."

**Absent entirely:** no Terminal-Bench (any version), BFCL, τ²-bench, GDPval, AutomationBench, Toolathlon, MCP-Atlas or BrowseComp figure; no HLE, AIME, ARC-AGI, CritPt, Omniscience or hallucination measurement; no LiveCodeBench, SWE-bench Pro or DeepSWE figure; and **no MRCR, RULER or AA-LCR measurement at any depth** despite a 262K-or-1M window.

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 70/100.** The best-aligned evidence in this report. **PinchBench 85.37** is a high score on a real-world OpenClaw agent harness — and OpenClaw is one of the two harnesses NVIDIA explicitly built this model for, so this is the benchmark that matters most for the stated use case rather than a convenient one. The surrounding engineering supports the claim: **DSpark speculative decoding, NVFP4 quantization and the NemoClaw security-and-management stack** all exist specifically to make always-on agent execution cheap and reliable. Held out of the high 70s because the supporting evidence is absent rather than negative — **no Terminal-Bench, BFCL, τ²-bench, GDPval, AutomationBench or MCP figure exists at all** — and because BenchLM's NVFP4 profile ranks the quantized variant #200 of 220, which suggests the broader agentic picture may be weaker than one strong number implies.
- **Reasoning: 66/100.** **MMLU-Pro 81.94 and GPQA Diamond 75.44 from a model activating 3B parameters** are genuinely strong — comparable to models an order of magnitude larger in active compute, and consistent with the hybrid Mamba-MoE design being an efficient use of capacity. Capped because **no benchmark designed to resist memorization has been run**: there is no HLE, AIME, ARC-AGI or CritPt figure, and critically **no Omniscience or hallucination measurement at all**, so nothing is known about how it behaves when it does not know something — a material gap for a model intended to run unattended.
- **Context window: 60/100.** A **262K window is solid and a 1M window would be excellent**, but the two figures come from different sources and I cannot adjudicate between them, so I score conservatively on the lower. The architecture makes a long window plausible — **Mamba-2 state-space layers carry sequence information in fixed-size state**, which is exactly how models of this size afford long context. But **no retrieval measurement of any kind exists** — no MRCR, RULER, needle test or AA-LCR — and no max-output figure is published, so usable recall at depth is entirely unverified.
- **Multimodal: 15/100.** Text in, text out. No image, audio or video input and none claimed. Template floor.
- **Coding: 56/100.** **SWE-bench Verified at 51.56 is a respectable result for 3B active parameters** and establishes that real repository patching works, and NVIDIA lists coding among the target workloads. But the coverage is thin and the quantized profile is discouraging: **no LiveCodeBench, SWE-bench Pro, Terminal-Bench or DeepSWE figure**, and BenchLM's NVFP4 composite of 26.9/100 at rank #200 of 220 does not suggest a coding-strong model. Mid-band on one solid number.
- **Cost efficiency: 93/100.** Near best-in-class, and for reasons beyond the token price. The **OpenMDW licence permits commercial use**, **3B active of 30B** keeps inference cheap, **near-lossless NVFP4 quantization** cuts memory further, **DSpark speculative decoding** cuts latency, and the model is **optimized for local deployment on a DGX Spark** — a single workstation-class box. Hosted rates are very low at **$0.08 / $0.20 with $0.04 cached input**, and there is a **free OpenCode Zen tier plus an NVIDIA trial**. For an always-on agent that makes thousands of calls a day, the combination of a permissive licence, local deployment, speculative decoding and a free tier is close to ideal. Docked only because the headline capability ceiling means many tasks will still escalate to a larger planner — which is, to be fair, exactly the architecture NVIDIA recommends.
- **Overall Score: 53.4/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dims (70 + 66 + 60 + 15 + 56) / 5 = 53.4. Read it as a **role-specific score**: NVIDIA did not build this to top leaderboards, it built it to be the cheap, fast, locally-deployable executor underneath a frontier planner, and on the one benchmark that measures that role — **PinchBench 85.37 on an OpenClaw harness** — it performs. Best fit: **high-volume, low-latency task execution inside always-on agent stacks** — OpenClaw and Hermes Agent workflows, RAG retrieval and synthesis, scoped code edits, and routine tool calls — self-hosted on a DGX Spark or run free on Zen, with a stronger model handling planning. Two honest cautions: **the evidence base is four vendor numbers and one unflattering third-party composite**, with no agentic benchmark beyond PinchBench and no hallucination data at all; and **the context window is either 262K or 1M depending on which source you read**, with no retrieval measurement either way.
