## Model card

- **Name:** Qwen3.8-Max, released **2026-08-02** via Qwen's official blog and QwenCloud; open weights followed on **2026-08-12**. **Roughly eight weeks old.**
- **Short description:** Qwen's most capable model to date and **the first open-weight model at Max scale**. A **2.4-trillion-parameter MoE with 95B active** (512 experts, 10 routed plus 1 shared), built on the Qwen 3.5-family hybrid architecture of **Gated DeltaNet + MoE + Gated Attention**. Qwen frames it around four claims: **autonomous coding** — "10+ days of self-evolving development from an empty folder to production without hand-holding," with the complete project trace published; **production-quality deliverables** across hundreds of specialised legal, financial and design tasks; **long-horizon mastery** — "system-level autonomous planning with closed-loop adaptive learning, driving 500+ turns of chip design optimization"; and **native multimodal intelligence** in which "vision functions as a continuous feedback loop for planning, execution, and self-correction," not merely an input the model reads once.
- **Provider / access:** QwenCloud (managed API), Alibaba Cloud Model Studio, Qwen Studio, plus DeepInfra, Fireworks, Novita and Together. Self-hosting via vLLM, SGLang, TokenSpeed or Transformers.
- **Release / knowledge:** API released 2026-08-02, open weights 2026-08-12. Training data is current to roughly mid-2026.
- **IDs:** hosted API `qwen3.8-max`; open weights `Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B` on Hugging Face and ModelScope.
- **Context window:** **1M on the hosted API**, with maximum input/output of **991.80K / 131.07K tokens**; Artificial Analysis measures it at 984k. The **open weights are 262,144 native, extensible to roughly 1.01M** — so the self-hosted model has a materially smaller native window than the API. Max output is 131.07K on the API and 131.1K on third-party providers.
- **Modalities — the API and the weights are different products.** The **hosted `qwen3.8-max` is text, image and video in**, text out, with built-in tools and optional thinking. The **open weights are text-only, with thinking required-on, and no built-in tools or vision** — you bring your own. Artificial Analysis states explicitly that Qwen3.8 2.4T A95B has **no image input support**, contrasting it with Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, which does. This is the single most important procurement fact in this report.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-09-28), per 1M, first-party QwenCloud:** **$2.00 input / $6.00 output / $0.25 implicit cache / $2.50 explicit cache create / $0.17 explicit cache read. Flat across the entire 1M window — no long-context price tiers.** Limits: **2M TPM and 15K RPM**. Third-party: DeepInfra $1.65/$0.206/$4.95, Fireworks $2.00/$0.250/$6.00, Novita $2.00/$0.250/$6.00, Together $2.50/$0.500/$6.25. Artificial Analysis' blended figure is **$1.18/M** at a 7:2:1 cache-hit/input/output ratio.
- **Speed:** **39.2 output tokens/s and 2.93s TTFT** for the hosted model; 37.7–40.0 tokens/s and 2.63–3.02s TTFT for the open weights. Fireworks reports 113 c/s; Novita reports 5 c/s.
- **Open-weights licence — not Apache or MIT.** The custom **Qwen3.8-Max License** carries real commercial conditions: retain copyright and permission notices; **if your commercial product or service exceeds 100M MAU or $20M monthly revenue, prominently display the model name in the UI**; **if you run a Model-as-a-Service or AI Work Assistant business whose aggregate revenue exceeds $50M TTM, you must obtain a separate licence from Qwen before commercial use** — with an internal-use carve-out where you do not expose the model, its outputs or its capabilities to third parties. Artificial Analysis classifies it as open weights but **"commercial use restricted,"** while the hosted API runs under QwenCloud's terms of service.

The v4.3 leaders are **Claude Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra on 53, Claude Opus 5 on 51, Claude Fable 5 on 50, Muse Spark 1.3 on 48 and GPT-5.6 Sol on 47** — meaning Qwen3.8 Max now sits **behind Muse Spark 1.3 and GPT-5.6 Sol** on the current independent composite. apxml's older index readings were 0.47 for the Intelligence Index, 0.50 for the Agentic Index and 0.72 for the Coding Index, all at rank 18, 18 and 31.

**Vals AI:** **SWE-bench 85.60%, #17 of 88.** Vals dates the release 2026-08-03.

**apxml:** GPQA 0.926 (#10), LiveBench Global 0.78 (#9), LiveBench Average 0.78 (#9), LiveBench Data Analysis 0.78 (#13), LiveBench Reasoning 0.88 (#15), LiveBench Mathematics 0.91 (#17), LiveBench Agentic 0.65 (#4), Agent Arena 0.06 (#16), **WebDev Arena 1686 (bronze, #3)**, Text Arena 1480 (#20).

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 84/100.** Up 2. **Terminal Bench 2.1 at 86.6%** is the anchor, and Qwen's disclosure of the harness — Claude Code, avg@10, five-hour timeout, 131,072 max tokens — makes it directly comparable to the Anthropic TB 2.1 figures rather than flattering by default. **CoWorkBench 74.8%** (in-house, so weighted accordingly) and **LiveBench Agentic 0.65 at #4** support it. The 10+ day autonomous build and 500+ turn chip-design optimization are the real differentiators if they hold. Held at 84 for three gaps: **AutomationBench, BrowseComp, τ³-Banking, Toolathlon and MCP Atlas figures are all absent**, so tool use rests heavily on Terminal-Bench; **Agent Arena at 0.06 ranks #16**, which is weak; and **Artificial Analysis' Agentic Index places it at 0.50 and #18** — mid-table for a flagship. The 10-day demonstrations are published with a project trace, which is more than most vendors offer, but they remain vendor-run.
- **Reasoning: 85/100.** Down 1. Strong vendor-side numbers: **GPQA Diamond 92.6%** (apxml 0.926, #10), **HLE 43.6%**, a **2.7-point AIME 2024 gain**, **LiveBench Reasoning 0.88 (#15)**, **LiveBench Mathematics 0.91 (#17)**, **LiveBench Global 0.78 (#9)**, and **PaperBench 93.0%, up from 64.8%** — the largest single jump on Qwen's card, and on a benchmark (research-paper reproduction) that few models are evaluated on at all. Held at 85 rather than higher on the decisive independent measure: **Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3 places Qwen3.8 Max (0902) at 45, behind Muse Spark 1.3 at 48 and GPT-5.6 Sol at 47**, and the model scored **58 on the older v4.1.1 index** — it has lost ground as the evaluation suite hardened, which is the opposite of what usually happens to a two-month-old flagship. CritPt, GDP.pdf and AA-Omniscience component scores are not published individually for it.
- **Context window: 90/100.** Up 2. **1M on the API with 991.80K maximum input and 131.07K maximum output**, measured at 984k by Artificial Analysis, plus an **MRCR v2 256K 8-needle** result on the vendor card. The commercially important detail is that **pricing is flat across the entire 1M window with no long-context tiers**, at **2M TPM and 15K RPM** — that combination is rarer and more useful than a larger headline window. Held at 90 because the captured MRCR score was truncated and therefore cannot be used, 1M is table stakes in this dataset, and **the self-hosted weights have only 262,144 native context** even though they extend to roughly 1.01M.
- **Multimodal: 86/100.** Up 2. The **hosted API accepts text, image and video**, and the distinctive claim is architectural rather than tabular: vision operates as "a continuous feedback loop for planning, execution, and self-correction" across the entire task cycle, enabling "deep semantic analysis of ultra-long documents and extended video content" in long-horizon tasks. Held at 86, and this score applies to the **API only** — **the open weights are text-only with no image input support**, which Artificial Analysis states explicitly. A team that self-hosts Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B gets no vision at all. No MMMU-Pro, Video-MMMU or MathVision figure is published for either artifact in the sources available.
- **Coding: 85/100.** Up 3. **SWE-bench Pro 67.7%** (vendor, up from 60.6%), **Vals SWE-bench 85.60% at #17 of 88**, **Terminal Bench 2.1 86.6%**, **PaperBench 93.0%**, **IFBench 82.8%**, and **WebDev Arena at 1686 — bronze, #3, ahead of Claude Opus 4.8's 1563**. Held at 85 for two specific reasons. First, **no vendor SWE-bench Verified figure exists** — Vals' 85.60% is the only Verified number, and it is a third-party run. Second, **Artificial Analysis' Coding Index places the model at 0.72 and #31**, which is poor for a flagship of this class and sits oddly beside the third-place WebDev Arena result. Also unmeasured here: **LiveCodeBench, Codeforces, deepSwe and FrontierSWE are all absent**, so the competitive-coding profile that carries DeepSeek V4.1 Flash's 3,471 Codeforces rating is simply unknown for this model.
- **Cost efficiency: 80/100.** Up 14 — the largest cost movement in this batch, and the most defensible. **$2.00/$6.00 with $0.25 implicit and $0.17 explicit cache reads, flat across the whole 1M window, at 2M TPM and 15K RPM** is a genuinely strong commercial position. Against **Kimi K3 at $3/$15** that is 33% cheaper on input and 60% cheaper on output; against **Claude Opus 4.8 and Opus 5 at $5/$25** it is **2.4× cheaper on input and 4.2× cheaper on output**; against Gemini 3.8 Flash at $0.75/$3.75 it is dearer but in a different capability class. **DeepInfra at $1.65/$4.95 is cheaper still**, and Artificial Analysis' blended figure is $1.18/M. Held at 80 rather than higher because the self-hosted path is not free in any practical sense — 2.4T parameters with 95B active is a substantial cluster — and because **provider throughput varies enormously**, from Fireworks' 113 c/s to Novita's 5 c/s at identical price. The **2.4T weights under a licence that triggers a separate commercial agreement above $50M TTM revenue for MaaS** is a real constraint that a pure price-per-token comparison misses.
- **Overall Score: 86.0/100.** Half-up mean of the five quality dims: (84 + 85 + 90 + 86 + 85) / 5 = 86.0. Best fit: **the most capable open-weight model in the world and the best-value frontier-adjacent API in this dataset, with a catch that procurement teams will trip over.** Qwen delivered the first Max-scale open-weight model — 2.4T parameters, 95B active, downloadable — at $2/$6 with flat 1M-window pricing, and its vendor card shows genuine step-change gains over Qwen3.7-Max: **Terminal Bench 2.1 74.5 → 86.6, SWE-bench Pro 60.6 → 67.7, PaperBench 64.8 → 93.0**. **WebDev Arena bronze at #3** confirms the coding-front-end strength independently. The two facts that qualify this: **on the current Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3 the model scores 45 and sits behind Muse Spark 1.3 and GPT-5.6 Sol**, having scored 58 on the previous version of the same index; and **the hosted API and the open weights are materially different products** — multimodal with built-in tools versus text-only, vision-less, thinking-required and tool-less. If you want vision and 1M native context you must buy the API; if you want the weights you get neither.

The **open weights `Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B`** (2026-08-12) are **text-only with no image input support**, **thinking required-on**, **no built-in tools** (bring your own), and **262,144 native context extensible to roughly 1.01M** rather than 1M out of the box. A team that self-hosts for cost reasons and then discovers it has no vision capability has made an expensive mistake. The move from Terminal-Bench v2.1 to v4.0 in the index is the likely cause — the vendor's headline **Terminal Bench 2.1 86.6%** is a number on a benchmark version the index has already moved past.
