## Model card

- **Name:** Qwen3.8-Max. **Open weight** — the official model card is published on Hugging Face as **`Qwen/Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B`**, which is a significant and unusual fact: this is an open-weight model at the top of Alibaba's range, ranked **#16 of 783 on a composite benchmark tracker (72.12/100)** — the highest-placed Alibaba model and ahead of every other Qwen entry by nearly nine points. Variant/alias flag: a **Qwen3.8 Max Preview** exists as a separate, earlier sibling; the Qwen3.8 line also includes **Qwen3.8-Flash-Next**, **Qwen3.8-27B** and **Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash**, none of which are this model.
- **Short description:** Alibaba's flagship Qwen3.8 model, documented in Qwen's own release benchmarks. The model ID encodes its architecture directly: **2.4 trillion total parameters with 95 billion active** — a sparse mixture-of-experts at frontier scale, released with public weights. Its profile is **exceptionally broad rather than narrow**: 61 published benchmarks spanning agentic, coding, multimodal, long-context, knowledge and instruction-following, with **24 distinct multimodal and grounded benchmarks alone** — the most comprehensive multimodal evidence base in this research pass.
- **Provider / access:** Alibaba Cloud DashScope, Hugging Face weights (`Qwen/Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B`) and third-party hosts. **Open weights mean self-hosting is legally available**, though at 2.4T total parameters this is a datacentre-scale deployment rather than a workstation one. Reasoning: explicit reasoning mode.
- **Release / knowledge:** Released in the Qwen3.8 cycle. **No verified public release date or knowledge cutoff found.**
- **IDs:** `Qwen/Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B` (Hugging Face model card). Sibling: Qwen3.8 Max Preview.
- **Context window:** **1,000,000 tokens**, and unusually for this dataset it comes with **strong published retrieval evidence: MRCRv2 at 92.9%** plus LongBench v2 at 66.3%. Max output: no verified public figure found.
- **Modalities:** **Text, image and video input → text output**, evidenced directly by the benchmark set: five separate video benchmarks (Video-MME with subtitle 90.4%, VideoMMMU 88.7%, MLVU 90.8%, MMVU 82.4%, LVBench 81.8%) plus extensive document and OCR coverage. **No audio input and no non-text output** documented or benchmarked. **No vendor specification sheet was retrievable**, so the modality list is inferred from the benchmark set.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-03):** **No rate card was verified for this model.** The relevant cost fact is structural rather than per-token: **the weights are public**, so the per-token rate card is escapable for any organisation able to run a 2.4T-parameter MoE. **Re-verify the live hosted rate before budgeting** — the Cost efficiency score below is explicitly flagged as resting on the open-weight licence rather than a confirmed price.
- **Architecture:** **Sparse Mixture-of-Experts — 2.4 trillion total parameters, 95 billion active per token**, per the Hugging Face model card identifier. Expert count, routing topology, layer count and training corpus: not disclosed in the sources reached.

### Raw benchmarks found

> **61 of 645 tracked benchmark slots are populated**, with per-row source attribution. **(Qwen)** = Qwen's own Qwen3.8-Max release benchmarks; **(Vals)** = Vals AI's independent leaderboards; plus four further independent leaderboards (VulcanBench, OpenHarmony Bench, Proximal's FrontierSWE v2). **The vendor-versus-independent gaps on this model are the largest in this research pass and are called out row by row — they are the single most important thing on this page.**

Agent / tool use:

- **Terminal-Bench 2.1: 86.6% (Qwen)** but **67.4% (Vals, independent)** — **a 19.2-point gap, the largest vendor-versus-independent discrepancy recorded anywhere in this research pass.** The vendor figure approaches the methodology's ~88% frontier anchor; the independent figure sits in the mid band.
- **OSWorld-Verified: 86.1% (Qwen)** versus **OSWorld 2.0: 19.4% (Qwen)** — **a ~67-point gap between two figures Qwen published itself.** Different benchmark versions, but the magnitude means the 86.1% cannot be read as general computer-use capability.
- Tau2-bench / Tau3-Banking: **no verified public score found** — notable, since sibling Qwen models publish strong τ² and τ³ figures.
- GDPval-AA: **no verified public score found.**
- Claw-Eval / ClawProBench: **no verified public score found** — also notable, since Qwen3.6-Plus has one.
- MCP Atlas: **no verified public score found.** **Toolathlon-Verified: 72.5% (Qwen)**
- **AndroidWorld: 85.3% (Qwen)** and **MobileWorld: 77.8% (Qwen)** — strong mobile-UI agency, and the best AndroidWorld figure in this dataset.
- **WideResearch: 81.9% (Qwen)**; **CoWorkBench: 74.8% (Qwen)**; **skillsBench: 70.2% (Qwen)**; **WebArena-Verified: 66.8% (Qwen)**; **JobBench: 53.4% (Qwen)**; **Agents' Last Exam: 52.4% (Qwen)**

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 83/100.** The breadth is real and some results are category-leading: **AndroidWorld at 85.3% is the best mobile-agency figure in this dataset**, MobileWorld 77.8% corroborates, **WideResearch 81.9%** and **Toolathlon-Verified 72.5%** are solid, **HLE with tools at 56.2%** shows genuine tool-augmented reasoning, and OSWorld-Verified reaches 86.1%. Four deductions hold it to 83, and the first is serious. **Terminal-Bench 2.1 drops from Qwen's 86.6% to Vals AI's independently measured 67.4% — a 19.2-point gap, the largest vendor-independent discrepancy in this entire research pass**, which forces a discount on every unverified vendor row here. **Qwen's own two computer-use figures differ by ~67 points** (OSWorld-Verified 86.1% against OSWorld 2.0 19.4%). **AutomationBench at 27.3%** is weak on end-to-end business execution. And **GDPval-AA, Tau2/Tau3, Claw-Eval and MCP Atlas are all unreported** — four of the methodology's six tool-use references have no value, which is striking given that sibling Qwen models publish τ², τ³ and Claw-Eval figures.
- **Reasoning: 88/100.** Two frontier references are met and the corroboration is unusually strong in the right direction: **GPQA Diamond at 92.6% from Qwen is independently measured *higher* at 93.7% by Vals AI** — when an independent lab beats the vendor's own claim, confidence should rise rather than fall — and **HLE at 43.6% clears the 40%+ reference**, with Qwen reporting the same figure with and without tools. **MRCRv2 at 92.9%** is strong long-context reasoning, **MMLU-Pro is independently confirmed at 88.6%**, and **IFBench at 82.8% is the best instruction-following figure in this dataset**. Capped at 88 by a significant evidence gap rather than by weak results: **no Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index value exists for this model**, **no CritPt figure**, and **no hallucination or Omniscience measurement at all** — so general-intelligence standing and factual reliability are both entirely unmeasured, which matters more given the Terminal-Bench discrepancy above.
- **Context window: 96/100.** A **1,000,000-token window** places it in the ≥1M tier (95–100), and it earns above the floor on evidence most peers lack: **MRCRv2 at 92.9%** is genuine retrieval measurement and among the better figures in this dataset, with **LongBench v2 at 66.3%** alongside it. It stops short of the ceiling because the tier's 100 condition needs **≥98% retrieval at 512K+** and 92.9% falls short, the measurement length is not specified, and **max output tokens are undocumented**. No independent replication of the MRCR figure was found.
- **Multimodal: 89/100.** **The most comprehensive multimodal evidence base in this research pass — 24 distinct benchmarks** spanning vision, documents, OCR, video, medical imaging, screen understanding and embodied reasoning. The standouts are genuinely category-leading: **CharXiv at 88.4% *without tools* is the best unaided chart-reasoning figure in this dataset**, ahead of Claude Opus 4.8 (80.5%); **MathVision 95.2%** approaches saturation; **OmniDocBench 1.5 at 92.1%** and OCRBench V2 74.2% give a strong document stack; and **five separate video benchmarks all land between 81.8% and 90.8%**, which confirms video input far more convincingly than a single score would. That places it at the top of the methodology's "+video/PDF in = 75–90" band. It cannot exceed 90 because **there is no audio input and no non-text output**, and two results temper the picture: **ZeroBench at 24.0%** and PerceptionBench at 63.5% show the hardest perceptual reasoning remains out of reach, and all 24 figures are vendor-reported with no independent multimodal replication.
- **Coding: 86/100.** The independently measured results are strong and carry the score: **SWE-bench at 85.6% and LiveCodeBench at 87.9%, both from Vals AI**, are among the best in this dataset and do not depend on Qwen's harness, while **VulcanBench v3 at 81.2%** adds a third independent datapoint. **SWE-bench Pro at 67.7% beats GPT-5.6 Sol and GPT-5.5**, and PaperBench reaches 93.0%. Three deductions: **DeepSWE at 56.6% is roughly 17 points below the 74%+ frontier reference**; **FrontierSWE collapses from Qwen's 73.5% to 15.8% on Proximal's independent v2 leaderboard** — a ~58-point gap that, even allowing for a harder benchmark version, is the starkest reliability warning here; and **SciCode and Vibe Code Bench are absent**, with NL2Repo (55.9%) and MLS-Bench Lite (41.0%) both mid-to-low.
- **Cost efficiency: 80/100.** **This score carries an explicit evidentiary caveat: no rate card was verified for this model.** What is verified, and what drives the score, is structural: **the weights are public on Hugging Face as `Qwen/Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B`**, so unlike every proprietary peer in its benchmark class the per-token rate card is escapable entirely for organisations able to self-host. That is a genuine and rare cost-structure advantage for a model ranked #16 of 783. Three deductions: at **2.4 trillion total parameters self-hosting is a datacentre project**, not a workstation one, so the escape is theoretical for most teams; the **hosted price is unconfirmed** and must be re-verified before budgeting; and **no free tier is documented**. The Qwen Max tier is historically Alibaba's most expensive, so the hosted rate should not be assumed to match the cheap Plus tier.
- **Overall Score: 88.4/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dimensions (83 + 88 + 96 + 89 + 86) / 5 = 88.4 — the strongest open-weight model in this research pass and the best multimodal evidence base in it. Choose it for **document, chart, OCR and video pipelines**, where **CharXiv 88.4% unaided is the best in this dataset**, OmniDocBench 1.5 reaches 92.1% and five video benchmarks all clear 81%; for **mobile and screen automation** (AndroidWorld 85.3%, MobileWorld 77.8%, ScreenSpot Pro 84.5%); for **independently verified coding** (SWE-bench 85.6% and LiveCodeBench 87.9%, both Vals-measured); for **long-context work** (MRCRv2 92.9%); and for **any deployment where owning the weights matters** — no proprietary peer at this benchmark standing offers that. Three cautions must govern adoption. **Discount the vendor numbers**: Terminal-Bench 2.1 falls 19.2 points under independent testing and FrontierSWE falls ~58 points on its v2 leaderboard, so treat Qwen's unreplicated figures as upper bounds and run your own harness. **The agentic reference set is largely missing** — no GDPval-AA, no τ², no τ³, no Claw-Eval, no MCP Atlas — so long-horizon autonomy is unmeasured, and AutomationBench at 27.3% is not encouraging. And **general intelligence and factual reliability are entirely unmeasured**: there is no Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index value, no CritPt and no hallucination rate for this model at all.
