## Model card

- **Name:** Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber. Proprietary, **Reasoning**. **Variant/alias finding: this is a cybersecurity fine-tune of Gemini 3.8 Flash, not a general-purpose model.** Google DeepMind describes it as built for "finding, validating and patching vulnerabilities", and it is distributed through the **Fairwind Program** — a restricted-access arrangement.
- **Short description:** A domain specialist, and **the benchmark tracker leaves it unranked with no public Overall score, assigning it just 3 of 613 benchmark slots.** Only three figures exist: **CyberGym at 86.2%**, **CWE-Bench at 47.2%** and **Gray Swan IPI at 6.0%**. On the first of those, a striking inversion: **DeepSeek V4.1 Flash — a general-purpose model costing $0.15/$0.60 — scores 88.1% on CyberGym, and MiMo-V2.6-Flash reaches 95.1%, both above Google's dedicated security fine-tune at 86.2%.** On CWE-Bench the gap is wider still: **DeepSeek V4.1 Flash reaches 55.0% against this model's 47.2%.** The specialist does not lead its own domain. Two further warnings: **prompt-injection resistance is 6.0%**, and the tracker records the context window as **"Coming soon"**, suggesting the model is announced rather than generally available.
- **Provider / access:** **Restricted — available via the Fairwind Program only.** Google published a joint announcement for "Gemini 3.8 Flash and Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber" (`blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/3-8-flash-and-3-8-flash-cyber/`), which supplies two of the three rows.
- **Release / knowledge:** **No verified release date or knowledge cutoff found.** **The tracker lists the context window as "Coming soon"**, which implies the model is not yet generally available and that published figures are preliminary.
- **IDs:** `google/gemini-3-8-flash-cyber`; base model `gemini-3.8-flash`. **No free-tier ID, and no public pricing of any kind** — access is program-gated.
- **Context window:** **Disputed. This repository's curated metadata records 1,048,576 tokens with 65K maximum output; the benchmark tracker records "Coming soon".** I could not resolve which is current — the conflict is recorded rather than silently resolved. **No long-context measurement of any kind exists** for this variant: no AA-LCR, MRCR, RULER, CorpusQA or GraphWalks figure.
- **Modalities:** **Text and code input → text and code output** (curated metadata). **No image, audio, video or PDF path is documented**, which is a notable narrowing from the base model — Gemini 3.8 Flash is omnimodal with **CharXiv at 86.2%, LVBench at 87.1% and AA-MMMU-Pro at 85.6%**. For a vulnerability-analysis workload, that loss of diagram, screenshot and PDF-input capability is a real functional reduction, not a cosmetic one.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-03):** **No public pricing. Access is restricted to the Fairwind Program** (curated metadata: "Restricted Fairwind Program (no public pricing, no Zen Free ID)"). **There is no rate card to evaluate and no free tier**, so cost cannot be planned at all — this is a procurement-by-negotiation product, not a metered API.
- **Architecture:** Proprietary, closed weights. Parameter count and topology: not disclosed. **It is a fine-tune of Gemini 3.8 Flash**, whose weights are also closed.

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 72/100.** One genuinely strong result, heavily discounted by what surrounds it. **CyberGym at 86.2%** shows real capability in agentic vulnerability discovery, and for a security-specialised model that is the benchmark that matters most. But it is **not leading, and notably it is beaten on its own specialty by two general-purpose open-weight models** — MiMo-V2.6-Flash at 95.1% and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash at 88.1% — which is the central fact about this model: **the specialist does not beat the generalists at the task it was built for.** Three further deductions. **Gray Swan IPI at 6.0% means it resists indirect prompt injection only about one time in seventeen**, which for a tool-invoking security agent operating on untrusted code and attacker-controlled artefacts is a serious operational risk — an adversarial payload in a scanned repository is exactly the threat this should handle. **Eight agentic reference benchmarks are entirely unreported**, including Claw-Eval, whose absence the methodology explicitly penalises. And the 6.0% figure comes from **another model's launch chart** rather than this model's own materials, so even the safety number has weak provenance.
- **Reasoning: 70/100. This is an evidence-absence score and is labelled as such.** **Not one reasoning or knowledge benchmark exists for this variant** — no GPQA, HLE, ARC-AGI, FrontierMath, MMLU-Pro, CritPt, Intelligence Index, AA-IFBench or AA-Omniscience. The base model's strong figures (**AA-GPQA Diamond 95.3%, ARC Prize-verified ARC-AGI-2 89.2%, MMLU-Pro 90.2%, AA-Omniscience +29.6% on 54.6% accuracy**) are **deliberately not credited here**, because domain fine-tuning for vulnerability work can materially shift general reasoning and this report has direct evidence that this fine-tune underperforms generalists on its own specialist benchmarks — so assuming it retains the base model's general capability would be unsupported. The score sits mid-range rather than low because a CyberGym result of 86.2% requires substantial reasoning to achieve, so capability in the target domain is evidenced even though the methodology's instruments cannot measure it. **There is also no AA-Omniscience measurement, so the hallucination behaviour is unknown** — and across this research pass that figure has ranged from 25.0% to 96.5%, which for a model whose output is security patches is the highest-stakes unknown here.
- **Context window: 88/100.** Not confidently scored — recorded as such on contested evidence. The two available sources **disagree**: this repository's curated metadata states **1,048,576 tokens with 65K maximum output**, while the benchmark tracker records **"Coming soon"**. The "Coming soon" entry is a signal that the model may not be generally available and that figures are preliminary, so the higher figure is treated as intended rather than confirmed. The score is placed in the 500K–1M band rather than the ≥1M tier for three reasons: **the specification is disputed with no primary Google source confirming it**, **there is no long-context measurement of any kind** — no AA-LCR, MRCR, RULER, CorpusQA or GraphWalks — so nothing demonstrates usable depth, and **the 65K output ceiling is modest** against the 128–131K common across this pass and DeepSeek's 384K. For a vulnerability-scanning workload, a large context is genuinely valuable, which is why the score is not lower despite the uncertainty.
- **Multimodal: 14/100.** **Text and code only**, which caps it in the methodology's "text-only = 10–20" band regardless of the base model's capabilities. **This is a functional narrowing from the base model**: Gemini 3.8 Flash accepts image, audio, video and PDF input and posts **CharXiv 86.2%, LVBench 87.1% and AA-MMMU-Pro 85.6%**, and **none of that transfers to this variant** — the curated metadata documents no non-text input path, and **no multimodal benchmark of any kind exists for it**. For security work that loss is concrete rather than cosmetic: architecture diagrams, UI screenshots, network diagrams and vendor PDF advisories are common inputs to vulnerability analysis, and this variant cannot accept them. Placed at the bottom of the band rather than the top because there is no visual or non-text capability of any kind to credit.
- **Coding: 70/100.** Assessed on the one relevant benchmark, discounted for the fine-tune's demonstrated shortfall. **CWE-Bench at 47.2% is the figure most directly aligned with the stated purpose of "finding, validating and patching vulnerabilities", and it sits 8 percentage points below DeepSeek V4.1 Flash's 55.0% on the closely related CWE-bench v1** — again, a general-purpose model beating the specialist on the specialist's own task. Four further deductions: **no SWE-bench of any variant exists**, so repository-scale patching cannot be checked at all; **no DeepSWE, LiveCodeBench, AA Coding Index, SciCode, CursorBench or ProgramBench**; and **the base model's general coding figures (DeepSWE 73.8%, AA Coding Index 76.3%, CursorBench 3.2 69.2%) are not attributed to this variant and cannot be assumed to carry over**, since the fine-tune provably lost ground on its own domain benchmarks. The score credits a real, if second-place, specialist capability rather than assuming general coding parity with the base model.
- **Cost efficiency: 20/100.** **There is no rate card, no free tier and no metered pricing — access is restricted to the Fairwind Program and cost is negotiated.** That alone makes the dimension unplannable rather than merely expensive. Three further problems compound it. **You cannot benchmark it before committing**, since there is no free or low-cost evaluation path. **The value proposition is weak on its own terms**: a program-gated, negotiated-cost specialist that scores **86.2% on CyberGym against open-weight generalists at 95.1% (MiMo-V2.6-Flash) and 88.1% (DeepSeek V4.1 Flash)**, and **47.2% on CWE-Bench against 55.0% (DeepSeek V4.1 Flash)** — where the competitors are **self-hostable for free** and DeepSeek's is additionally available at **$0.15/$0.60 off-peak with cache hits at $0.003**. And **prompt-injection resistance of 6.0%** imposes a supervision cost that no rate card would show, since outputs on attacker-controlled inputs cannot be trusted without review. The score is not lower only because the model is genuinely restricted rather than publicly priced, so the comparison is against what it costs to *use* rather than what it costs to buy — and the capability gap is the deciding factor.
- **Overall Score: 62.8/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dimensions (72 + 70 + 88 + 14 + 70) / 5 = 61.6 — **and the honest headline is the inversion, not the score: this dedicated security fine-tune does not lead its own domain.** **MiMo-V2.6-Flash scores 95.1% on CyberGym against this model's 86.2%, and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash scores 88.1% on CyberGym and 55.0% on CWE-bench against this model's 47.2%** — and both are **open-weight, self-hostable generalists**, one of them available at **$0.15/$0.60 off-peak**. Meanwhile this model is **program-gated with no public pricing, has no free evaluation path, and its prompt-injection resistance is 6.0%** — resisting indirect injection roughly one time in seventeen, which for an agent that reads untrusted code and advisories is a real operational exposure. It is also **text-and-code only**, losing the base model's image, audio, video and PDF input that security work often needs, and the tracker still lists its context window as **"Coming soon"**, suggesting the published figures are preliminary. Two things argue mildly in favour. **CyberGym at 86.2% is a genuinely strong absolute result** and does evidence real capability in the target domain. And **the model is a Gemini 3.8 Flash fine-tune**, so if it is ever released with transparent pricing and its general capability is measured, it could be evaluated properly. Until then: **if you need vulnerability discovery or patching today, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash or MiMo-V2.6-Flash beat this model on the relevant benchmarks, are self-hostable, and cost a fraction as much.**
