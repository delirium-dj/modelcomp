## Model card

- **Name:** Claude Sonnet 5 — the Sonnet-tier model preceding Claude Sonnet 5.5. No free API tier. Variant/alias flag: **superseded by Claude Sonnet 5.5 on 2026-09-28 at an identical $2/$10 price**, which is the decisive commercial fact here — a composite tracker scores Sonnet 5.5 at **83.42 against this model's 67.37**, a 16-point gap for the same money.
- **Short description:** Anthropic's mid-tier workhorse of the Claude 5 generation, documented in its own system card. Its standout credential is **an Artificial Analysis Agentic Index of 44.3% — the highest figure recorded anywhere in this research pass**, ahead of Claude Opus 4.8 (42.6%) and far ahead of most frontier models. Paired with **GDPval-AA at ~1,600 Elo** and **SWE-bench Verified at 85.2%**, it was an unusually capable mid-tier model for its moment.
- **Provider / access:** Claude API (`claude-sonnet-5`, Messages API), Amazon Bedrock, Google Cloud, Microsoft Foundry and Claude Platform on AWS. Reasoning: **Adaptive thinking** with a `high` default effort.
- **Release / knowledge:** Released in the Claude 5 cycle, ahead of Claude Opus 5 (2026-07-24). **No verified public release date or knowledge cutoff found for this specific model.**
- **IDs:** `claude-sonnet-5` (Claude API and most platforms); `anthropic.claude-sonnet-5` (Amazon Bedrock pattern). **No free-tier ID.**
- **Context window:** **1M tokens input, 128K max output**, and **eligible for the 300K Batch-output beta** with the `output-300k-2026-03-24` header — an eligibility its far more expensive stablemates Claude Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 do not have. Tokenizer caveat: 1M tokens is roughly 555k words on the current tokenizer. Published long-context retrieval is **mediocre — GDM-MRCR v2 at 81.5%** (see benchmarks).
- **Modalities:** Input: **text and images only** → output: text. No audio, video or PDF path; no non-text output.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-03):** **$2 / MTok input, $10 / MTok output**, with **cache read $0.20** and **Batch at $1 / $5**. One concrete disadvantage against its successor: the **minimum cacheable prompt is 1,024 tokens on Sonnet 5, halved to 512 on Sonnet 5.5**, so short system prompts cannot be cached here. The tokenizer is identical between the two, so token counts are directly comparable.
- **Architecture:** Proprietary, closed weights. Parameter count, active parameters and topology: not disclosed.

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 85/100.** The headline is genuinely notable: **an Artificial Analysis Agentic Index of 44.3% is the highest recorded in this entire research pass**, which is a strong independent signal that this mid-tier model handles agentic loops better than most frontier models. **GDPval-AA at ~1,600 Elo is corroborated within 5 Elo by two separate sources**, BrowseComp reaches 84.7%, OSWorld-Verified 81.2%, Harvey LAB-AA 90.1% and HLE-with-tools 57.4%. Five deductions hold it to 85: GDPval remains ~150 Elo short of the ~1750+ reference; **Terminal-Bench falls from 80.4% vendor to 74.5% independent and collapses to 14.6% on v3.0 and 10.3% on v4.0**; **AutomationBench at 10.7% and ApprenticeBench at 16% are both near-floor** on real business and GUI automation; the vendor's own OSWorld figures span 24 points across versions; and **Tau2-bench, Tau3-Banking, Claw-Eval, MCP-Atlas and Toolathlon are every one of them unreported** — five of six methodology references have no value, triggering the explicit penalty.
- **Reasoning: 85/100.** **HLE clears the 40%+ frontier reference on every no-tools measurement** (43.2% Anthropic, 43.1% restated, 41.3% AA — unusually tight agreement across three sources) and reaches 57.4% with tools; **AA-LCR at 82.0%** and LABBench2 at 80.1% are solid; MMLU-Pro is independently confirmed at 87.5%. Four deductions: **GPQA Diamond clears the 90%+ reference under Artificial Analysis (91.1%) but not under Vals AI (88.9%)**, so the frontier threshold is contested; **the Intelligence Index of 38.2% is well below the 60+ marker** and the conflicting 55 from Google's older index cannot be reconciled; **HLE-Verified drops to 31.0% under Google's harness**, a 12-point third-party penalty; and **GDM-MRCR v2 at 81.5% is weak** while CritPt (16.9%) and AA-Omniscience accuracy (40.1%) are both mid-to-low.
- **Context window: 93/100.** A **1M-token input window with 128K synchronous output** places it in the ≥1M tier, and it carries a real commercial advantage: **eligibility for the 300K Batch-output beta that Claude Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 — at five times the price — do not have**. It sits below the tier floor because the published retrieval evidence is actively weak rather than merely absent: **GDM-MRCR v2 at 81.5%** is 15 points below what Qwen3.8-Max achieves and 15.5 below Gemini 3.7 Flash, meaning roughly one needle in five is missed. **AA-LCR at 82.0%** corroborates that mid-grade picture. The post-4.7 tokenizer further means 1M tokens holds about 555k words.
- **Multimodal: 68/100.** Input is **text and images only** with text-only output, placing it in the methodology's "+image in = 60–70" band, and it does **not** earn the top of that band. **CharXiv at 77.0% without tools is respectable**, and AA-MMMU-Pro reaches 77.3%. But three results drag it down: **Chartography at 15.6% is the weakest multimodal figure recorded in this entire research pass** — its own successor lifted it 46 points to 61.6%, which quantifies exactly how poor it was; **SWE Multimodal at 28.1%** shows vision does not transfer to multimodal engineering; and **GDP.pdf at 28.0%** is weak document handling. A published LVBench score of 68.5% contradicts the documented image-only input surface and was not credited.
- **Coding: 87/100.** **SWE-bench Verified at 85.2%** is strong even after independent measurement trims it to 79.6%, **the AA Coding Index at 71.5% clears the methodology's 70%+ frontier reference**, **SWE Multilingual reaches 78.3%**, LiveCodeBench is independently measured at 82.4%, and **VulcanBench CII v1 at 89.2% from an independent frontier evaluation** is a genuinely strong coding-integrity result. Four deductions: **DeepSWE v1.1 at 53.8% is roughly 20 points below the 74%+ frontier reference**; **CursorBench collapses from 61.5% on v3.2 to 34.1% on v4.0**, a 27-point version-to-version fall measured by Cursor itself; **AA-SciCode at 54.3% falls fractionally short** of the 55%+ reference and FrontierCode 1.1 reaches only 42.7%; and SWE Multimodal at 28.1% is poor.
- **Cost efficiency: 58/100.** At **$2 / $10** it beats the $3/$15 anchor that maps to ~60, with **cache reads at $0.20**, Batch at $1/$5, and 300K Batch-output eligibility that models at five times the price lack. For its moment it was excellent value — a mid-tier model with the best Agentic Index in this dataset and SWE-bench Verified at 85.2%. The score lands just below the anchor rather than above it for one decisive reason: **Claude Sonnet 5.5 shipped at exactly the same $2/$10 with a composite score of 83.42 against this model's 67.37** — a 16-point capability gain for zero additional cost — plus a halved 512-token minimum cacheable prompt that makes caching work on short system prompts. Two further deductions: the 1,024-token caching floor here wastes spend on short prefixes, and there is no free tier. Paying Sonnet 5 prices for Sonnet 5 capability is only rational where an acceptance suite is already pinned to this exact model.
- **Overall Score: 83.6/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dimensions (85 + 85 + 93 + 68 + 87) / 5 = 83.6 — a strong mid-tier model that its own successor obsoletes at identical pricing. Its genuine credentials are worth stating: **the highest Artificial Analysis Agentic Index in this entire dataset at 44.3%**, GDPval-AA at ~1,600 Elo corroborated by two sources, SWE-bench Verified 85.2%, an AA Coding Index of 71.5% that clears the frontier reference, VulcanBench CII v1 at 89.2%, and HLE clearing the 40%+ reference under three separate measurements. Choose it for **agentic loops and autonomous research** (Agentic Index 44.3%, BrowseComp 84.7%, OSWorld-Verified 81.2%) and for **repository coding at mid-tier cost** — provided your acceptance suite is already validated against it. Do not choose it for **business-process automation** (AutomationBench 10.7%, ApprenticeBench 16%), for **chart or visual work** (Chartography 15.6% is the worst figure in this dataset), for **the hardest terminal agency** (Terminal-Bench 3.0 at 14.6%, 4.0 at 10.3%), or for **genuinely long contexts** (GDM-MRCR v2 81.5%). And for any new build the recommendation is unambiguous: **Claude Sonnet 5.5 costs the same and scores 16 composite points higher**, including a 46-point Chartography improvement that fixes this model's single worst weakness.
