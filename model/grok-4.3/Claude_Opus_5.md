## Model card

- **Name:** Grok 4.3. Proprietary, **Reasoning**, `base` variant.
- **Short description:** xAI's **April 2026** enterprise reasoning flagship. A composite tracker places it **#68 of 784 at 53.61/100** across 30 benchmarks — but the composite badly obscures what this model actually is, because **its profile is the most sharply bimodal found anywhere in this research pass.** It is **elite** at four things: **τ²-bench at 97.7%** (second-highest in this pass), **AA-IFBench at 81.3%** — **the highest instruction-following figure found anywhere in this pass** — **GPQA Diamond clearing 90% under three separate measurements**, and most notably **an AA-Omniscience Index of +18.0% on a 25.0% hallucination rate, which is the single best reliability profile found anywhere in this entire research pass**, ahead of GLM-5.3's +14.3% / 29.6%. It is **near-worst** at autonomous agency: **an AA Agentic Index of 17.2%** and **GDPval-AA at 1,018 Elo**, both the lowest recorded in this pass, with **Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 41.9%** and **an AA Coding Index of 42.3%**. The repository's curated description — "elite support-agent tool use and science reasoning" — is **precisely validated**, and the precision matters: the strength is *conversational, schema-bounded* tool use, not autonomous multi-step work.
- **Provider / access:** xAI API, OpenRouter. Independent measurement via Artificial Analysis and Vals AI.
- **Release / knowledge:** **April 2026** (repository metadata). **No verified knowledge cutoff found.**
- **IDs:** `grok-4.3` (OpenRouter `x-ai/grok-4.3`). **No free-tier ID.**
- **Context window:** **1,000,000 tokens.** **But note the vendor's own price schedule discourages using it: the rate doubles at a ≥200K prompt** (see Pricing). **AA-LCR at 64.3% is weak for this nominal size** — materially below GLM-5.3 (79.7%), DeepSeek V4 Pro (80.3%) and GPT-5.4 (82.0%), all at the same 1M. Max output: no verified figure found.
- **Modalities:** **Text and image input → text output.** Image input is confirmed by **MMMU-Pro at 78.1%, measured identically by two sources.** No audio, video or PDF path; no non-text output.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-03, two-tier by prompt length):**
  - **$1.25 / 1M input, $2.50 / 1M output for prompts under 200K**
  - **$2.50 / 1M input, $5.00 / 1M output for prompts at or above 200K**
  - The sub-200K output rate of **$2.50 is six times cheaper than the methodology's $3/$15 anchor on the output leg**, which is the leg that matters most for a reasoning model emitting long chains. **The doubling at 200K is the structural catch** — using the advertised 1M window costs twice as much per token as staying inside 200K.
- **Architecture:** Proprietary, closed weights. Parameter count and topology: not disclosed.

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 58/100. This dimension requires splitting, because a single number misrepresents the model badly.** On **bounded, schema-driven, conversational tool calling it is excellent**: **τ²-bench at 97.7% is the second-highest figure in this research pass** and clears the Tau-family reference by nearly 48 points, independently measured by Artificial Analysis. That one result genuinely substantiates the "elite support-agent tool use" positioning for customer-support-style workloads where the tool schema is fixed and the horizon is a few turns. On **autonomous, multi-step, open-horizon agency it is close to the worst in this pass**: **the AA Agentic Index of 17.2% is the lowest recorded here**, **GDPval-AA at 1,018 Elo is also the lowest, 732 points below the ~1750+ reference**, **Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 41.9% is 46.3 points below the anchor**, and **APEX-Agents-AA (17.0%) and ResearchClawBench (12.4%) both collapse**. Add that **Claw-Eval, OSWorld, MCP-Atlas, BrowseComp, CyberGym, Toolathlon and Terminal-Bench 3.0 are all unreported**, with the methodology explicitly penalising the Claw-Eval absence. The score is the honest average of one elite capability and six poor-to-absent ones.
- **Reasoning: 80/100.** Genuinely strong where it counts for enterprise use, with a specific and unusual distinction. **The AA-Omniscience Index of +18.0% on a 25.0% hallucination rate is the best reliability profile found anywhere in this entire research pass** — better than GLM-5.3's +14.3% / 29.6%, and far better than the 68–94% hallucination rates that characterise most frontier models assessed here. For enterprise deployment that is arguably the most valuable single property a model can have, because it is the one failure mode downstream tooling cannot fix. **AA-IFBench at 81.3% is also the highest instruction-following figure in this pass**, which compounds the same virtue: it does what it is told and declines what it does not know. **GPQA Diamond clears the 90%+ reference under three measurements within 1.3 points (90.1% / 90.1% / 91.4%)**, validating the science-reasoning claim, and **MMLU-Pro reaches 85.8%**. Five deductions keep it out of the high 80s: **AA-HLE at 37.2% and HLE at 35% both miss the 40%+ reference**; **the Intelligence Index of 37.6% is 22.4 points below the 60+ marker**; **CritPt at 8.0% is near-floor** on frontier physics; **AA-LCR at 64.3% is weak**; **raw Omniscience accuracy is only 34.6%**, so the excellent calibration sits on a narrow knowledge base; and **neither ARC-AGI nor FrontierMath has any figure**, leaving abstract reasoning and research mathematics unmeasured.
- **Context window: 88/100. Set deliberately below the ≥1M tier floor of 95, with two independent justifications.** First, **measured depth does not support the nominal size**: **AA-LCR at 64.3% is materially weaker than every other 1M-class model assessed in this pass** — GLM-5.3 79.7%, DeepSeek V4 Pro 80.3%, GPT-5.4 82.0%, GLM-5.3-Flash 80.0% — and **no MRCR, RULER, CorpusQA or GraphWalks figure exists** to demonstrate retrieval at depth. Second, and unusually explicit, **xAI's own price schedule discourages using the window**: the rate **doubles from $1.25/$2.50 to $2.50/$5.00 at a ≥200K prompt**, so the vendor has priced the advertised million as a premium product rather than a default. The score lands in the 500K–1M band as a fair reflection of depth that is both measured-weak and economically penalised, rather than crediting a headline the evidence does not carry.
- **Multimodal: 68/100.** Input is **text and image** with text-only output and no audio, video or PDF path, fixing it in the methodology's "+image in = 60–70" band. It sits in the **upper part** of that band on a credible, doubly-sourced measurement: **MMMU-Pro at 78.1%, reported identically by two routes**, which is comparable to GPT-5.6 Luna's 78.4% and ahead of Claude Sonnet 4.6's 70.6%. It cannot reach the band ceiling because the measured breadth is a single benchmark — **no CharXiv, document, OCR, video or audio figure exists**, so chart reasoning and document extraction are entirely unmeasured — and **Design Arena Website at 1,202 Elo is explicitly excluded** as an output-quality rather than input-modality measure.
- **Coding: 62/100.** The weakest dimension relative to the model's positioning, and the evidence is consistent rather than patchy. **LiveCodeBench at 84.5% from Vals AI is genuinely good** and shows real competitive-style coding ability. Everything else falls short. **The AA Coding Index at 42.3% is 27.7 points below the methodology's 70%+ reference** and among the lowest in this research pass. **SWE-bench at 71.4% is modest** — 25 points behind DeepSeek V4 Pro on the identical Vals harness — so repository-scale patching is mid-field at best. **Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 41.9% is 43.1 points below the 85%+ coding reference**, which rules out terminal-driven agentic coding. **AA-SciCode at 48.3% misses its reference by about 7 points.** And **SWE-bench Verified, SWE-bench Pro, DeepSWE, Codeforces, CursorBench and Vibe Code Bench are all unreported**, so six of the dimension's reference benchmarks cannot be checked. The pattern — strong on self-contained problems, weak on repository and terminal work — mirrors the tool-use split exactly.
- **Cost efficiency: 66/100.** The sub-200K rate card is genuinely aggressive and well-matched to this model's actual strengths. **$1.25 / $2.50 puts output at six times cheaper than the methodology's $3/$15 anchor on the output leg**, which is the leg that dominates spend for a reasoning model emitting long chains, and it is the right price for the workloads it is actually good at — support-agent tool calling, instruction-following and science QA inside 200K. Three deductions. **The rate doubles to $2.50 / $5.00 at ≥200K**, so the headline 1M window is a premium product and the economically sane operating range is a fifth of it. **It is superseded within its own family**: Grok 4.5 (63.89 composite) and Grok 4.6 (67.89) both score well above this model's 53.61, and Grok 4.6 additionally posts the highest SWE-bench figure in the xAI line at 95.6%. And **the capability purchased is narrow** — an Agentic Index of 17.2%, a Coding Index of 42.3% and GDPval at 1,018 Elo mean this rate card does not buy a general-purpose workhorse, so the saving is only real if your workload matches the narrow band it excels at.
- **Overall Score: 71.2/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dimensions (58 + 80 + 88 + 68 + 62) / 5 = 71.2 — **and this is a case where the mean is the wrong summary statistic, because the model is excellent at a few things and poor at many.** Its curated description, "elite support-agent tool use and science reasoning", is **validated precisely and should be read literally.** Four credentials are real and independently measured: **τ²-bench at 97.7%, second-highest in this research pass**; **AA-IFBench at 81.3%, the highest instruction-following figure found anywhere in this pass**; **GPQA Diamond clearing 90% under three measurements within 1.3 points**; and above all **an AA-Omniscience Index of +18.0% on a 25.0% hallucination rate — the best reliability profile found anywhere in this entire research pass.** That last point deserves weight: in a field where comparable models hallucinate at 68% to 94%, a model that declines to answer rather than inventing is uniquely suited to regulated and customer-facing deployment, and no amount of downstream tooling substitutes for it. Note also that **every figure in this report is independent or third-party** — no xAI results table was reached — so none of it carries vendor-inflation risk. Choose it for **customer-support agents, structured tool calling, instruction-bound workflows and graduate-level science QA inside a 200K prompt.** Four findings rule it out elsewhere. **Autonomous agency is near-worst in this pass** — Agentic Index 17.2%, GDPval 1,018 Elo, Terminal-Bench 41.9%, APEX-Agents 17.0%, ResearchClawBench 12.4%. **Coding is mid-to-weak** — AA Coding Index 42.3%, SWE-bench 71.4%, with six reference benchmarks unreported. **The 1M window is both measured-weak (AA-LCR 64.3%) and priced at double beyond 200K.** And **knowledge breadth is thin** — 34.6% raw accuracy, a 37.6% Intelligence Index, HLE missing its reference twice over, CritPt at 8.0%, and no ARC-AGI or FrontierMath measurement at all.
