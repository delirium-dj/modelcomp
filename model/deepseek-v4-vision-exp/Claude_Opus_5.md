## Model card

- **Name:** DeepSeek V4 Vision Exp. **Critical variant/alias finding, confirmed by DeepSeek's own primary documentation, which governs this entire report: this model has been retired.** DeepSeek's Models & Pricing page states explicitly that **"the legacy names `deepseek-v4-flash` and `deepseek-v4-flash-vision-exp` are still accepted, but the corresponding models have been retired, their requests are served by the DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash model and billed at the Flash price."** So the ID still resolves, but **what answers is DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash** — and that is a materially *better* model than the experimental vision checkpoint this ID originally named. The quality dimensions below are therefore assessed on **DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash**, because that is what actually serves requests, and they carry the same values as my DeepSeek V4.1 Flash findings file in this research pass **except for Context window and Multimodal**, which differ for this tier.
- **Short description:** Originally DeepSeek's "experimental native multimodal vision-language MoE model designed for multi-modal code understanding, UI layout reasoning, and image-to-code generation" (this repository's curated metadata). In practice, calling it today gets you a model placed **#23 of 882 at 67.81/100** by a composite tracker — DeepSeek's strongest Flash-tier release. Its standout results are **Codeforces at 3,471 Elo**, the highest competitive-programming figure found anywhere in this research pass; **Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 90.6%, above the methodology's ~88% anchor**; **AA-LCR at 84.0%, the highest long-context reasoning figure in this entire pass**; **DeepSWE at 74.2%, clearing the 74%+ reference**; and **AA AutomationBench at 68.9%, also pass-leading**. The counterweight is severe: **a 96.5% AA-Omniscience hallucination rate, the worst found anywhere in this research pass.**
- **Provider / access:** **OpenCode Zen free tier** ("available for multimodal experimentation and vision benchmarking", per curated metadata). Underlying weights public at `deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash` on Hugging Face, with a separate technical report PDF. DeepSeek's own API serves it as `deepseek-flash` on **both an OpenAI-format and an Anthropic-format base URL**, with **JSON output, tool calls, Responses API, Anthropic API and chat prefix completion** all supported, FIM completion in non-thinking mode only, and **Vision: ✓** — the Flash tier is the vision-capable DeepSeek model, where V4-Pro explicitly is not.
- **Release / knowledge:** The originally-named checkpoint is retired; the serving model is DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash. **No verified release date or knowledge cutoff found for either.**
- **IDs:** `opencode/deepseek-v4-vision-exp`; DeepSeek API legacy name `deepseek-v4-flash-vision-exp`, which **still resolves but routes to `deepseek-flash` (DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash)**. **This is itself a free-tier ID on OpenCode Zen.**
- **Context window:** **200,000 tokens on this tier** (curated metadata), against **DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash's native 1,000,000 with 384K maximum output** confirmed on DeepSeek's own pricing page — **a fivefold reduction.** The truncation is the main cost of using this ID rather than `deepseek-flash` directly, and it is a pure product limit rather than a capability one: **AA-LCR at 84.0% is the highest long-context reasoning figure recorded in this entire research pass**, so the model reasons at depth better than anything else assessed here, and this tier simply does not let you use that.
- **Modalities:** **Text, image and PDF input → text output** (curated metadata). DeepSeek's own documentation confirms **Vision: ✓** for the Flash tier. Measured visual capability is reasonable — **BabyVision with Python at 89.6%, Chartography with tools at 78.9%, AA-MMMU-Pro at 77.0%** — but **GDP.pdf at 12.8% is near-floor**, which directly undercuts the PDF claim for document-grounded agentic work. No video or audio path.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-03):** **$0 on the OpenCode Zen free tier.** Underlying DeepSeek Flash rates, from DeepSeek's own Models & Pricing page, with **off-peak at exactly half of peak** (peak = 01:00–04:00 and 06:00–10:00 UTC, Mon–Fri, excluding Chinese public holidays — roughly 35 of 168 hours, so off-peak is the normal case):
  - **Input, cache miss: $0.15 off-peak / $0.30 peak**
  - **Output: $0.60 off-peak / $1.20 peak**
  - **Input, cache hit: $0.003 off-peak / $0.006 peak** — **roughly one-fiftieth of cache-miss, the cheapest cache tier found anywhere in this research pass.**
  - **Concurrency limit: 2,500** — five times DeepSeek V4-Pro's 500.
- **Architecture:** **Open weights.** Sparse MoE; the sibling `deepseek-v4-flash` ID is documented at **284B total / 13B active under MIT**, and the V4.1-Flash parameter count is not separately stated in the sources read.

### Normalized scores (1–100)

> **Tool use, Reasoning and Coding are identical to my DeepSeek V4.1 Flash findings in this research pass, because that model is what serves this ID. Context window and Multimodal differ for this tier, and Cost efficiency differs because this tier is free.**

- **Tool use: 86/100.** Genuinely category-leading in three places. **AA AutomationBench at 68.9% is the highest recorded in this research pass**, **Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 90.6% is above the ~88% anchor and the highest TB2.1 figure here**, and **Terminal-Bench 3.0 at 30% edges GLM-5.3's 28.3%** — so performance holds up better than most across benchmark generations. **CyberGym at 88.1%** is second only to MiMo-V2.6-Flash in this pass, and **GDPval-AA at 1,600 Elo** beats DeepSeek's own V4-Pro, GPT-5.4, Muse Spark 1.1 and GLM-5.2. Four deductions: **the Terminal-Bench 2.1 vendor-to-independent gap is 16.1 points** (90.6% against 74.5% at Vals), so the headline cannot stand unqualified; **GDPval remains 150 Elo below the ~1750+ reference**; **ExploitGym at 15.3%, Agents' Last Exam at 31.8% and GDP.pdf at 12.8% all fall away** on exploitation, long-horizon and document-grounded work; and **Claw-Eval, OSWorld, MCP-Atlas, τ²-bench and Toolathlon are all unreported**, with the methodology explicitly penalising the Claw-Eval absence.
- **Reasoning: 74/100.** Three results are strong and one failure is disqualifying for a whole class of use. **AA-LCR at 84.0% is the highest long-context reasoning figure recorded in this entire research pass**, **GPQA Diamond at 90.9% clears the 90%+ reference**, and **Apex at 65.6% is remarkable** — DeepSeek's own much larger V4-Pro scores 38.3% on the same benchmark. Against that: **the AA-Omniscience hallucination rate of 96.5% is the worst found anywhere in this research pass**, and because accuracy is a respectable 46.4% this is a pure calibration failure rather than ignorance — it knows a great deal and asserts regardless, which makes it unusable as a source of truth without retrieval and verification. **Both HLE figures miss the 40%+ reference** (36.8% vendor, 39.2% independent). **The Intelligence Index of 39.5% is 20.5 points below the 60+ marker.** **MLCR-AA at 22.8% sits 61.2 points below AA-LCR** — the widest long-context harness divergence in this pass, meaning the pass-leading AA-LCR result does not generalise. And **ARC-AGI, FrontierMath and MMLU-Pro are entirely unmeasured.** Note also that the GPQA figure is vendor-only, with no independent corroboration.
- **Context window: 76/100. This is the principal cost of using this ID rather than `deepseek-flash` directly, and the score reflects the tier rather than the model.** **200,000 tokens** places it in the methodology's 200K–500K band (65–84), a **fivefold reduction from DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash's native 1,000,000 with 384K maximum output**, both confirmed on DeepSeek's own pricing page. It is set well above the 200K anchor of 70 for a specific and strong reason: **AA-LCR at 84.0% is the highest long-context reasoning figure in this entire research pass**, so within the window it does expose, this model reasons at depth better than anything else assessed here — the truncation is a pure product limit, not a capability one. Three reasons it is not higher: **MLCR-AA at 22.8% is 61.2 points below AA-LCR**, so depth performance is severely harness-dependent; **no MRCR, RULER, CorpusQA or GraphWalks figure exists**; and **the 200K ceiling forfeits the single best long-context capability in this pass**, which is a real waste rather than a neutral trade.
- **Multimodal: 76/100.** **PDF input, per this repository's curated metadata for this specific ID, places it in the methodology's "+video/PDF in = 75–90" band**, and DeepSeek's own documentation independently confirms **Vision: ✓** for the Flash tier — notable because the same page explicitly denies vision for DeepSeek V4-Pro, so the Flash line is the vision-capable DeepSeek. Measured capability within the band is reasonable: **BabyVision with Python at 89.6%** is strong, **Chartography with tools at 78.9%** is solid, and **AA-MMMU-Pro at 77.0%** is mid-field. It is placed **at the band floor rather than higher**, and the reason is specific and evidential rather than conservative: **GDP.pdf at 12.8% is near-floor on document-grounded agentic work**, which directly undercuts the PDF-input claim that justifies the band in the first place — the model can accept documents but demonstrably struggles to act on them. Add **ZeroBench with Python at 49.0%** showing hard visual reasoning remains limited, **no CharXiv or OCR measurement at all**, and **no video or audio path**, and the band floor is the honest placement. **This is also the dimension where the original vision-exp checkpoint's purpose — UI layout reasoning and image-to-code — is least verifiable, since that checkpoint no longer runs and was never benchmarked.**
- **Coding: 88/100.** The best value-for-capability coding profile in this research pass. **Codeforces at 3,471 Elo is the highest competitive-programming figure found anywhere here**, above DeepSeek's own V4-Pro at 3,206. **DeepSWE at 74.2% clears the methodology's 74%+ frontier reference** — one of very few models in this pass to manage it, and notably ahead of GLM-5.3 (66.9%), GPT-5.6 Luna (67.2%) and MiMo-V2.6-Flash (67.9%). **Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 90.6% clears the 85%+ coding reference** comfortably, and **NL2Repo at 65.4%** is the strongest NL2Repo figure in this pass. Four deductions: **the Terminal-Bench figure carries the same 16.1-point Vals discount**; **AA-SciCode at 51.9% misses the 55%+ reference by 3.1 points**; **ProgramBench at 20.3%** indicates weak novel synthesis; and **no SWE-bench of any variant, no LiveCodeBench and no AA Coding Index exist**, so the most-cited repository-engineering measures cannot be checked and the dimension rests on competitive programming, terminal work and DeepSWE.
- **Cost efficiency: 96/100. Among the highest I have assigned in this research pass, and the structure behind it is unusually robust.** This tier is **$0 on OpenCode Zen**. If the promotion ends, the fallback is not a cliff but one of the cheapest frontier rate cards in existence: **$0.15 input / $0.60 output off-peak** (one-twentieth and one-twenty-fifth of the methodology's $3/$15 anchor), with **off-peak being the normal case** — peak covers only about 35 of 168 hours weekly — and **cache hits at $0.003 per 1M input, roughly one-fiftieth of cache-miss and the cheapest cache tier found anywhere in this pass**. Add **a 2,500 concurrency limit, five times DeepSeek V4-Pro's 500**, **384K maximum output** on the untruncated ID, **open weights** so the rate card is escapable entirely, **both OpenAI- and Anthropic-format endpoints** for near-zero migration cost, and the fact that what you get is **Codeforces 3,471**, **DeepSWE above reference**, **Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 90.6%**, **the pass-leading AA-LCR** and **the pass-leading AA AutomationBench**. Three deductions: **this is a retired ID**, so the routing is a vendor convenience that could change without notice — call `deepseek-flash` directly for stability; **the 200K ceiling forfeits 80% of the window** you would get from the canonical ID at the same price; and **the 96.5% hallucination rate imposes a verification cost** no rate card shows.
- **Overall Score: 80.0/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dimensions (86 + 74 + 76 + 76 + 88) / 5 = 80.0 — and **the most useful thing in this report is the identification, not the number.** This ID names a model DeepSeek has retired; what answers today is **DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash**, a materially better model placed #23 of 882. That is good news, with a catch: **you get the better model but at a 200K context ceiling instead of its native 1M with 384K output.** What holds up: **Codeforces at 3,471 Elo — the highest competitive-programming figure anywhere in this pass**; **DeepSWE at 74.2%, clearing the frontier reference where most peers fail**; **Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 90.6% above the agentic anchor and 3.0 at 30% above GLM-5.3**; **AA AutomationBench at 68.9% and AA-LCR at 84.0%, both pass-leading**; **CyberGym at 88.1%**; **GPQA Diamond at 90.9%**; and **Apex at 65.6%**, where DeepSeek's far larger V4-Pro manages 38.3%. All of it at **$0**, with an exceptionally cheap paid fallback. Choose it for **free high-volume agentic coding, competitive programming, terminal automation and security discovery inside 200K tokens.** Four cautions. **It hallucinates at 96.5% — the worst rate found anywhere in this research pass** — and with 46.4% accuracy that is a calibration failure, not ignorance, so never use it as a source of truth. **Call `deepseek-flash` directly instead of this legacy alias** if you want stability and the full 1M window; the retired-ID routing is a courtesy, not a contract. **The PDF-input claim is weakly supported** — GDP.pdf at 12.8% is near-floor, and the original vision-exp checkpoint's UI-layout and image-to-code purpose was never benchmarked and no longer runs. And **reasoning has hard limits** — both HLE figures miss 40%, the Intelligence Index is 39.5%, MLCR-AA sits 61.2 points below AA-LCR, and ARC-AGI, FrontierMath and MMLU-Pro are entirely unmeasured.
