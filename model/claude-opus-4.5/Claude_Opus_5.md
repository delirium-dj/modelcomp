### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 78/100.** Two results genuinely clear their references: **τ³-bench at 70.2% sits roughly 20 points above the methodology's ~50%+ Tau3 frontier reference** — a benchmark almost nothing in this dataset reports — and **τ²-bench at 86.3%** clears the Tau-family reference too, with a **published Claw-Eval of 59.6%** satisfying a requirement the methodology explicitly penalises models for missing. WideResearch (76.4%) and MCP-Tasks (71.8%) corroborate on structured tool use. Four deductions: **Terminal-Bench stops at 2.0 (59.3%)** with no 2.1+ figure, so the ~88% anchor cannot be tested and the available number sits mid-band; **GDPval-AA is unreported entirely**; **MCP Atlas (42.3%), Toolathlon (43.5%), DeepPlanning (26.4%) and VITA-Bench (23.3%) are all weak**, so autonomous multi-step work is poor; and **the majority of its agentic evidence comes from a competitor's comparison table**, where Anthropic's own harness was not used.
- **Reasoning: 68/100.** Competition mathematics is excellent — **AIME 2026 95.1%, HMMT Nov 2025 93.3%, MMLU-Redux 96.6%** — and MMLU-Pro is closely corroborated at 89.5%/88.9%. Everything else is below reference or actively poor. **GPQA Diamond misses the 90%+ reference under both measurements (87% Anthropic, 81.0% independent)**, with a 6-point vendor-independent gap. **HLE spans 30.8% to 13.2% — a 17.6-point gap, with both well below the 40%+ reference** and the independent figure among the lowest in this dataset. **The Intelligence Index of 23.7% is mid-band**, 36 points off the frontier marker. **CritPt is effectively zero at 0.3%**, **Epoch AI's independent FrontierMath v2 Tier 4 is 4.2%**, instruction following collapses from IFEval 90.9% to AA-IFBench 43.0%, and the **AA-Omniscience Index is negative at −4.1% on a 76.2% hallucination rate.**
- **Context window: 70/100.** **200,000 tokens sits exactly at the methodology's 200K anchor (= 70)** within the 200K–500K tier, and the long-context evidence gives no reason to move off that anchor in either direction: **AI-Needle 74%, LongBench v2 64.4% and AA-LCR 70.7%** are all mediocre, with **no MRCR, RULER or GraphWalks figure** and max output tokens undocumented. For context within its own family, every later Claude Opus model in this dataset offers 1M — five times this window — so this is a different class rather than merely a smaller one.
- **Multimodal: 66/100.** Input is **text and images only** with text-only output, placing it in the methodology's "+image in = 60–70" band, and it sits mid-band rather than at the ceiling. **AA-MMMU-Pro at 71.2% is closely corroborated by the competitor table's 70.6%** and MathVision reaches 74.3%, which is respectable. Three deductions: **ScreenSpot Pro at 45.7% is weak** screen understanding — roughly half what Claude Opus 4.6 (83.1%) and Claude Opus 4.8 (87.9%) achieve, so this capability improved sharply after this generation; **CharXiv at 68.5% and V\* at 67.0% are mid-field**; and **no document-understanding, OCR or audio benchmark exists**. A VideoMMMU figure of 84.4% appears in a competitor's table but was **not credited**, since Anthropic documents image input only and crediting it would misstate the input surface.
- **Coding: 80/100.** **SWE-bench Verified at 80.9% is genuinely strong and was the figure that led its generation** — ahead of Gemini 3 Pro (76.2%) and GPT-5.1 (76.3%) — with **LiveCodeBench v6 at 84.8%** and **SWE Multilingual at 77.5%** backing it up. Four deductions: **Terminal-Bench 2.0 at 59.3% is far below the methodology's 85%+ coding reference** and no newer version was measured; **SWE-bench Pro at 57.1% is mid-field** and NL2Repo only 43.2%; **DeepSWE, SciCode, the AA Coding Index and Vibe Code Bench are all absent**, so four of the band's references cannot be checked; and the strongest figures other than SWE-bench Verified come from a competitor's harness.
- **Cost efficiency: 30/100.** At the **$5 / $25 Anthropic Opus-tier standard** it sits meaningfully worse than the $3/$15 anchor that maps to ~60, with only generic mitigations (10% cache reads, Batch at 50%, 300K Batch-output eligibility). What drives it well below the anchor is that it is dominated on every axis by cheaper models from the same vendor: **Claude Opus 5.5 costs $4/$20 — 20% less — and scores 87.78 against this model's 55.66, a 32-point composite gap**; **Claude Sonnet 5.5 costs $2/$10 — 60% less — and scores 83.42**, a 28-point gap at two-fifths the price. Even within its own generation an independent comparison placed **Claude Sonnet 4.5 at $3/$15** as the better coding value. Five Opus generations have shipped since. There is no workload for which this price is defensible.
- **Overall Score: 72.4/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dimensions (78 + 68 + 70 + 66 + 80) / 5 = 72.4 — a deep-legacy model whose one durable credential is a coding result that led its own generation. **SWE-bench Verified at 80.9%** was genuinely best-in-cohort when published, and **τ³-bench at 70.2% remains one of only three such figures in this entire dataset** and clears the frontier reference by ~20 points — if your evaluation specifically depends on τ³-style banking tool use, that is a real datapoint. Add LiveCodeBench v6 84.8%, AIME 2026 95.1% and MMLU-Redux 96.6%. Everything else argues against use. **The reasoning evidence is poor and internally inconsistent**: GPQA misses 90% on both measurements with a 6-point vendor gap, HLE spans 30.8% to 13.2%, CritPt is 0.3%, the Intelligence Index is mid-band, and the **AA-Omniscience Index is negative on a 76.2% hallucination rate** — so this model cannot be trusted on facts. **The context window is 200K**, a fifth of every later Claude Opus. **Screen understanding is half** what the next generation achieved. And the commercial position is indefensible: **Claude Opus 5.5 is 20% cheaper and 32 composite points better; Claude Sonnet 5.5 is 60% cheaper and 28 points better.** Use it only where an acceptance suite is already pinned to this exact model; route everything new to Opus 5.5 or Sonnet 5.5.
