## Model card

- **Name:** Laguna XS 2.1
- **Short description:** Poolside's **33B-total / 3B-active MoE "designed for agentic coding and long-horizon work on a local machine"** ([Poolside model card](https://huggingface.co/poolside/Laguna-XS-2.1)). The headline achievement is size-for-capability: **70.9% SWE-bench Verified and 47.6% SWE-bench Pro from a model compact enough to run on a Mac with 36 GB of RAM** — and on Poolside's own comparison table it beats the 120B gpt-oss on SWE-bench Pro by **31.4 points**. It is an upgrade of Laguna XS.2 with "a **+5.4% jump on SWE-bench Multilingual** as well as stronger performance on terminal-style tasks". Distinct model; `laguna-s-2.1` has its own folder, and Laguna M.1 and XS.2 are separate models.
- **Provider / access:** **Open weights on Hugging Face under OpenMDW-1.1** — Poolside's framing is unusually direct: "a **fully permissive license. Use it, modify it, and build commercial products on it. No permission required.**" Launch-day support in **vLLM (≥0.21.0), SGLang, Transformers (≥5.7.0), llama.cpp and TRT-LLM** (the last "thanks to the support of the team at NVIDIA"). **Official FP8, NVFP4 and INT4 quantized variants**, official **GGUF** conversions (BF16 and Q4_K_M), an **Ollama** library entry (`ollama run laguna-xs-2.1`), **Atomic Chat** desktop support, MLX support, **OpenRouter**, and **OpenCode Zen**. Poolside also ships `pool`, "a lightweight terminal-based coding agent and a dual Agent Client Protocol client-server" with automatic Zed and JetBrains configuration. Hugging Face itself reports no Inference Provider deploys it (2 requests), but OpenRouter does. **349,701 downloads in the last month**, 256 likes, **13 finetunes and 41 quantizations**.
- **Release / knowledge:** **2026-07-02** (the Hugging Face collection is dated Jul 2). Knowledge cutoff: no verified public date found. Poolside's technical report covers "data automixing and **async off-policy agent RL**".
- **IDs:** `poolside/Laguna-XS-2.1` (Hugging Face), `poolside/laguna-xs-2.1` (OpenRouter), `laguna-xs-2.1` (Ollama, Zen). Plus `poolside/Laguna-XS-2.1-DFlash` and `-DFlash-FP8` (draft models) and `poolside/Laguna-XS-2.1-GGUF`. **Free inference was offered on OpenRouter "for a limited time"** for this model and Laguna M.1; self-hosting is free under OpenMDW-1.1.
- **Context window:** **262,144 tokens.** Worth noting precisely: Poolside **benchmarked at 256K**, not at the full 262K — "a context length of 256K tokens" is stated in the methodology. Max output: no verified public figure found.
- **Modalities:** **"Modality: text-to-text"** — explicitly text in, text out. No image, audio or video. Reasoning: **yes, native, with interleaved thinking between tool calls and a per-request enable/disable toggle.** One operational detail matters a great deal and is easy to get wrong: the model "is designed to work best with **preserved thinking**, where reasoning content from prior assistant messages is preserved in the message history… **Reasoning may not be generated in follow-up steps if prior thinking blocks are dropped.**" Tool calls: yes, with a dedicated `poolside_v1` parser.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-08):** approximately **$0.06 / MTok input and $0.12 / MTok output** on OpenRouter, with a **time-limited free tier** at launch. Self-hosting is free, and at **33B total / 3B active it fits on a 36 GB Mac**.
- **Architecture:** Documented in more detail than most open-weight releases, and the detail is genuinely interesting:
  - **33B total / 3B activated per token**, **256 experts plus 1 shared expert**, **40 layers**, trained with the **Muon optimizer**
  - **Mixed sliding-window and global attention in a 3:1 ratio** — "10 layers with global attention, 30 layers with sliding window attention" — using "**sigmoid gating with per-layer rotary scales**", with a **512-token sliding window**
  - **KV cache quantized to FP8**, "reducing memory per token" — which together with the 3B activation is what makes 262K context on consumer hardware plausible
  - Optional **DFlash speculative decoding**: "a 5-layer Llama-style draft model that proposes **up to 7 tokens per step at ~70% per-position acceptance on coding tasks**"
  - Known issue Poolside discloses: on Apple Silicon, `ollama run` "may currently return empty output… (a Metal f16 overflow in the MoE down-projection; fix pending)"

### Raw benchmarks found

> **Methodology first, because Poolside's is strong and it changes how much weight the numbers deserve.** All benchmarking used "**Laude Institute's Harbor Framework** with our agent harness, with a **maximum of 500 steps** and sandboxed execution", with identical sampling across every run — "**temperature=1.0, top_k=20 and top_p=1, with thinking mode enabled and a context length of 256K tokens**". Per-task resources were 8 GB RAM / 2 CPUs, except Terminal-Bench 2.0 at **48 GB RAM / 32 CPUs**. Attempt counts are published per benchmark: **SWE-bench Verified and Multilingual mean pass@1 over 4 attempts, SWE-bench Pro over 2, Terminal-Bench 2.0 over 5**. Poolside discloses that "some base task images and verifiers were **patched to fix infrastructure reliability issues**", and — importantly — "we also ran a **reward-hack judge post-hoc** on Laguna XS 2.1 evaluation runs and **did not find significant reward hacking** after joint judge review and manual review." Three of the four results also carry **leaderboard-linked Hugging Face evaluation entries** (SWE-bench Verified 70.9, SWE-bench Pro 47.6, Terminal-Bench 2.0 37.5), which is better provenance than a prose claim.
>
> Poolside also handicaps itself in the comparison: "We used the **highest publicly-referenced scores for all comparison models** across each benchmark." [BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/models/laguna-xs-2-1) mirrors the four figures and assigns **no overall score** (5 of 626 benchmarks, unranked). I found no independent third-party measurement.

Poolside's own comparison table, reproduced in full because the size column is the point:

The comparisons that matter: against **gpt-oss-120B**, a model roughly 3.6× its size, Laguna XS 2.1 wins SWE-bench Pro by **31.4 points** and Terminal-Bench 2.0 by **18.8**. Against **Claude Haiku 4.5** it wins SWE-bench Pro by 8.1 and Terminal-Bench 2.0 by 7.7. Against **Qwen3.6-35B-A3B at essentially identical size, it loses all four** — by 2.5, 4.1, 1.9 and a substantial 14.0 points on Terminal-Bench. The generational gain over XS.2 is real but modest outside SWE-Multilingual (+1.0, **+5.4**, +1.3, +1.8).

Two further figures published by Poolside in its **Laguna S 2.1 announcement leaderboards**, where XS 2.1 appears as a compared row and the figures are attributed specifically to it:

- **Terminal-Bench 2.1: 33.4%** — consistent with 37.5% on the easier TB 2.0
- **DeepSWE v1.1: 0.3%** — effectively a zero. DeepSWE is the one benchmark in Poolside's set that still spreads models out, and this model cannot do it at all.

**Absent entirely:** no GPQA Diamond, MMLU-Pro, AIME, HLE, ARC-AGI, CritPt, Omniscience or hallucination figure; no LiveCodeBench or SciCode; no Toolathlon, BFCL, GDPval, MCP-Atlas or BrowseComp; and **no MRCR, RULER, AA-LCR or needle-retrieval measurement at any depth** despite a 262K window.

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 62/100.** **Terminal-Bench 2.0 at 37.5%, averaged over five attempts with 48 GB / 32 CPU sandboxes and a 500-step cap**, is a credible agentic result — and beating **gpt-oss-120B (18.7%) and Claude Haiku 4.5 (29.8%)** from a 33B model is a real achievement. **Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 33.4%** corroborates. The engineering supports it: native interleaved thinking between tool calls, a dedicated tool-call parser, and `pool` as a first-party agent harness. Two things cap it firmly: it **loses Terminal-Bench 2.0 to the same-size Qwen3.6-35B-A3B by 14 points**, and there is **no Toolathlon, BFCL, GDPval, MCP-Atlas or BrowseComp figure at all**, so breadth is unmeasured.
- **Reasoning: 48/100.** Native reasoning is real and carefully engineered — interleaved thinking, per-request toggling, and the documented preserved-thinking requirement — but **there is not a single reasoning benchmark**: no GPQA, MMLU-Pro, AIME, HLE, ARC-AGI or CritPt. The one long-horizon reasoning datum that exists is **DeepSWE v1.1 at 0.3%**, which is a floor result on precisely the kind of task the model is marketed for. Scored below the midpoint because the only available evidence is negative and the rest is absent. Worth stating plainly: this is a 3B-active model, and expecting general reasoning depth from it would be a category error.
- **Context window: 70/100.** **262,144 tokens from a 33B model that runs on a 36 GB Mac** is the most impressive spec here, and unlike most 262K claims the *mechanism* is documented: **a 3:1 mix of sliding-window and global attention across 40 layers (30 SWA at a 512-token window, 10 global), sigmoid gating with per-layer rotary scales, and an FP8-quantized KV cache**. That is a coherent engineering answer to why long context is affordable at this size, and Poolside honestly notes it **benchmarked at 256K rather than 262K**. Held below the high 70s because there is **no retrieval measurement whatsoever** — no MRCR, RULER, AA-LCR or needle test — so usable recall at depth is entirely unverified, and no max-output figure is published.
- **Multimodal: 15/100.** "Modality: text-to-text", stated outright. No image, audio or video input and none claimed. Template floor.
- **Coding: 76/100.** The model's purpose, and the results are strong once size is held in view. **SWE-bench Verified 70.9% and SWE-bench Pro 47.6%, both leaderboard-linked**, from **33B total / 3B active**, is the standout — the SWE-bench Pro figure in particular beats a 120B model by 31.4 points and Claude Haiku 4.5 by 8.1. **SWE-bench Multilingual 63.1%** with its **+5.4-point generational gain** is the cleanest evidence the upgrade did something. The methodology is rigorous: four-attempt averaging, fixed sampling parameters, a 500-step cap, patched task images disclosed, and a **post-hoc reward-hack judge that found nothing significant**. Capped below 80 because it **loses all four benchmarks to the same-size Qwen3.6-35B-A3B**, **DeepSWE v1.1 is 0.3%**, there is **no LiveCodeBench or SciCode figure**, and nothing has been independently reproduced.
- **Cost efficiency: 96/100.** Essentially best-in-class, and on more axes than price. **OpenMDW-1.1 is fully permissive — "no permission required"** for commercial use. **33B total with 3B active runs on a Mac with 36 GB of RAM**, with official **FP8, NVFP4, INT4 and GGUF** variants, a one-line Ollama install, MLX support, Atomic Chat, and launch-day vLLM/SGLang/Transformers/llama.cpp/TRT-LLM support. Hosted inference is **~$0.06 / $0.12 per MTok** with a time-limited **free OpenRouter tier**. The optional **DFlash speculator (7 tokens per step at ~70% acceptance)** cuts latency further. The ecosystem has validated it: **349,701 monthly downloads, 41 quantizations, 13 finetunes**. Getting 70.9% SWE-bench Verified on a laptop-class machine for free is close to the best capability-per-dollar result in this dataset. Docked only for the disclosed Apple Silicon Metal bug and the absence of a Hugging Face-native inference provider.
- **Overall Score: 54.2/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dims (62 + 48 + 70 + 15 + 76) / 5 = 54.2. The multimodal floor and the complete absence of reasoning benchmarks hold this well below what the coding numbers alone would suggest — read the dimensions, not the total. Best fit: **local and laptop-class agentic software engineering** — repository patching, multilingual SWE work, terminal-driven tasks, and long-context codebase navigation — on hardware you own, under a licence that imposes no conditions. Three practical notes that matter more than the score: **preserve thinking blocks in message history or the model stops reasoning mid-task**; **avoid DeepSWE-class long-horizon work (0.3%)**; and if you are choosing purely on benchmarks at this size, **Qwen3.6-35B-A3B beats it on all four of Poolside's own comparisons** — Laguna XS 2.1's advantages are the permissive licence, the 262K window, the FP8 KV cache, the quantization matrix and the first-party agent tooling.
