## Model card

- **Name:** Ring-2.6-1T
- **Short description:** InclusionAI's (Ant Group) **trillion-parameter flagship reasoning model**, released **2026-05-08** — the deep-reasoning sibling to Ling-2.6-1T (optimized instead for instant, token-efficient responses). Built by **architecturally migrating** the Ling-2.0 base rather than training from scratch, then large-scale post-training, with **~63B activated parameters per inference** (Ant's official developer documentation, a figure the first pass did not record). Its explicit target is production **long-horizon agentic execution** — agent workflows, engineering development, scientific analysis, complex business systems and enterprise automation. Open weights (MIT). **Ant Group has since shipped three cheaper and, on the independent composite, considerably stronger successors in the same family: Ling-3.0-flash (2026-08-04, 124B/5.1B), Ling-3.0-flash-VL (2026-09-10) and Ling-3.1-flash (2026-10-01, 572B/~25B, 1M context).** Ant's own Ling-3.0-flash card states the 124B/5.1B model "matches or outperforms its predecessor" across key benchmarks — the 1T flagship is superseded by the same lab's 1/8th-active model.
- **Provider / access:** Hugging Face `inclusionAI/Ring-2.6-1T` (1T params / 63B active, BF16 and FP8 safetensors, **1,319 downloads last month**; 5 Spaces using it); ModelScope (mainland China); InclusionAI API at `ling.tbox.cn`; online demo. Self-host via vLLM or SGLang (documented 4-node, tp-size 8 × pp-size 4 launch using Ant's `ling_2_5` SGLang branch; BF16 and FP8 both supported). **Third-party hosted: OpenRouter and ZenMux at $0.30/$2.50 — and at least one tracker lists the OpenRouter route as free.** No Hugging Face Inference Provider deployment. No OpenCode Zen ID found.
- **Release / knowledge:** Released **2026-05-08**; technical report **`arXiv:2606.15079v1`, "Ling and Ring 2.6 Technical Report: Efficient and Instant Agentic Intelligence at Trillion-Parameter Scale," June 2026**. Repo created 2026-05-14, 16 commits through 2026-06-17. **Model-card change since the first pass:** a **Training content summary** section was added **2026-09-28**, linking a public training-content disclosure document (explicitly stated not to replace the technical documentation, usage terms or licence). A SWE-bench Verified eval result was added 2026-05-18. **Knowledge cutoff: not disclosed.** *Flagged:* the card carries **no safety evaluation, red-teaming or risk assessment** — worth noting for a model shipping with a controllable long-horizon agent mode.
- **IDs:** `inclusionAI/Ring-2.6-1T` (HF), `Ring-2.6-1T` (SGLang/vLLM `model-path`), Ant developer portal "Ring-2.6-1T"
- **Context window:** **128K native, extensible to 256K via YaRN** — stated explicitly in the official HF download table. Artificial Analysis, llm.ing, Choosemodel and modeligent all list **262,144** (the YaRN-extended figure); modeligent's page header says 256K. **Still no measurement of retrieval at 262K beyond what this pass added** (see Long context below).
- **Modalities:** **Text in → text out only.** HF repo is a `Text Generation` / `AutoModelForCausalLM` checkpoint with no vision tower; Inference Providers listed under "Text Generation." **Reasoning is mandatory and not switchable off** — two effort levels only, `high` (production default) and `xhigh`. Tool calls / function calling: yes, plus `tool_choice` and structured response formats.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-17):** **Conflict, unresolved.** Ant's own pricing page lists **¥4.50 in / ¥18.00 out / ¥0.90 cache read per 1M** (≈$0.63/$12.5 — the CNY page is not directly comparable) and, for third parties, **OpenRouter and ZenMux both at $0.30 in / $2.50 out / $0.06 cache read**. This matches the first pass and matches Artificial Analysis's model page exactly. **However** BenchmarkList, OpenKey and themodelbeat all list **$0.075 in / $0.625 out**, and Choosemodel lists the model as **"Free on OR Custom."** The $0.075/$0.625 pair is exactly a **4× discount** on $0.30/$2.50, which is not any discount Ant's own page announces (its limited-time offers apply to Ling-3.x, not Ring). **The first-party $0.30/$2.50 figure is preferred** and the ~$0.075 figure is recorded as an unexplained promotional or stale-relay rate. Ant's own page also confirms Ling-3.x carries explicit limited-time discounts with **no published end date** ("优惠结束后将逐步恢复原价") — Ring-2.6-1T is *not* one of the discounted models. **MIT-licensed open weights** make self-hosting free of token cost. No Free tier from Ant.
- **Architecture:** **1T parameters**, `bailing_hybrid` architecture — a **hybrid linear attention design combining Lightning Attention with MLA (Multi-head Latent Attention)**, **~63B activated per inference**. Upgraded from the Ling-2.0 base via architectural-migration pre-training plus Evolutionary Chain-of-Thought, Linguistic Unit Policy Optimization, bidirectional preference alignment, and shortest-correct-response distillation. Agent capability trained with **KPop**, an RL framework for large-scale environment-grounded data with asynchronous scheduling across coding, search, tool use and workflow execution; the technical report clarifies that **KPop replaces IcePop's uniform fixed-ratio constraint with binary KL divergence**, combined with **asynchronous RL** decoupling rollout from parameter updates. Training agent used three tools (`execute_bash`, `search_replace`, `task_done`), max 200 turns in training and 500 in evaluation. **MIT License.**

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 72/100** (was 88). **The largest drop in this report, and it is evidence-driven.** The first pass scored 88 on a card figure — Tau2-Bench Telecom 95.32, PinchBench 87.60, ClawEval 63.82 — plus the observation that Terminal-Bench was "notably absent." **That absence has now been filled, and it fills badly: Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 43.1% (#91 of 194) and Terminal-Bench Hard at 28.8% (#79 of 326)**. For a model whose entire stated purpose is long-horizon agentic execution, those two rows are the ones that matter, and they are mid-pack and below-median respectively. **Tau3-Banking 17.9%** and **GDPval-AA 925 Elo (#126 of 352)** are consistent with that, and **AA-Briefcase 656 Elo with a 10.9% rubric pass rate** — the newest and most on-point independent benchmark, since AA-Briefcase measures exactly the multi-week professional knowledge work Ring claims to be built for — is the worst of the set. **AA Agentic Index 18.86** caps it outright. What keeps this at 72 rather than lower: the genuinely strong rows survive independent checking — **Tau2-Bench Telecom 92.4–95.32 (#42 of 332, 88th pct)**, **ClawEval 63.82**, **SWE-bench Verified 74%**, **GAIA-2 Search 77.90%**, **Terminal-Bench 2.1 43.1%** (median, not floor). The model is a strong telecom/vertical agent and a mediocre general one; Ant's card chose to publish the vertical.
- **Reasoning: 74/100** (was 86). **Lowered.** The confirmed reasoning rows are solid — **GPQA Diamond 88.27 (vendor) / 85.7% (independent, #77 of 468)**, **AIME 2026 95.83**, **AA-LCR 70.0%** — but this pass added the caps that were previously missing: **HLE 21.6% (#112 of 479)**, up from 18.3% but still mid-pack; **CritPt 3.7%**, effectively the floor on frontier physics/science reasoning; **AA-Omniscience −37.75**, so the abstention and hallucination behaviour is weak; and an **AA Intelligence Index of 17** on the current index (against 30.57 on the v4-era verified snapshot and 45+ in some stale mirrors). ARC-AGI-2 66.18 remains **vendor-only** — no independent run exists, and the first pass's confidence in it was unwarranted.
- **Context window: 70/100** (was 74). **Lowered, and for a specific reason.** This dimension previously rested on "no long-context retrieval benchmark has ever been published" — a penalty for *missing* evidence. That penalty is now resolved in the model's favour on the measurement (AA-LCR 70.0%, 69th pct; Context Arena GDM-MRCRv2 average 69.7–69.8%, and at `xhigh` **#5 of 11 where Ring is the field leader**) but resolved *against* it on the shape. **Context Arena's AUC at 1M tokens is 10.8% against 64.1–64.7% at 128K** — the model is strong across its native 128K window and effectively fails past it, and 256K/262K is a YaRN extrapolation of exactly the region where the measured curve collapses. 70 is a small *reduction* from 74 despite better evidence, because knowing that the extrapolated part of the window does not work is worse than not knowing.
- **Multimodal: 15/100.** **Text-only**, unchanged and re-confirmed this pass (HF `Text Generation` causal-LM checkpoint, no vision tower; OpenKey independently lists "Text-only in and out, no vision"; Choosemodel lists `text → text`). Floor score by methodology. InclusionAI's vision-capable siblings are Ling-3.0-flash-VL and Ming-flash-omni-2.0 — different models with their own folders. *Forward-looking note:* the Ling/Ring technical report itself names native multimodality as the next transition for the family, which confirms this gap is a known limitation on Ant's side rather than an oversight.
- **Coding: 70/100.** **Unchanged — the new evidence lands on both sides and nets out.** Better than the first pass knew: **SciCode 45.0%** (#77 of 296, 74th pct) up from 42.4%, and **Terminal-Bench 2.1 43.1%** / **Terminal-Bench Hard 28.8%** are now real terminal evidence rather than gaps. Worse than the first pass knew: **ALE-Bench 432.57, roughly half the field average**, and **AA Coding Index 42.83 — still well below Ring's own reasoning profile**, which was already the first pass's stated concern. SWE-bench Verified 74% remains independently confirmed. With one confirmed SWE-bench row, one confirmed SciCode row, two confirmed terminal rows and no SWE-bench Pro, DeepSWE, LiveCodeBench or Vibe Code Bench, 70 remains the right read.
- **Cost efficiency: 91/100.** **Unchanged, but the arithmetic underneath it was materially wrong and is now corrected.** The first pass cited **$4.14 per Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index task**; AA's current page says **$0.29**, and BenchmarkList's verified snapshot says $0.35 — a **~13× correction in the model's favour**, which by itself would argue upward. It is not raised because the price *itself* is unchanged and still unflattering: **$0.30 in / $2.50 out** is the most expensive of the five/six InclusionAI models by blended price (against Ling-3.0-flash-VL at $0.075/$0.22), AA ranks cost **#23 of 42** and calls the output rate "somewhat expensive," and AA's own intelligence task cost, while cheap in absolute terms, is being spread over a model that scores 17. **MIT-licensed weights** remove token cost entirely for anyone able to self-host a 1T model, and speed is genuinely good (**114.7 tok/s, #18 of 77**). The unexplained **$0.075/$0.625** rate that BenchmarkList, OpenKey and themodelbeat publish would, if real, put this well into the 95+ band — **that discrepancy is the single thing worth re-checking next pass**, and it is a 4× ratio consistent with an unannounced promotion.
- **Overall Score: 60/100.** `(72 + 74 + 70 + 15 + 70) / 5 = 301 / 5 = 60.2 → 60`. Best fit: **vertical/multi-turn agentic workflows where tool-execution stability matters more than breadth** — Tau2-Bench Telecom near the leader, a top-15 ClawEval, a leaderboard-confirmed 74% SWE-bench Verified and 70% long-context retrieval at its native 128K justify a place in a telecom, business-process or tool-collaboration stack, especially at `high` effort or self-hosted on MIT weights. **What this pass changes is the recommendation's confidence, not its direction:** Ant's own 124B/5.1B **Ling-3.0-flash** (2026-08-04) is claimed by Ant to "match or outperform" this model, carries an **AA Intelligence Index of 20.1** against Ring's 17, costs a fraction as much, and Ling-3.1-flash reaches **41.1**. For any new deployment, the 1T/63B-active Ring is very likely the wrong choice within its own family.
