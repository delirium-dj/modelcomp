## Model card

- **Name:** Ring-2.6-1T
- **Short description:** inclusionAI's (Ant Group's) **trillion-parameter MIT-licensed open-weight reasoning flagship** — the thinking half of the Ring 2.6 pair, and "the reasoning-focused counterpart to Ling-2.6-1T" ([vLLM Recipes](https://recipes.vllm.ai/inclusionAI/Ring-2.6-1T)). inclusionAI is explicit that scale is not the point: "The goal of Ring-2.6-1T is **not simply to pursue larger parameter scale**, but to address the real production environments that large models are entering: agent workflows, engineering development, scientific research analysis, complex business systems, and enterprise automation processes" ([Google DeepMind-style model card on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/inclusionAI/Ring-2.6-1T)). Its framing of the capability shift is the clearest statement of agentic intent in this dataset: **"From 'Understanding Tasks' to 'Continuously Executing Tasks'"** — "models need not only to 'answer questions,' but also to understand context, plan steps, invoke tools, execute continuously, and maintain stability over long-horizon tasks." Distinct model; `ling-2.6.1t`, `ling-2.6-flash`, `ling-3.0-flash`, `ling-3.0-tiny`, `ling-3.0-flash-sante` and `ling-3.1-flash` all have their own folders.
- **Provider / open weights:** **Hugging Face and ModelScope under the MIT License** (`inclusionAI/Ring-2.6-1T`), shipped in **F32, BF16 and FP8 (F8_E4M3)**. Official SGLang and vLLM recipes; a Transformers path with `trust_remote_code=True` (architecture `bailing_hybrid`). Hosted routing via **OpenRouter (including a free endpoint), ZenMux and Kilo**; a first-party chat at `ling.tbox.cn/chat`. Note that **Hugging Face itself reports "This model isn't deployed by any Inference Provider"** (7 outstanding requests), so HF-native inference is unavailable. **1,298 downloads in the last month** — low, and the serving requirements below explain why.
- **Release / knowledge:** **approximately 2026-05-08** (convergent across OpenRouter, Benchable, LLM Reference and ZenMux; the Hugging Face page is dated 2026-05-14). The technical report — "**Ling and Ring 2.6 Technical Report: Efficient and Instant Agentic Intelligence at Trillion-Parameter Scale**", [arXiv 2606.15079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15079) — was published 2026-06-13. Knowledge cutoff: no verified public date found. inclusionAI reports entering **OpenRouter's Programming top 10 in the 2026-05-18 audit**.
- **IDs:** `inclusionAI/Ring-2.6-1T` (Hugging Face, ModelScope), `inclusionai/ring-2.6-1t` (OpenRouter, ZenMux), `ring-2.6.1t` (Zen). **A free OpenRouter endpoint exists** (Kilo lists it at $0.00/MTok); self-hosting is free under MIT.
- **Context window:** **128,000 native, extensible to 256,000 via YaRN** — inclusionAI's own download table states "128K -> 256K (YaRN)". ⚠️ This matters: the 256K figure is a **positional-extrapolation extension rather than a natively trained window**, so quality at 256K is not the same claim as quality at 128K, and inclusionAI publishes no retrieval measurement for either. Max output: no verified public figure found.
- **Modalities:** **Text in → text out only.** No image, audio or video. Reasoning: **yes, and prominently** — the model emits **explicit `<think>…</think>` traces**, and the chat template accepts a **`reasoning_effort` field (default `high`)** with two levels, **`high` and `xhigh`**. Tool calls: yes, and this is the model's design centre.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-08):** **no vendor rate card** — inclusionAI publishes weights, not an API price. Third-party routing exists on OpenRouter, ZenMux and Kilo, **including a free tier at $0.00/MTok**. Self-hosting is the primary intended path, and it is expensive: see below.
- **Architecture:** **1T total parameters with 63B active** (a `bailing_hybrid` MoE; the 63B-active figure is consistent across OpenRouter, Benchable and ZenMux). Two aspects are genuinely novel and worth recording:
  - **The Reasoning Effort mechanism.** inclusionAI's reasoning for two levels rather than a continuum is practical: "A format conversion or information organization task has entirely different demands on the model's depth of thinking compared to a math competition problem." **`high`** is "designed for high-frequency agent workflows… production-grade default invocation. It maintains a high task completion rate while reducing unnecessary reasoning token overhead." **`xhigh`** is "tailored for high-difficulty tasks such as mathematics, scientific research, complex logical analysis, and multi-path exploration, granting the model more extensive reasoning space." **The benchmark results below are split by effort level accordingly, which is unusually honest reporting.**
  - **Asynchronous RL training plus the IcePop algorithm.** inclusionAI names the specific failure modes of synchronous RL at trillion scale — "**GPU waiting**: Low GPU resource utilization, with substantial computational power wasted on synchronization waits", "**Insufficient training throughput**", and "**Instability in long-horizon training**: Prone to policy collapse or reward signal degradation during extended training." Their fix decouples "policy sampling and parameter updates into independent pipelines", claiming "sampling and updates execute in parallel, dramatically increasing GPU utilization and boosting training efficiency by **several times**". On top of that they apply **IcePop, carried over from Ring-1T**, specifically to address instability. A **Stick-Breaking algorithm combined with the Async architecture** is promised in a forthcoming report.
  - **Serving cost is the practical constraint.** inclusionAI's own SGLang example runs **four nodes at `--tp-size 8 --pp-size 4 --nnodes 4`** — i.e. **32 GPUs**. 63B active parameters reduce compute per token but do nothing for the memory needed to hold 1T weights.

### Raw benchmarks found

> **Every figure below is inclusionAI's own.** [BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai) has no page for this model (404 on the machine-readable endpoint), and I found no Artificial Analysis, Vals AI or other independent measurement. Partial mitigations worth noting: the model card's **Hugging Face "Evaluation results" block carries leaderboard-linked entries** for GPQA Diamond, SWE-bench Verified, MathArena AIME 2026 and Claw-Eval, the figures are **reported separately per reasoning-effort level** rather than cherry-picked from the better one, and inclusionAI publishes both a **technical report (arXiv 2606.15079)** and a **training-content disclosure summary (PDF)** — the latter being rare.

**`high` effort — agent execution.** inclusionAI frames these as "real-world task execution evaluations":

- **PinchBench: 87.60** — "notably higher than GPT-5.4 xHigh and Gemini-3.1-Pro high"
- **Tau2-Bench (Telecom): 95.32** — "with a gap of **less than 1 point** from the highest-scoring model, demonstrating its stable execution capability in complex business processes, tool collaboration, and industry-specific tasks"
- **ClawEval: 63.82** — "ranking among the top comparable models" (confirmed in the HF leaderboard block as Claw-Eval General 63.82)
- **GAIA2-search:** listed among its "leading results" by OpenRouter and ZenMux, but **no number published**

**`xhigh` effort — hard reasoning.** inclusionAI presents these as the model's "capability ceilings":

- **ARC-AGI-V2: 66.18** — "surpassing Gemini-3.1-Pro high and Claude-Opus-4.7 xhigh". **This is the most significant number in the report.** ARC-AGI-2 is the benchmark most resistant to memorization, and most models I have assessed in this research pass score between 0% and 5% on it.
- **AIME 2026: 95.83** — "on par with multiple leading models" (confirmed in the HF leaderboard block via MathArena)
- **GPQA Diamond: 88.27** — "reflecting robust professional knowledge comprehension and complex reasoning capabilities" (confirmed in the HF leaderboard block)

**Coding**, from the Hugging Face evaluation-results block rather than the prose:

- **SWE-bench Verified: 74** — a strong result that inclusionAI, oddly, does not mention in its own narrative
- **No LiveCodeBench, SWE-bench Pro, Terminal-Bench or DeepSWE figure found**

**Absent entirely:** no HLE, no MMLU-Pro, no AA-LCR, no MRCR or needle-retrieval figure at any depth, no Omniscience or hallucination measurement, and no max-output specification. For a model whose entire pitch is "maintain stability over long-horizon tasks", **the absence of any long-context retrieval measurement is the most conspicuous gap.**

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 84/100.** The agentic case is specific and strong. **Tau2-Bench Telecom at 95.32, within a point of the field leader**, is an excellent result on a benchmark built around realistic multi-turn business workflows, and **PinchBench 87.60 beating GPT-5.4 xHigh and Gemini-3.1-Pro high** is a serious claim about a demanding real-world agent harness. **ClawEval 63.82** adds breadth, and the OpenRouter Programming top-10 placement is independent-ish corroboration that practitioners actually route work to it. The design intent matches the results — "task decomposition, step planning, tool invocation, error correction, and context continuation" — and the `high` effort level exists precisely to make agent loops affordable. Held below the high 80s because **GAIA2-search is claimed without a number**, there is no Terminal-Bench or Toolathlon figure, and nothing here has been independently verified.
- **Reasoning: 86/100.** **ARC-AGI-V2 at 66.18 is the standout result of this research pass on that benchmark.** ARC-AGI-2 is deliberately constructed to defeat pattern-matching, and the models I assessed alongside this one returned 2.6% (GPT-5 nano) and 2.6% (DiffusionGemma) — a 66.18 is a different category of capability, and inclusionAI's claim that it beats Gemini-3.1-Pro high and Claude-Opus-4.7 xhigh is plausible on that basis. **GPQA Diamond 88.27** and **AIME 2026 95.83** are both near the top of this dataset and both appear in leaderboard-linked evaluation blocks rather than only in marketing prose. The **Async RL plus IcePop** work is a credible mechanism for why long reasoning chains stay stable rather than collapsing. Capped by the absence of **HLE, MMLU-Pro and any hallucination or calibration measurement**, and by the fact that the top figures require `xhigh`, which costs materially more.
- **Context window: 58/100.** The weakest dimension, and the gap between pitch and evidence is wide. **128K native is modest** for a 2026 flagship, and the headline 256K is explicitly **"128K -> 256K (YaRN)"** — positional extrapolation, not a trained window, which historically degrades retrieval quality well before the nominal limit. inclusionAI publishes **no MRCR, RULER, AA-LCR or needle figure at any depth**, and no max-output cap. That is a real problem for a model marketed on "maintain stability over long-horizon tasks" and "context continuation": the agentic benchmarks imply it holds state well, but nothing measures it. Scored on measured evidence, which is none, with partial credit for the native 128K and the YaRN path.
- **Multimodal: 15/100.** Text in, text out. No image, audio or video input and none claimed. Template floor for a text-only model.
- **Coding: 78/100.** **SWE-bench Verified at 74 is a strong figure** — comfortably above most open-weight models — and it carries a leaderboard-linked evaluation entry, which is better provenance than a prose claim. **ClawEval 63.82** and the OpenRouter Programming top-10 placement support the "optimized for coding agents" positioning, and inclusionAI explicitly targets "code generation, task decomposition, and engineering collaboration in coding agent scenarios". Capped because the supporting set is thin: **no LiveCodeBench, no SWE-bench Pro, no Terminal-Bench and no DeepSWE number**, so there is no evidence about terminal-driven or long-horizon repository work specifically, and nothing is independently verified.
- **Cost efficiency: 72/100.** A genuinely split assessment. On **licence and access it is excellent** — the **MIT License** is the most permissive in this dataset (more so than the various bespoke "open weight" licences), **BF16 and FP8 checkpoints** are both published, official SGLang and vLLM recipes exist, a **training-content disclosure summary** is provided, and **a free hosted endpoint is available on OpenRouter** at $0.00/MTok. On **practical deployment it is poor**: inclusionAI's own serving example requires **four nodes of eight GPUs — 32 accelerators** — Hugging Face reports **no Inference Provider deploys it**, and **1,298 monthly downloads** confirms that essentially nobody is self-hosting a 1T model. 63B active parameters cut compute per token but not the memory footprint. The `high`/`xhigh` split is a real cost lever and earns credit; the inability to actually run the thing is the deduction.
- **Overall Score: 64.2/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dims (84 + 86 + 58 + 15 + 78) / 5 = 64.2. The multimodal floor and the unmeasured long-context dimension pull the mean well below what the reasoning and agentic numbers alone would suggest. Best fit: **hard reasoning and enterprise agent workflows accessed through a hosted endpoint** — ARC-AGI-class abstract problems, competition mathematics, graduate science, telecom-style multi-turn business processes, and coding agents — using `high` for production loops and `xhigh` only where the problem justifies it. Two honest caveats: **nothing here is independently verified**, and **the long-context story is asserted rather than measured** (128K native, 256K only via YaRN, zero retrieval benchmarks) despite long-horizon stability being the model's central claim. Practically, treat self-hosting as out of reach unless you have 32 GPUs, and use the free OpenRouter endpoint to evaluate.
