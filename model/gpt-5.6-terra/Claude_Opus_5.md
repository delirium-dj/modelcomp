## Model card

- **Name:** GPT-5.6 Terra. No free tier. Variant/alias flag: **Terra is the balanced middle tier of the GPT-5.6 family**, which OpenAI's documentation says "roughly corresponds to the mini model tier used in earlier GPT-5 families" — siblings are **Sol** (flagship, $4/$20) and **Luna** (cheapest). The tier names are durable and advance on their own cadence, so do not confuse this with GPT-5.6 Sol or with any GPT-6-generation Terra.
- **Short description:** OpenAI's cost/intelligence balance model, released **2026-07-09** alongside Sol and Luna. The notable thing about it is that it is not a cut-down model in the ways that usually matter: it carries the **same 1,050,000-token context, the same 128K output ceiling, the same six reasoning-effort levels, the same full agentic tool surface and the same rate limits as the flagship Sol**, at half the token price. In a competitor's own August 2026 comparison table it **tied for the highest Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index score of any model shown (57)** and led five of the eighteen rows.
- **Provider / access:** OpenAI, on **both Chat Completions and Responses**, plus the Batch API. Not supported: Realtime, Live, Assistants, fine-tuning, embeddings, image/video/speech generation, moderation, legacy completions. Supported features: streaming, structured outputs, function calling, file search, image input, web search, **prompt caching**. Responses-API tools are the complete set — web search, file search, image generation, code interpreter, **hosted shell, apply patch, skills, computer use, MCP and tool search** — identical to GPT-5.6 Sol and GPT-6 Astra. **Rate limits are identical to the flagship's: Tier 1 starts at 500 RPM / 500K TPM**, rising to 15,000 RPM / 40M TPM at Tier 5.
- **Release / knowledge:** Released 2026-07-09. **Knowledge cutoff 2026-02-16** (same as the whole GPT-5.6 family).
- **IDs:** `gpt-5.6-terra`. **No dated snapshot exists** — the only listed snapshot is the bare ID, so there is nothing to pin for reproducibility. No free-tier ID.
- **Context window:** **1,050,000 tokens total context, maximum input 922,000 tokens, 128,000 max output** — identical to Sol and to GPT-6 Astra. The 922K input cap means the headline million cannot be filled with input, and the 272K pricing threshold (below) sets the economically sane ceiling far lower.
- **Modalities:** Input: **text and image only**. Output: text only. No audio or video in either direction; image generation is available only as a hosted Responses-API tool. Reasoning: yes, with reasoning-token support and **six effort levels — `none`, `low`, `medium` (default), `high`, `xhigh`, `max`**. The presence of **`none` matters commercially**: thinking can be switched off entirely, a lever GPT-6 Astra removed. Tool calls: yes (full surface above). JSON mode / structured outputs: yes.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-03):** **$2.00 / 1M input, $0.20 / 1M cached input, $12.00 / 1M output**, taken from OpenAI's own documentation. Structural terms: **prompts over 272K input tokens reprice the entire request at 2x input and 1.5x output**, and **cache writes bill at 1.25x the uncached input rate**. Batch halves the standard rate. **Price-history caveat:** a sourced third-party page verified against OpenAI's pricing page on 2026-07-18 listed Terra at **$2.50 / $15.00**, so the current card reflects a cut of roughly 20% on both meters. OpenAI documents an explicit promotional expiry for Sol's reduced rate (2026-11-21) but **attaches no such note to Terra**, so whether this rate is permanent is unverified. Family context: Sol $4 / $0.40 / $20; GPT-5.4 Mini $0.75 / $0.075 / $4.50.
- **Architecture:** Proprietary, closed weights. Parameter count, active parameters and topology: not disclosed.

### Raw benchmarks found

> Two provenances. **OpenAI-reported** = figures published by OpenAI at launch, via a source that links every number to openai.com or developers.openai.com (verified 2026-07-18). **Competitor-reported** = Terra's column in **Google DeepMind's August 2026 Gemini 3.7 Flash model card comparison**, verified directly in this research session. Google's stated conditions for that table: Gemini scores are generally pass@1 with default sampling; **non-Gemini numbers are provider-reported unless its methodology says otherwise, with maximum available reasoning settings preferred**; several evaluations were self-computed; and harnesses, tool access, run aggregation and even video frame limits vary by model.

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 84/100.** **Terminal-bench 2.1 at 87.4% is the highest figure in a rival's own five-model comparison** and sits right at the methodology's ~88% frontier anchor, **OSWorld 2.0 at 50.2% leads that table**, Terminal-bench 3.0 leads it too, and the platform surface is identical to the flagship's — hosted shell, apply patch, skills, computer use, MCP, tool search, with 500 RPM available from Tier 1. Four deductions: **GDPVal-AA v2 at 1,578 Elo is well below the ~1750+ reference** and loses to three models in the same table; **AutomationBench (23.6%), Agent's Last Exam (28.0%) and Harvey LAB-AA (85.2%) all lose to Gemini 3.7 Flash**; Terminal-bench 3.0's lead is on a floor-level 20.8%, so terminal strength does not survive a harder harness; and **Tau3-Banking, Claw-Eval, Toolathon and MCP-Atlas are all unreported** (explicit methodology penalty).
- **Reasoning: 89/100.** The best-evidenced dimension and the reason this model matters: **the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index of 57 tied for the highest score in Google's whole comparison table** — a mid-tier model matching the best general-intelligence figure shown — with **GPQA Diamond 92.9% above the 90%+ reference**, **HLE-Verified 51.1% comfortably above the 40%+ reference**, **GDM-MRCR v2 93.5% just under the 95%+ reference**, and the **leading BioMysteryBench human-difficult split at 49.4%**, which is the harder half of that benchmark. Capped at 89 because the Index of 57 is still **below the 60+ frontier marker**, HLE-Verified and LABBench2 both lose narrowly to Gemini 3.7 Flash, **CritPt and any hallucination measurement are entirely absent**, and the Index figure comes from an older version that cannot be compared with the v4.3.x scores elsewhere in this dataset.
- **Context window: 95/100.** The nominal 1,050,000-token context reaches the ≥1M tier, and unusually for an OpenAI model there is **real retrieval evidence — GDM-MRCR v2 at a 93.5% 128K average, measured by a competitor rather than by OpenAI**, which is more credible than a vendor self-report. Three constraints keep it off the tier ceiling: **maximum input is 922,000 tokens, not 1,050,000**; the retrieval figure is a **128K average with no measurement at 512K+**, so the 100 condition (≥98% at 512K+) is unverified; and the **272K threshold reprices the whole request at 2x input and 1.5x output**, making the economically sane window roughly a quarter of the headline. Offsets: cached input at a true 10% ($0.20), cache writes at only 1.25x, and prompt caching as a first-class feature.
- **Multimodal: 70/100.** Input is **text and image only** with text-only output and no audio or video path, fixing it in the methodology's "+image in = 60–70" band. It earns the top of that band on measured evidence: **CharXiv Reasoning at 85.9% without tools leads Google's entire comparison table**, beating both Gemini Flash generations on dense scientific charts, and OSWorld computer use at 50.2% is entirely screenshot-grounded. The counterweight is documents — **GDP.pdf at 24.7% is 9.3 points behind Gemini 3.7 Flash and 3.3 behind Claude Sonnet 5** — and a competitor table's 78.9% LVBench row cannot be reconciled with OpenAI's documented image-only input, so it was not credited.
- **Coding: 86/100.** **DeepSWE v1.1 at 69.6% leads Google's five-model table**, and **Terminal-bench 2.1 at 87.4% clears the methodology's 85%+ coding reference** while also topping that table — two genuine wins against two Gemini Flash generations, Claude Sonnet 5 and Muse Spark 1.2. Four deductions: **DeepSWE is still 4.4 points below the 74%+ frontier reference**; **Code Arena WebDev Elo of 1,523 is the lowest in the table**, making web and UI generation a clear weakness; FrontierCode 1.1 at 41.3% loses to both Gemini 3.7 Flash and Claude Sonnet 5; and **SWE-bench Verified, SWE-bench Pro, LiveCodeBench, SciCode and Vibe Code Bench are all absent**, so three of the band's four references cannot be checked.
- **Cost efficiency: 74/100.** The strongest value case among the proprietary frontier-adjacent models in this dataset. **$2 / $12 is better than the $3/$15 anchor that maps to ~60**, and what you get for it is unusual: **the tied-highest Intelligence Index in its comparison table at half the flagship's price**, with the flagship's full context window, output ceiling, tool surface and rate limits intact. Four further levers: **`none` reasoning effort means thinking can be switched off entirely**, cached input at $0.20 is a true 10% rate with cache writes at only 1.25x, **Batch halves everything to $1/$6**, and GPT-5.4 Mini at $0.75/$4.50 exists for downward routing. Deductions: the **272K full-request repricing cliff** punishes exactly the long-context work the window advertises; the rate appears to have been **cut from $2.50/$15 as recently as July 2026 with no documented permanence** (OpenAI attaches a promotional-expiry note to Sol's reduced rate but not to Terra's, so the status is unverified); and there is no free tier.
- **Overall Score: 84.8/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dimensions (84 + 89 + 95 + 70 + 86) / 5 = 84.8 — the clearest "buy the middle tier" case in this dataset. Choose it for **terminal-centric agentic coding** (Terminal-bench 2.1 87.4% and DeepSWE 69.6%, both leading a rival's own comparison), **graduate-level science and hard-science reasoning** (GPQA 92.9%, BioMysteryBench human-difficult 49.4%, LABBench2 81.2%), **chart- and figure-heavy analysis** (CharXiv 85.9%, best in table), and **long-input retrieval at 128K scale** (GDM-MRCR v2 93.5%, independently measured by a competitor) — all at half GPT-5.6 Sol's price with identical limits and the ability to turn thinking off. Do not choose it for **web or UI generation** (Code Arena WebDev 1,523, lowest in its table), **document/PDF pipelines** (GDP.pdf 24.7%), **high-Elo professional knowledge work** (GDPVal-AA 1,578, well below frontier), or **autonomous business-workflow automation** (AutomationBench 23.6%). Keep prompts under 272K input tokens or the entire request reprices, and re-verify the rate card before budgeting — it was 25% higher as recently as July 2026 with no documented guarantee at the current level.
