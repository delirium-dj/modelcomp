## Model card

- **Name:** Mistral Large 4 (unofficially "ML4", nicknamed "Le Chonk")
- **Short description:** Mistral's largest and most capable model to date, launched 2026-10-06 in **public API preview** (version `v26.10`) — a 1.05-trillion-parameter natively multimodal MoE with ~49–52B active parameters. Mistral positions it as state-of-the-art *among open-weight models* on cybersecurity, finance and law, and as the strongest open-weight model from the US or Europe. Top use cases: sovereign/enterprise deployment (EU data residency, including a Mistral-operated European deployment "independently of other digital service providers and under European law"), document-heavy legal and financial work, and defensive security research.
- **Provider / access:** Mistral Studio / La Plateforme (`mistral-large-4`, alias `mistral-large-4-0`, version `v26.10`); also OpenRouter (`mistralai/mistral-large-4-0`), Vercel AI Gateway, Puter, B.AI. Chat Completions API. **Status on Mistral's own official lifecycle page:** `legal.mistral.ai/ai-governance/models/mistral-large-4` lists **Mistral Large 4 — base, general purpose AI model, release date October 6 2026, retirement date N/A, Status: Active.** This is the authoritative answer on lifecycle and it supersedes any aggregator label; the model is **not** deprecated, retired or scheduled for retirement. **Weights still not downloadable.** Mistral's Hugging Face page `mistralai/Mistral-Large-4.0-1T05-A52B` is a reserved **"Upcoming release"** namespace — as of 2026-10-17 it showed a countdown with **79 people waiting** and **no checkpoint files**. **Conflict recorded:** Vals AI's model page already reports **"Weights: Open"**, which is wrong on the ground today. **Weight-date conflict, four answers:** Mistral says "by the end of the month"; the Hugging Face ETA says **2026-10-31**; press reports say **2026-10-27** (The Next Web) and **2026-10-26** (Brief IA). No OpenCode Zen ID found.
- **Release / knowledge:** Announced 2026-10-06. Training data collection date reported as **September 2026** (crawler period through July 2026) per Mistral's public training-content summary. **A critical vendor caveat the first pass did not record:** per The Next Web, "the reinforcement learning phase ... is **still ongoing**," and **"the benchmark results are preliminary, and the company expects them to change before the weights are released."** Nearly every number in this report is therefore explicitly provisional by Mistral's own account. Mistral has also committed to publishing more architecture detail, more benchmarks, post-training method and safety testing before the checkpoint lands.
- **IDs:** `mistral-large-4` (Mistral API), `mistral-large-4-0` (alias / OpenRouter `mistralai/mistral-large-4-0`)
- **Context window:** **Still disputed, and now documented on three separate axes.** Mistral's official docs page (`docs.mistral.ai/models/mistral-large-4`) shows **Context 1M**; Mistral's Hugging Face blurb and some trackers repeat 1M; **Artificial Analysis's launch analysis states "Context Window: 512k tokens"** and **Vals AI's model page gives 512k**; earlier reporting found **524,288** in OpenRouter/Vercel metadata. Max output **256K (Vals) / 262,144 (provider metadata)**. Scored on the **verified ~512–524K endpoint limit**, not the 1M card claim. The gap is real and unresolved; it is not an aggregator artefact, since both AA (independent) and Vals (independent) now agree on 512k against the vendor's 1M.
- **Modalities:** Text and images in → text out (native multimodal, 1.6B-parameter vision encoder). **New this pass:** Artificial Analysis notes the API "now accepts **100 images per request, up from 8** for previous Mistral models" — a real multimodal capability change. Reasoning: hybrid instruct-and-reasoning, `reasoning_effort: "high"` or `"none"`. Tool calls / function calling: yes. Structured outputs, document Q&A, prefix completion, batch, agents: yes. **No** video, file, or audio input; **no** non-text output — and Vals independently confirms this ("Video input: not supported, File input: not supported").
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-17):** List **$1.36 in / $0.14 cached / $4.18 out** per 1M, with the crossed-out **50% launch sale at $0.68 / $0.07 / $2.09**. Batch −50%, Priority +75%, Regional inference +10%. **Discount end date — resolved, and the answer is "officially none":** Mistral's own model documentation displays the promo rate with the list rate struck through and **publishes no end date at all.** The Oct 19/20 boundary is a *third-party inference*: Artificial Analysis said "for the first two weeks"; Vercel's gateway table was reported at 2026-10-20; and SXF's rate schedule independently schedules the launch rate through **2026-10-19** and the regular rate from **2026-10-20**. Multiple trackers state plainly that Mistral does not commit to the duration and that on a pass-through route the price "ends with it rather than being absorbed into somebody's margin." **As of this report's date the sale is still live but is expected to lapse within days; budget the list rates.** No Free API tier.
- **Architecture:** Granular **mixture-of-experts, 1.05T total / 49B active in experts (52B including embeddings and output layers)**, plus a 1.6B vision encoder — roughly 5% active. Trained from scratch in Mistral's own European datacenters over roughly two months using about **10 megawatts**: Mistral states **3,800 NVIDIA Grace Blackwell GPUs**, while The Next Web reports **~4,000** — both vendor-adjacent figures that do not reconcile, so both are recorded. Trained on >10T text tokens and 1M–1B images; natively fluent in **160+ languages** including every official EU language. Self-hosting footprint is ~**1.05 TB at FP8 and ~525 GB at 4-bit**; quantized FP8 and FP4 variants are said to deploy on 4–8 B200/B300 GPUs. Mistral's distribution terms for the preview remain *proprietary* (SaaS/partner-served, or on-premise under a bespoke self-deployment agreement); the open-weight licence is **not yet published** (Mistral's own framing "by the end of the month" is the only commitment).

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 75/100** *(up from 74).* AutomationBench 59.9% and AA-Briefcase 1,393 Elo are respectable enterprise-agent numbers, and CyberGym-E2E at 81.7% plus a top-five global Cyber Index placement are world-leading on security work specifically. The raise is earned by **breadth of independent coverage that the first pass did not record**: Vals now measures Mistral Large 4 on **Tax Agent Bench (30.39%, #25/70) and Legal Research Bench (31.73%, #43/79)** alongside Finance Agent and Harvey Legal, giving four independent industry-agent measurements rather than two. Held well below the frontier band by an AA Intelligence Index of only 38 (#64/225), a Vals cost per test of **$14.07 — the worst of 49 models** — ProofBench v1.1 at 10.00% (#45/50), and the continued absence of any Tau3, GDPval-AA or MCP-Atlas number.
- **Reasoning: 72/100** *(unchanged — deliberately, on a broader base).* AA-LCR 81.3% is genuinely strong long-document reasoning. Two new independent datapoints cut against: the **Vals Index slipped to #34 of 49** and **Legal Research Bench sits at 31.73% (#43/79)**. Two cut for: **IOI 45.28% (#31/44)** and **MedCode 40.69%** are mid-pack measurements the first pass had no access to, and ProofBench v1.1 at 10.00% is weak on formal proof. The score is held because the added breadth improves the *basis* for the number without moving its *level* — and because there is still **no published GPQA Diamond or HLE score for this model** and GDP.pdf all-pass is only 18.6%.
- **Context window: 87/100** *(unchanged).* Still scored on the **verified ~512K endpoint limit**, not the advertised 1M. The second pass did strengthen the *independent* side of that finding: Artificial Analysis's own launch article now states "Context Window: 512k" and Vals' model page gives "512k / 256k max output" — so the 1M figure is contradicted not just by provider metadata but by the two leading independent evaluators, while Mistral's own docs still show 1M. The unresolved card-vs-endpoint gap remains the cap; AA-LCR 81.3% at ~100K inputs is the only retrieval evidence and it stops well short of the window.
- **Multimodal: 76/100** *(up from 74).* The new evidence is concrete rather than rhetorical: **SWE-bench Multimodal at 33.13%, independently measured by Vals** — the first non-vendor multimodal coding measurement for this model — plus **the API now accepting 100 images per request, up from 8** on previous Mistral models, which is a genuine capability change that materially extends long-document visual workflows, and AA attributing an 18-point GDP.pdf improvement over Large 3 partly to exactly that. With native multimodal architecture, a 1.6B vision encoder and Dense200 42%, it sits comfortably in the band. Still below the 75–90+ upper range because input is text+image only (no video, PDF or audio path confirmed) and output is text only.
- **Coding: 68/100** *(up from 66).* The load-bearing change is the **Terminal-Bench 4.0 revision**: Vals now reports **27.78% ±1.82 (#20 of 49)** against the 22.73% recorded in the first pass, which closes the 5.6-point harness disagreement with AA's 28.3% down to 0.5 points. DeepSWE 61.7% (or 62% on Mistral's own chart), SWE-Atlas-QnA 59.4%, Coding Agent Index 49.8% and the Surge blind human eval at 3.74 (2nd of 5) are unchanged. IOI 45.28% (#31/44) adds a mid-pack datapoint that does not help. Agentic and hard-codebench performance is still the model's clear weakness — TB4 under 30%, Vibe Code Bench 1–100 at 14.26%, Code Migration 30.56% — and **there is still no SWE-bench Verified, SWE-bench Pro or LiveCodeBench number for it from anyone**, while SciCode's "state of the art among open weights" claim has no published value behind it.
- **Cost efficiency: 78/100** *(down from 85 — the largest single revision in this report).* The reasoning changed on three pieces of evidence, all newly established this pass. **First, the discount is now a known liability, not a hidden one:** Mistral's own documentation publishes **no end date** for the 50% sale, and three independent trackers converge on **2026-10-19/20** as the boundary — i.e. within days of this report. **Second, the list rate is genuinely high:** $1.36 / $4.18 sits between the ~$1.25/$4.25 (≈88) and $3/$15 (≈60) anchors, roughly ~80. **Third, and decisive, the per-task economics are the worst in the comparison set:** Vals' cost per Index test rose to **$14.07 — the worst of 49 models** — and Artificial Analysis states in terms that ML4 costs **"over 4× the Cost per Task of similar-intelligence open weights models"** ($1.13 at list, $0.57 discounted, against GLM-5.3-Flash $0.25 and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash Max $0.27). Buying frontier-adjacent open-weight capability at 4× the per-task cost of the nearest open-weight alternatives is poor value even at the discounted rate. There is no Free API tier, and the model is a preview that can be re-priced with little notice.
- **Overall Score: 76/100** *(was 75).* Arithmetic: **(75 Tool + 72 Reasoning + 87 Context + 76 Multimodal + 68 Coding) / 5 = 378 / 5 = 75.6 → 76.** Cost efficiency (78) is excluded by the v4 rule. The Overall edges up on **Multimodal +2** and **Tool use +1**, partly offset by **Coding +2 being smaller than the cost revision** — and the cost revision is the finding a buyer should actually act on. Best fit: **EU-sovereign or self-hosted deployments doing legal, financial, or defensive-security document work**, where Mistral's willingness to answer, the independently-operated European deployment under European law, and the imminent open weights matter more than raw frontier capability — but validate against your actual workload, since the broad independent index trails cheaper open models, Terminal-Bench 4 is under 30%, and per-task cost is the worst of 49 models Vals measures. **Budget the $1.36/$4.18 list rate, not the $0.68/$2.09 sale that is expected to lapse within days.**
