## Model card

- **Name:** GPT-5.1. No free API tier. Variant/alias flag: **`gpt-5.1-codex` and `gpt-5.1-codex-max` are separate models** tracked independently (the Codex variant scores 51 on a composite tracker against this model's 58.96). **This is a deep-legacy entry** — GPT-5.1 was one of the post-training refinements that preceded OpenAI's first fully retrained base model since GPT-4.5 (GPT-5.5, 2026-04-23), and a composite tracker now places it **#53 of 783 at 58.96/100**, behind fourteen later OpenAI models.
- **Short description:** The GPT-5.1-generation flagship, and at its moment **the cheapest frontier model available** at $1.25/$10.00 — which was its principal selling point. Independent comparison at the time positioned it as the model to choose "for cost-sensitive production deployments, rapid iteration, the ChatGPT ecosystem, when 'good enough' ships faster", against Gemini 3 Pro for reasoning and multimodal work. Its defining weakness is unusually stark and well documented: **ScreenSpot-Pro at 3.5%**, against Gemini 3 Pro's 72.7% on the same benchmark — a roughly 20x gap in screen and interface understanding.
- **Provider / access:** OpenAI API and ChatGPT, plus OpenRouter (`openai/gpt-5.1`). Reasoning: explicit reasoning mode — independent comparison describes it as a **binary thinking/instant split** rather than the adaptive, complexity-scaled reasoning that Gemini 3 Pro introduced.
- **Release / knowledge:** Released in the GPT-5.1 cycle, before GPT-5.2 (2025-12-11). **No verified public release date or knowledge cutoff found.**
- **IDs:** `gpt-5.1`; `openai/gpt-5.1` (OpenRouter). Separate Codex siblings exist. **No free-tier ID.**
- **Context window:** **Conflicting figures. A benchmark tracker lists 200K; an independent model guide lists 400K** and adds the important practical qualification that quality is **"degraded beyond 120K tokens"**. Both values are recorded. Max output: no verified public figure found. **AA-LCR at 80.0%** is the only long-context measurement and is respectable.
- **Modalities:** **Text and image input → text output**, with published MMMU-Pro and Video-MMMU figures. No audio input or non-text output documented. **No OpenAI specification sheet for this model was retrievable** — its documentation page appears to have been retired in favour of current models — so the modality list is inferred from the benchmark set.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-03):** **$1.25 / 1M input, $10.00 / 1M output**, per an independent model guide that explicitly labelled it the **cheapest frontier model** of its generation (against Gemini 3 Pro at $2–4/$12–18, Claude Sonnet 4.5 at $3/$15 and Claude Opus 4.5 at $5/$25). **Re-verify against OpenAI's live pricing page before budgeting**, since no primary source was retrievable.
- **Architecture:** Proprietary, closed weights. Parameter count and topology: not disclosed.

### Raw benchmarks found

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 68/100.** **τ²-bench at 81.9% clears the methodology's Tau-family ~50%+ frontier reference**, which is the one solid result on this dimension, and **AA-IFBench at 72.9%** indicates it follows instructions reliably — a real prerequisite for tool use. Everything else is mid-band or worse. **GDPval-AA at 930 Elo is roughly 820 below the ~1750+ reference** and among the lowest figures in this dataset; **Terminal-Bench 2.0 at 47.6% sits in the methodology's mid band** and behind both Gemini 3 Pro and Claude Opus 4.5; **Gert Labs at 41.24% is low**; and **Claw-Eval, Tau3-Banking, MCP-Atlas, Toolathlon, OSWorld and AutomationBench are every one of them unreported** — six of the methodology's references have no value at all, which triggers the explicit missing-evidence penalty and reflects genuinely thin coverage (19 benchmarks total).
- **Reasoning: 72/100.** The evidence is unusually consistent across sources and unusually mid-tier. **GPQA Diamond at 87.3% (AA) and 87.0% (independent guide) agree within 0.3 points and both fall below the 90%+ frontier reference**; **HLE at 28.5% and 26.5% likewise agree closely and both fall ~12 points below the 40%+ reference**; and the **Intelligence Index of 24.7% is squarely mid-band**, 35 points off the frontier marker. Three further results are genuinely weak: **ARC-AGI-2 at 17.6%** is roughly half Gemini 3 Pro's base score and less than half Claude Opus 4.5's; **MathArena Apex at 1.0% against Gemini 3 Pro's 23.4%** shows research-level mathematical reasoning is essentially absent despite AIME 2025 at 94%; and **SimpleQA Verified at 34.9% against Gemini 3 Pro's 72.1%** is less than half on factual recall, consistent with a 51.9% hallucination rate. **AA-LCR at 80.0%** and MMLU at 91.5% are the two creditable figures.
- **Context window: 74/100.** **The specification itself is contested: a benchmark tracker lists 200K and an independent model guide lists 400K.** Taking the lower figure places it at the methodology's 200K anchor (70); taking the higher places it mid-band in the 200K–500K tier (65–84). What resolves the score upward is **AA-LCR at 80.0%**, a respectable long-context reasoning measurement. What holds it down is the independent guide's explicit practical caveat that quality is **"degraded beyond 120K tokens"** — so the usable window may be well under even the lower nominal figure — plus the absence of any MRCR, RULER or GraphWalks retrieval measurement and of any documented max-output limit. 74 reflects the midpoint of the contested range adjusted upward for the one solid retrieval-adjacent datapoint and downward for the documented degradation.
- **Multimodal: 64/100.** Input is **text and image** with text-only output, nominally placing it in the methodology's "+image in = 60–70" band, and it sits in the **lower half** of that band. **MMMU-Pro is corroborated at 76% / 75.5% across two sources** and **Video-MMMU at 80.4% beats Claude's 77.8%** — both creditable. But the dimension is dominated by a single catastrophic result: **ScreenSpot-Pro at 3.5% against Gemini 3 Pro's 72.7% is a roughly 20x deficit and the most extreme single-benchmark gap in this entire research pass**, meaning screen and interface understanding is effectively unavailable — which rules out computer-use, GUI-automation and screenshot-driven workflows entirely. **Design Arena Website at 1,194 Elo is also the lowest recorded here.** The published Video-MMMU figure implies video input that no specification sheet confirms, so that capability is unverified.
- **Coding: 72/100.** **SWE-bench Verified at 76.3% is genuinely respectable** — essentially tied with Gemini 3 Pro and within 4.6 points of Claude Opus 4.5 — and **LiveCodeBench Elo of 2,243 beats Claude's ~2,100**, so algorithmic and single-file coding are solid. Four deductions: **the AA Coding Index at 49.4% is 20.6 points below the methodology's 70%+ frontier reference**, which is the single most informative composite here; **Vibe Code Bench at 24.61%, independently measured, is roughly a third of Claude Opus 4.6's 57.57%** on real-world build tasks; **Terminal-Bench 2.0 at 47.6% is far below the 85%+ coding reference**; and **SWE-bench Pro, DeepSWE and SciCode are all absent**, so three of the band's four references cannot be checked. Competent at writing code, weak at agentic and repository-scale engineering.
- **Cost efficiency: 68/100.** At **$1.25 / $10.00 it beats the $3/$15 anchor that maps to ~60 on both meters**, and at its moment it was explicitly **the cheapest frontier model available** — the correct choice, per independent comparison at the time, for cost-sensitive production, rapid iteration and "good enough ships faster". That positioning is real and is what the score reflects. Four deductions keep it in the high 60s: **the price is unconfirmed by any primary source** (OpenAI's documentation page for this model was not retrievable) and must be re-verified; **no free tier exists**; the **capability-per-dollar case has been comprehensively overtaken** — a composite tracker places it #53 of 783, behind fourteen later OpenAI models including **GPT-5.6 Terra at $2/$12 with a 1.05M context and a far higher score**; and the degraded-beyond-120K context caveat means long-context work needs chunking, adding orchestration cost the rate card does not capture.
- **Overall Score: 70/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dimensions (68 + 72 + 74 + 64 + 72) / 5 = 70 — a competent, cheap, comprehensively superseded generalist with one disqualifying blind spot. Its remaining credible uses are narrow: **cost-sensitive single-file coding** (SWE-bench Verified 76.3%, LiveCodeBench Elo 2,243), **structured tool-calling** (τ²-bench 81.9%, AA-IFBench 72.9%), **competition mathematics** (AIME 2025 94%), and **long-input reasoning up to ~120K tokens** (AA-LCR 80.0%). Do not choose it for **anything involving a screen** — ScreenSpot-Pro at 3.5% against a peer's 72.7% means GUI automation, computer use and screenshot workflows are simply not available. Do not choose it for novel abstract reasoning (ARC-AGI-2 17.6%), research mathematics (MathArena Apex 1.0%), factual recall (SimpleQA Verified 34.9%, hallucination 51.9%), repository-scale engineering (AA Coding Index 49.4%, Vibe Code Bench 24.61%), or autonomous knowledge work (GDPval-AA 930 Elo). And note the broader evidence problem: **only 19 benchmarks exist for this model**, almost all from one evaluator, with no OpenAI-published figures retrievable — so confidence in every dimension here is lower than for better-documented peers. For any new build, **GPT-5.6 Terra at $2/$12 offers a 1.05M context and a substantially higher composite score**, which makes this model hard to justify.
