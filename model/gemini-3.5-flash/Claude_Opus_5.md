## Model card

- **Name:** Gemini 3.5 Flash — **generally available with a stable model ID from launch day**, not a preview. No `-free` API SKU verified. Variant/alias flag: four siblings share the 3.5 name and must not be conflated with this entry — **Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite**, **Gemini 3.5 Flash Cyber**, **Gemini 3.5 Transcribe**, and (per launch coverage) **Gemini Spark**. A point of historical note: **Gemini 3.5 Pro never shipped** — Google launched 3.5 Flash at I/O 2026 while Gemini 3.1 Pro remained the Pro tier in preview, so there is no 3.5 Pro to compare against.
- **Short description:** Released **2026-05-19 at Google I/O 2026** and positioned as "frontier-level intelligence at 4x the speed of comparable models", with Google claiming it **beats Gemini 3.1 Pro on coding and agents** despite being a Flash-tier model at a fraction of the Pro price. **This is a legacy entry**: Google shipped Gemini 3.6 Flash on 2026-07-21, 3.7 Flash on 2026-08-13 and 3.8 Flash on 2026-09-02, so three successors followed within fifteen weeks.
- **Provider / access:** Gemini API (`gemini-3.5-flash`), Google AI Studio, Vertex AI, the Gemini app and OpenRouter (`google/gemini-3.5-flash`). **Native Google Search grounding is built in** rather than bolted on, which is a genuine differentiator at this tier.
- **Release / knowledge:** Released 2026-05-19. **Knowledge cutoff: no verified public figure found.**
- **IDs:** `gemini-3.5-flash` (stable, GA); `google/gemini-3.5-flash` (OpenRouter). **No free-tier API ID verified.**
- **Context window:** **1,048,576 input tokens / 65,536 max output** — the same context length as Gemini 3.1 Pro. **This is the one model in this dataset with published evidence of severe long-context degradation**: MRCR v2 scores **77.3% at 128K but only 26.6% at the full 1M** (see benchmarks). Filling the window costs roughly $1.57 in input alone.
- **Modalities:** Input: **text, image, video, audio and PDF**. Output: **text only** — Google is explicit that despite accepting multimodal input the model cannot generate images; Imagen 4 or a dedicated model is required for that. Reasoning: yes. Tool calls: yes, with native Search grounding.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-03):** **$1.50 / 1M input, $9.00 / 1M output.** Note the output rate against its own successors: **Gemini 3.6, 3.7 and 3.8 Flash all list at $1.50 / $7.50** — the same input but **17% cheaper output** — and 3.7 and 3.8 Flash ran introductory pricing at **$0.75 / $3.75**, half that again. Google places 3.5 Flash between budget options like Gemini 3 Flash Preview and premium models like Gemini 3.1 Pro.
- **Architecture:** Proprietary, closed weights. **Parameter count, active parameters, topology, training corpus and compute are all undisclosed.**

### Raw benchmarks found

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 82/100.** **τ²-bench at 95.3%, independently measured by Artificial Analysis, is the highest such figure in this entire research pass** and clears the Tau-family frontier reference by a wide margin; **MCP Atlas at 83.6%** is a strong dedicated tool-use result; **OSWorld-Verified 78.4%** and **Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 76.2% vendor / 74.2% independent** (a tight 2-point agreement) are respectable for a Flash-tier model priced well below the Pro line. Four deductions hold it to 82: **GDPval-AA at ~1,345 Elo is roughly 400 below the ~1750+ reference** — the largest such gap among the strong performers here — and the **independent AA Agentic Index of 27.3%** corroborates that long-horizon autonomy is mid-tier; **Terminal-Bench 2.1 falls short of the ~88% anchor**; **Tau3-Banking and Claw-Eval are unreported** (explicit methodology penalty) with the related ResearchClawBench at only 18.0%; and Google's figures here come from **launch screenshots rather than a published model card**, which is weaker sourcing than peers provide.
- **Reasoning: 85/100.** **GPQA Diamond clears the 90%+ reference and is corroborated by two independent labs** (92.2% AA, 92.7% Vals) with no vendor number needed, **HLE clears the 40%+ reference on both measurements** with the independent figure (42.7%) exceeding Google's own (40.2%), and the **Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index of 50.2% is unusually high for a Flash-tier model** — 16 points above Gemini 3.6 Flash on the same current index. Against that, three substantial deductions. **The 60.7% hallucination rate is among the worst recorded here**, at only 51.9% accuracy. **CritPt at 13.1%** is weak and **FrontierMath v2 Tier 4 at 14.6%** (Epoch-measured) is low. And the headline **ARC-AGI-2 figure of 72.1% is vendor-reported from launch screenshots, not ARC Prize verified** — it happens to match exactly the figure ARC Prize *did* verify for Claude Opus 4.8, which is a coincidence that should increase rather than decrease scepticism until independently confirmed.
- **Context window: 90/100.** This score deliberately sits **below the ≥1M tier floor of 95**, and the justification is published by Google itself rather than inferred. The nominal window is **1,048,576 tokens**, but **MRCR v2 falls from 77.3% at 128K to 26.6% at the full 1M** — roughly three in four needles missed when the window is actually filled. A context window that cannot be used at its advertised length is not a 1M window in any operational sense; the honest read is a strong ~128K model with a 1M nominal ceiling, which maps closer to the 100K–200K tier behaviour at full extension. Three mitigations keep it at 90 rather than lower: the **128K figure of 77.3% is genuinely competitive** — independent coverage places it against Claude Opus 4.7's 46.9% and GPT-5.5's 41.4% on the same eval — **AA-LCR reaches 69.3%**, and filling the window is cheap at roughly $1.57 of input. The **65,536-token output ceiling** is half the frontier norm.
- **Multimodal: 91/100.** Input spans **text, image, video, audio and PDF** — audio placing it in the methodology's top band (90–100) — and the measured evidence is corroborated rather than vendor-only: **MMMU-Pro at 83.6% from Google is independently confirmed at 84.3% by Artificial Analysis**, a 0.7-point agreement, with **CharXiv at 84.2%** alongside it. It sits near the band floor for three reasons: output is **text only**, with Google explicitly directing image generation to Imagen 4 or a dedicated model; **Blueprint-Bench 2 at 33.6%** shows structured visual and architectural reasoning is weak; and there is **no document-understanding benchmark** (no OmniDocBench, no GDP.pdf) despite PDF being an accepted input.
- **Coding: 83/100.** The independent evidence is solid: **LiveCodeBench 87.6% and SWE-bench 78.8%, both measured by Vals AI**, are strong figures that do not depend on Google's harness, and **the AA Coding Index at 70.1% clears the methodology's 70%+ frontier reference**. Five deductions: **SciCode at 53.1–53.9% falls fractionally short of the 55%+ reference** on both measurements; **Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 74.2–76.2% is well below the 85%+ coding reference**; **SWE-bench Pro at 55.1%** is mid-field; **Vibe Code Bench at 48.68% and CursorBench at 48.8–49.8%** place it below half on real-editor and merge-acceptance work, and CursorBench *declined* from 3.1 to 3.2; and **DeepSWE is absent**, so one of the band's key references cannot be checked.
- **Cost efficiency: 74/100.** At **$1.50 input / $9.00 output** it beats the $3/$15 anchor that maps to ~60 on both meters, filling the entire 1M window costs about **$1.57**, and **native Google Search grounding is built in** rather than billed as a separate tool — a genuine structural advantage at this tier. Google's "frontier-level intelligence at 4x the speed of comparable models" positioning is supported by the Flash-tier Intelligence Index of 50.2%. Three deductions keep it in the mid-70s, and they are all about succession. **Its own successors are cheaper on output and better on capability**: Gemini 3.6, 3.7 and 3.8 Flash all list at **$1.50 / $7.50 — 17% cheaper output** — while 3.7 and 3.8 ran introductory pricing at **$0.75 / $3.75**, half that again. **No free API tier is verified.** And the long-context collapse means the 1M window you are paying to have available is not usable at length, so the cost-per-*useful*-token is worse than the rate card implies.
- **Overall Score: 86.2/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dimensions (82 + 85 + 90 + 91 + 83) / 5 = 86.2 — a strong Flash-tier generalist with one disqualifying flaw for long-context work. Choose it for **tool-calling agents**, where **τ²-bench at 95.3% is the best independently measured result in this entire dataset** and MCP Atlas reaches 83.6%; for **grounded research**, where native Search grounding is built into the model rather than billed separately; for **multimodal analysis up to ~128K tokens** (MMMU-Pro corroborated at 83.6–84.3%, CharXiv 84.2%, five input modalities); and for **graduate-level science** (GPQA corroborated at 92.2–92.7% by two independent labs). Do not choose it for **anything that actually fills the context window** — Google's own published MRCR figures show retrieval falling from 77.3% at 128K to **26.6% at 1M**, and no amount of nominal capacity compensates for missing three needles in four. Do not choose it for autonomous long-horizon work (GDPval-AA ~1,345 Elo, AA Agentic Index 27.3%), for unaided factual recall (60.7% hallucination rate), for structured visual reasoning (Blueprint-Bench 2 33.6%), or for real-editor coding (CursorBench ~49%, Vibe Code Bench 48.7%). And weigh the succession plainly: **three Flash generations have shipped since, all with cheaper output and better scores**, so this model's remaining case is a validated acceptance suite or a specific dependence on its τ²-bench behaviour.
