## Model card

- **Name:** DeepSeek V3.2
- **Short description:** DeepSeek's open-weight MoE flagship of late 2025 — **685B total / 37B active** — introduced in the paper "DeepSeek-V3.2: Pushing the Frontier of Open Large Language Models" as "a model that **harmonizes high computational efficiency with superior reasoning and agent performance**" ([arXiv 2512.02556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02556)). Its headline contribution is architectural: **DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA)**, "an efficient attention mechanism that substantially reduces computational complexity while preserving model performance in long-context scenarios". Distinct model, and two separations matter: ⚠️ **this folder is the non-thinking route** — [BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/models/deepseek-v3-2) classifies this ID as **Non-Reasoning** and tracks **DeepSeek V3.2 (Thinking)** as a separate sibling — and ⚠️ **DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale is a different model**, the one that carries DeepSeek's "gold-medal performance in IMO, CMO, ICPC & IOI 2025" claim. **None of Speciale's competition results belong to this model** and none are used below.
- **Provider / access:** **Open weights on Hugging Face** (`deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V3.2`, published 2025-12-01), the DeepSeek API, **OpenCode Zen**, OpenRouter and numerous third-party hosts.
- **Release / knowledge:** **2025-12-01/02** (Hugging Face 2025-12-01; the technical report is dated 2025-12-02). The lineage is worth recording because it explains the architecture: **DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp** shipped 2025-09-30 as "an experimental version… **an intermediate step toward our next-generation architecture**", building on V3.1-Terminus "by introducing DeepSeek Sparse Attention—a sparse attention mechanism designed to explore and validate optimizations for training and inference efficiency in long-context scenarios". V3.2 is the productionized result. Knowledge cutoff: no verified public date found.
- **IDs:** `deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V3.2` (Hugging Face), `deepseek/deepseek-v3.2` (OpenRouter), `deepseek-v3.2` (DeepSeek API, Zen). **No free tier / no free ID**, though self-hosting is free.
- **Context window:** **128,000 tokens** with a **max output of 8,192** (convergent across BenchLM and b3blog's specification record). ⚠️ **Correcting this repo's metadata:** `meta.json` recorded "164,000 total; 8K–128K max output (provider-dependent)". The 164K figure matches no vendor or primary source I could find — both independent records give 128K — and a 128K max output on a 128K window is not coherent. I have corrected it to 128K with an 8K output default, noting that individual hosts vary.
- **Modalities:** **Text in → text out only.** No vision, no audio, no video, no generated media. Reasoning: **this ID is the non-thinking route** (BenchLM: Non-Reasoning); the family unifies chat and deep reasoning, but the thinking path is a separate sibling model. Tool calls: yes, plus structured JSON output. The architecture also includes **Multi-Token Prediction**.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-08):** approximately **$0.21 / MTok input and $0.31 / MTok output, with cached input at $0.022** — roughly a 10× cache discount, and among the cheapest frontier-scale rates anywhere. Context: the V3.2-Exp release **"cut API prices by 50%+"**, and DeepSeek attributed that directly to DSA making long-context inference cheaper. Self-hosting is free under the open weights.
- **Architecture:** **685B total parameters with 37B active** per token (up from DeepSeek-V3's 671B/37B), Mixture-of-Experts, with two named efficiency mechanisms: **DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA)** — described in DeepSeek's own technical analysis as "a breakthrough in fine-grained sparse attention mechanisms, delivering substantial improvements in long-context training and inference efficiency while maintaining model quality" — and **Multi-Token Prediction**. The engineering story here is coherent and unusually verifiable: DSA was shipped as an explicitly labelled experiment first (V3.2-Exp), validated, and then productionized with a price cut passed to users.

### Raw benchmarks found

> **This model has the best independent-evidence profile of anything I have assessed in this research pass, and it is worth saying why.** BenchLM's 18-benchmark set for `deepseek-v3.2` contains **zero vendor-reported rows** — every figure comes from a third party: Artificial Analysis, the Claw-Eval leaderboard, the VitaBench leaderboard, Gert Labs, SWE-Rebench, React Native Evals, Epoch AI and OpenRouter. That is eight distinct independent evaluators and no vendor cherry-picking, the inverse of the typical pattern in this dataset. BenchLM ranks it **#101 of 891 at 48.86/100**.

Agent / tool use:

- **τ²-bench: 78.9%** ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/deepseek-v3-2)) — strong, and among the better τ²-bench figures I have recorded
- **AA-IFBench: 49.0%** (Artificial Analysis) — instruction following
- **Claw-Eval: 40.2%** ([Claw-Eval leaderboard](https://claw-eval.github.io/))
- **Gert Labs composite: 29.57%** ([Gert Labs](https://gertlabs.com/rankings))
- **VITA-Bench: 18.5%** ([VitaBench leaderboard](https://vitabench.github.io/)) — the weakest agentic result, on a real-world long-horizon harness
- **No Terminal-Bench, GDPval, Toolathlon, AutomationBench, MCP-Atlas or BrowseComp figure found**

Coding:

- **SWE-Rebench: 60.9%** ([SWE-Rebench leaderboard](https://swe-rebench.com/)) — **the single most valuable number in this report**, because SWE-Rebench is explicitly constructed to be **contamination-free**, which makes 60.9% more trustworthy than a comparable SWE-bench Verified figure would be
- **React Native Evals: 71.5%** ([RN Evals](https://rn-evals.vercel.app/)) — real framework work, independently measured
- **No SWE-bench Verified, SWE-bench Pro, LiveCodeBench, DeepSWE or SciCode figure found**

Reasoning / knowledge:

- **AA-GPQA Diamond: 75.1%** (Artificial Analysis)
- **FrontierMath v2, Tiers 1–3: 22.1%**; **Tier 4: 2.1%** ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/frontiermath-tier-4-v2)) — 22.1% on Tiers 1–3 is a respectable showing on a deliberately brutal benchmark
- **AA-LCR: 45.7%**; **AA-HLE: 11.2%**; **CritPt: 0.9%**
- **Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index: 16.0** — low, and sharply at odds with the GPQA figure
- **AA-Omniscience: Index −46.9%, Accuracy 24.0%, Hallucination Rate 93.3%.** That hallucination rate is the **second-highest I have recorded in this research pass** — on material it does not know, this model asserts an answer rather than declining roughly 93 times in 100.
- **No MMLU-Pro or AIME figure found** for this ID

Multimodal:

- **None, correctly** — this is a text-only model. Design Arena — Website at **1179 Elo** ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/deepseek/deepseek-v3.2/benchmarks)) is a web-design *preference* arena and is **not** multimodal evidence; it is recorded under coding-adjacent output quality.

Long context:

- **AA-LCR 45.7%** is the only quality measurement. **No MRCR, RULER or needle-retrieval figure at any depth** — which is a notable gap given that DSA exists specifically to make long context work, and DeepSeek validated it in a dedicated experimental release.

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 60/100.** A clean split that is useful to know about. **τ²-bench 78.9%** says this model handles structured, multi-turn, schema-bound tool dialogues well — that is a genuinely strong figure and it is Artificial Analysis's, not DeepSeek's. **AA-IFBench 49.0%** is adequate. But the real-world agent harnesses disagree: **VITA-Bench 18.5%** and **Claw-Eval 40.2%** indicate it struggles once tasks become long-horizon and under-specified, and the **Gert Labs composite of 29.57%** points the same way. With **no Terminal-Bench, GDPval, Toolathlon or AutomationBench figure at all**, there is nothing to resolve the tension. Scored at the midpoint: reliable function caller, unproven agent.
- **Reasoning: 52/100.** **AA-GPQA Diamond 75.1%** and **FrontierMath v2 Tiers 1–3 at 22.1%** are both real strengths — the FrontierMath figure especially, since Epoch AI's benchmark resists memorization by design. Everything else cuts the other way: an **Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index of just 16.0**, **HLE 11.2%**, **CritPt 0.9%**, **FrontierMath Tier 4 at 2.1%**, and decisively **AA-Omniscience at −46.9 with a 93.3% hallucination rate against 24.0% accuracy**. A model that fabricates on ~93% of what it does not know cannot be trusted for factual work regardless of its GPQA score. The one fair mitigation: **this is the non-thinking route**, and DeepSeek ships a separate Thinking variant that is not scored here.
- **Context window: 58/100.** **128K is modest** for a late-2025 flagship, and the **8K max output is genuinely restrictive** — it caps how much the model can produce in one pass regardless of how much it can read. Against that, **DSA is the most substantive long-context engineering work in this report**: a fine-grained sparse attention mechanism that DeepSeek shipped as a labelled experiment, validated, then productionized while **cutting API prices by over 50%** on the strength of it. That is efficiency delivered to users rather than claimed. Capped because **AA-LCR 45.7% is only middling** and there is **no retrieval curve at any depth** — DSA's quality-preservation claim remains unverified by any public needle test.
- **Multimodal: 15/100.** Text in, text out. No vision, audio or video, and none claimed. Template floor. The Design Arena Website Elo of 1179 is deliberately **not** counted here, since it measures web-output preference rather than visual understanding.
- **Coding: 66/100.** Two independent numbers, both decent, and one of them unusually credible. **SWE-Rebench 60.9%** matters more than its face value because the benchmark is built to be contamination-free — a 60.9% there is worth more than a higher SWE-bench Verified figure from a model trained on GitHub. **React Native Evals 71.5%** shows the capability transfers to a real framework, and **Design Arena Website 1179 Elo** indicates the generated output is reasonable to look at. Capped because the coverage is thin — **no SWE-bench Verified, SWE-bench Pro, LiveCodeBench, DeepSWE, Terminal-Bench or SciCode figure exists** — so there is no evidence about repository-scale or terminal-driven engineering, and the 8K output cap is a practical limit on large diffs.
- **Cost efficiency: 90/100.** Excellent on every axis that can be measured. **Open weights on Hugging Face**, **~$0.21 in / $0.31 out per MTok with cached input at $0.022** — a ~10× cache discount and roughly an order of magnitude below most models in this dataset at comparable capability — and a **documented 50%+ price cut that DeepSeek attributed directly to the DSA efficiency gain**, i.e. an architectural improvement actually passed through to users rather than retained as margin. **37B active of 685B** keeps inference compute low. Two deductions: **685B total parameters make self-hosting demanding** despite the sparse activation, and there is **no free tier**. Getting τ²-bench 78.9%, GPQA 75.1% and SWE-Rebench 60.9% at 21 cents per million input tokens is close to the best price-to-capability ratio here.
- **Overall Score: 50.2/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dims (60 + 52 + 58 + 15 + 66) / 5 = 50.2. The text-only floor and the modest 128K window hold the mean down more than capability warrants; read the dimensions rather than the total. Best fit: **high-volume, cost-sensitive text work with verified inputs** — structured tool calling and function-dispatch workloads (τ²-bench 78.9%), repository patching and framework-level coding (SWE-Rebench 60.9%, RN Evals 71.5%), and graduate-science Q&A (GPQA 75.1%) — ideally self-hosted or through the cached-input path. **Do not use it unguarded for factual work: a 93.3% hallucination rate against 24.0% accuracy is disqualifying for anything unverified**, and if you need reasoning depth, the separate Thinking variant rather than this route is the right target. The thing most worth respecting here is engineering honesty: DeepSeek labelled DSA an experiment, proved it, productionized it, and cut prices by half because of it.
