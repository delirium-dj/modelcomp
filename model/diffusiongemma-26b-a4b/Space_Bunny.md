## Model card

- **Name:** DiffusionGemma 26B A4B (instruction-tuned: DiffusionGemma 26B A4B IT)
- **Short description:** Google's experimental open-weight **discrete-diffusion** language model — the first diffusion LLM (dLLM) natively supported in vLLM. It generates blocks of 256 tokens in parallel across ~12 forward passes instead of decoding one token at a time, reaching ~1,500 output tokens/s on a single H100 (vLLM FP8: 1,288 tok/s on H200, 1,008 on H100). Fine-tuned from Gemma 4 26B A4B rather than trained from scratch, using under 10% of the AR model's training-token budget. Google explicitly states its **overall output quality is lower than standard Gemma 4** and recommends standard Gemma 4 for maximum quality. It is a distinct model from Gemma 4 26B A4B (same backbone, different decoding objective), not an alias.
- **Provider / access:** open weights on Hugging Face (`google/diffusiongemma-26B-A4B-it`, plus the base `diffusiongemma-26B-A4B`) under Apache 2.0; NVIDIA NIM (`google/diffusiongemma-26b-a4b-it`, BF16 build `nim-bf16-20260610`); vLLM model runner v2 (day-zero kernels; multi-GPU PR #46177 merged 2026-06-26); quantized FP8 / dynamic-activation and NVFP4 checkpoints from RedHatAI / NVIDIA via Model Optimizer; third-party early benchmarking by Unsloth reports up to ~2,000 tok/s on RTX 6000. Hosted at `opencode/diffusiongemma-26b-a4b`.
- **Release / knowledge:** released 2026-06-10 (Google launch post, vLLM and NVIDIA NIM same day); DiffusionGemma Technical Report arXiv 2608.00146 dated 2026-07-31. **Knowledge cutoff not stated by Google.** New this pass: NVIDIA's Model-Optimizer card discloses a **pre-training data collection cutoff of January 2025** — vendor-adjacent, not from Google, and treated as indicative only.
- **IDs:** `google/diffusiongemma-26B-A4B`, `google/diffusiongemma-26B-A4B-it`, NVIDIA NIM `google/diffusiongemma-26b-a4b-it`, NVIDIA `nvidia/diffusiongemma-26B-A4B-it-NVFP4`, hosted `opencode/diffusiongemma-26b-a4b`.
- **Context window:** 256K tokens (NVIDIA NIM and NGC say 262,144; the Google spec table says "Up to 256K"; llmboard lists NIM at 250K and Pioneer at 262.1K). No separate max-output figure published in any reviewed source.
- **Modalities:** **contradictory across sources, recorded not resolved.** Google's own model-card prose says it "is multimodal, handling **text, image, and video** inputs to generate text output", and NVIDIA NIM/NGC repeat "processes text, image, and video inputs". But Google's own **spec table** row reads **Supported Modalities: Text, Image** — video is omitted from the structured field on the same page. No audio input. Configurable thinking mode, native function calling, multilingual across 35+ languages. **Also supports standard left-to-right autoregressive decoding** as a fallback — quality recovers part of the TD-mode gap, at much lower throughput.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-17):** Apache 2.0 open weights, self-host in practice. **New this pass: a third-party hosted price now exists — Pioneer at $0.50 input / $0.50 output per 1M** (llmboard, updated 2026-10-16, 262.1K context). NVIDIA NIM still publishes no PAYG rate (container/self-host deployment). Google states the quantized 26B-total/3.8B-active MoE **fits inside 18 GB VRAM** on high-end consumer GPUs; Unsloth's Q4_K_M GGUF is 16 GB. Native NVFP4 kernels accelerate it on Hopper, Blackwell, DGX Spark/Station and RTX PRO.
- **Architecture:** MoE, 25.2B total / 3.8B active (Gemma 4 26B A4B backbone: 8 of 128 experts active + 1 shared, 30 layers, 262K vocab), converted to a diffusion head with bidirectional attention and block-wise parallel generation of 256-token blocks; encoder-decoder style denoising layout; ~20 tokens per forward pass vs ~3–6 TPF for state-of-the-art speculative decoding. Two-stage training: (1) SFT for bidirectional denoising, (2) RL + sampler distillation. Recommended sampler: Entropy-Bounded Denoising with Adaptive Stopping (max 48 denoising steps, temperature linear decay 0.8 → 0.4, entropy bound 0.1, adaptive-stopping entropy threshold 0.005).

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 40/100.** Cut 20 from 60 — the largest change in this report. The first pass recorded "no verified public score found" for Terminal-Bench, Tau3, MCP-Atlas and Claw-Eval and scored on Tau2 56.2% alone. Those rows now exist: **Terminal-Bench 2.1 12.4%** (#135 of 182) and **Tau3-Banking 6.6%** (#117 of 174), both Verified, plus **GDPval-AA 554 Elo** (#211 of 340). Both agentic evals are far below the methodology's mid band (TB2.1 45–60% → 50–70) and Tau3 at 6.6% is essentially floor. Google's own Tau2 56.2% remains the best figure available and is 12 points below the Gemma 4 backbone. No MCP-Atlas, Claw-Eval or Toolathlon row exists.
- **Reasoning: 56/100.** Cut 12 from 68. Table C contradicts Table A on the model's own terms: **AA-GPQA Diamond 66.9%** against Google's 73.2% and NVIDIA's 69.4%; **AA Index 13.5** (below the methodology's 20–35 mid band); **CritPt 0.29%**; **HLE 10.8%**; **AA-Omniscience Accuracy 16.58%**. GSM8K 96.3% shows arithmetic is intact and BigBench Extra Hard 47.6% is mid-pack, and AR-mode fallback recovers much of the gap (GPQA-D 79.8, AIME 84.2) — those are what keep this at 56 rather than near the floor.
- **Context window: 68/100.** Cut 8 from 76. The nominal 256K tier is unchanged, but the first pass's retrieval picture was already the weakest in the comparison and is now corroborated independently: **MRCR v2 @128k 32.0%** (vs 44.1% for the AR backbone) and **AA-LCR 18.3%** (#281 of 409) — llmboard's AA-LCR v1.1 reading is 19.67%. No RULER/GraphWalks figure exists at any length, and the 256K limit is documented but entirely unmeasured above 128K.
- **Multimodal: 62/100.** Held. Image and video input are supported and MATH-Vision 70.5% / MedXpertQA MM 49.0% are respectable, but MMMU Pro drops to 54.3% from 73.8% (#61 of 73) and OmniDocBench edit distance more than doubles (0.319 vs 0.149, #15 of 20) — document/OCR parsing degrades badly under parallel block generation. No audio input. The modality contradiction is recorded above and does not change the score, since video input is claimed by Google's own prose and by NVIDIA; only the structured spec field omits it.
- **Coding: 50/100.** Cut 14 from 64. New independent rows: **SciCode 34.3%** (#200 of 458) and **Terminal-Bench 2.1 12.4%**. LiveCodeBench v6 69.1% sits at the 36.9th percentile of 66 and Codeforces ranks **last of 20** on llmboard. Vendor-only figures remain LiveCodeBench v6 69.1% / Codeforces 1429, and TD-no-think falls to 60.6 / 959, so reasoning effort is load-bearing for code here. NVIDIA's NVFP4 table fills the gap with HumanEval 94.09% and IFEval 94.01% — function-level generation is fine — but that table is the straddling, undisclosed-harness one and is not used to move the score. No SWE-bench Verified or SWE-Pro figure exists at any harness.
- **Cost efficiency: 92/100.** Raised 4 from 88. The first pass had no published per-token anchor and said so. **Pioneer now publishes $0.50 in / $0.50 out per 1M** (262.1K context), which is cheaper than the methodology's $0.60/$2.20 ≈ 92 reference point on both axes, on top of Apache 2.0 open weights, a Q4_K_M build that fits a single 24 GB GPU, native NVFP4 kernels and ~1,500 tok/s on one H100. Not higher because NVIDIA NIM still publishes no PAYG rate, NVIDIA's own NIM card has been demoted to Past Releases, and mainstream llama.cpp/Ollama/LM Studio cannot load it at all.
- **Overall Score: 55.2/100.** (40 + 56 + 68 + 62 + 50) / 5 = **55.2**, cost excluded. Down from 66.0. Best fit is unchanged in kind — latency-critical interactive editing, high-throughput batch generation, and diffusion research — but the ceiling is now measured rather than assumed: the speed frontier of open text diffusion at roughly Gemma 4 26B A4B minus 15–20 points of quality, with independent agentic and coding scores near the floor. Explicitly not for maximum-quality production output, where Google itself recommends standard Gemma 4.
