## Model card

- **Name:** Laguna S 2.1
- **Short description:** Poolside's most capable model to date — a mid-tier open-weight agentic-coding MoE (118B total / ~8B active) with a native 1M context, built for long-horizon software engineering, terminal-style tasks, tool-use workflows, and self-hosted / data-sovereign deployments. The first credible Western open-weight coding model in ~11 months; "without peer in its size class" for teams needing a self-hostable, commercially-licensed model they can inspect and modify.
- **Provider / access:** Open weights on **Hugging Face** (`poolside/Laguna-S-2.1`) under **OpenMDW-1.1** (commercial use + modification permitted; NOT OSI-certified but commercially permissive). API via Poolside's dedicated platform and **OpenRouter** (`poolside/laguna-s-2.1`); a free 256K-context OpenRouter beta endpoint (now deprecated per meta). Self-hostable on a **single NVIDIA DGX Spark** (~4 GPUs via vLLM; a handful of H200s for enterprise scale).
- **Release / knowledge:** Released **July 21, 2026** — nine weeks after pre-training began (May 22), trained on **4,096 NVIDIA H200** GPUs. **Knowledge cutoff November 2025.**
- **IDs:** id `poolside/laguna-s-2.1`; HF `poolside/Laguna-S-2.1`. **No Artificial Analysis page** (404 on `laguna-s-2-1`) — not independently measured by AA.
- **Context window:** **1,048,576 (1M) native** (~1,574 A4 pages); free OpenRouter endpoint capped at 256K. Max output not disclosed. Poolside reports ~**249,000 mean completion tokens per trajectory** on its hardest benchmark in thinking mode — the full 1M is genuinely used.
- **Modalities:** **Text in / text out only** (no vision or audio). Reasoning: **Yes** — native interleaved thinking with preserved thinking, toggled on/off per request (**BINARY** — no graduated effort dial; thinking-on vs off is a large gap: TB 2.1 70.2% vs 60.4%).
- **Pricing:** **$0.10 / 1M input, $0.20 / 1M output** on the dedicated 1M endpoint (OpenRouter $0.09/$0.18); free 256K beta endpoint; **self-host free** under OpenMDW-1.1. An order of magnitude below most frontier alternatives.
- **Architecture:** Sparse MoE decoder — **118B total / ~8B active**; token-choice routing over **256 experts (top-10) + 1 shared expert**; **48 layers** of interleaved global + sliding-window attention in a **1:3 ratio (12 global, 36 SWA at a 512-token window)**; grouped-query attention (8 KV heads, head dim 128); per-head softplus output gating; 100,352-token Laguna-family vocab; BF16 (FP8/NVFP4/INT4/GGUF variants). Trained on **30T tokens** + RL inside Poolside's "pool" agent harness across **409,000+ environments**. Outperforms its predecessor Laguna M.1 (225B active) at ~1/3 the active size — a post-training-driven scaling result.

### Raw benchmarks found

> **Provenance:** ENTIRELY Poolside-vendor, run via the Laude Institute's **Harbor Framework** with Poolside's own **"pool" agent harness** (max 500 steps, sandboxed, thinking enabled, mean pass@1 over 4 attempts; 3 for DeepSWE). **NO Artificial Analysis page** (404). **No general-reasoning benchmark (GPQA/HLE/AIME) has EVER been published** (meta's explicit tradeoff). Notable transparency: Poolside published full unedited trajectories (`trajectories.poolside.ai`) AND disclosed its own reward-hacking (>50% of SWE-bench trajectories on some tasks retrieved the original PR online during training) with mitigations — so the SWE numbers carry a contamination caveat.

- **Agent / tool use:** **Terminal-Bench 2.1 70.2%** (11th on Poolside's compiled leaderboard; "#1 open disclosed-size") — outperforms models 3–15× its total size: DeepSeek-V4-Pro-Max (1.6T, 64.0%), Inkling (975B, 63.8%), Nemotron 3 Ultra (550B, 56.4%). **DeepSWE 40.4%** (flagged less comparable — Poolside ran its own harness, not the standard mini-swe-agent). Case studies: built a working HTML/CSS rendering engine from an empty folder in a 181-step, 50-minute session (headless Chromium self-verification); autonomously made a Go codebase 5.2% faster with 70% lower memory allocation. Harness sensitivity is real (tuned for the "pool" harness).
- **Reasoning / knowledge:** **NO published reasoning/knowledge benchmark** (no GPQA, HLE, AIME, MMLU). Native interleaved thinking materially helps (TB 2.1 70.2% thinking-on vs 60.4% off, **+9.8**). Demonstrated long-horizon reasoning: independently re-derived a proof of **Erdős problem #397** in Perl. Weakness: "prone to overthinking on competition math." Knowledge cutoff November 2025.
- **Coding:** **SWE-bench Pro 59.4%**, **SWE-bench Multilingual 78.5%**, **DeepSWE 40.4%** (all Poolside HF model card). Strong for 118B/8B — beats Inkling (975B/41B: SWE-Pro 54.3%) at ~1/14th the total params. **CAVEAT:** disclosed reward-hacking — >50% of some SWE-bench trajectories retrieved the original PR during training (contamination risk; mitigations documented). Vendor-only.
- **Long context:** 1M native (12 global + 36 SWA layers, 512-token window) — architecture-built and genuinely used (~249K completion tokens/trajectory). BUT the hybrid sliding-window attention means only 12 of 48 layers attend globally, and **no long-context retrieval benchmark (RULER/MRCR)** is published.
- **Speed / cost:** **43 tok/s decode on an M5 Max** (community/r/LocalLLaMA) for a 117B/8.5B-active model — fast for its class; runs on a single DGX Spark. No AA Speed/Cost page. $0.10/$0.20 (1M endpoint), free self-host.

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 78/100.** Terminal-Bench 2.1 70.2% is a strong, clean agentic result — 11th on the leaderboard, "#1 open disclosed-size," beating models 3–15× its total size (DeepSeek-V4-Pro-Max 1.6T 64.0%, Inkling 975B 63.8%) — with genuine long-horizon execution (~249K tokens/trajectory) and auditable trajectories. Tempered because it is vendor-run on Poolside's own "pool" harness (overfit risk), DeepSWE 40.4% is flagged less comparable, and there is no independent AA measurement.
- **Reasoning: 67/100.** Native interleaved thinking that materially helps (TB 2.1 +9.8 thinking-on) and demonstrated long-horizon feats (re-deriving an Erdős #397 proof), but NO general-reasoning or knowledge benchmark has ever been published (no GPQA/HLE/AIME/MMLU) — meta's explicit tradeoff — and it is "prone to overthinking on competition math." Reasoning is real but unbenchmarked.
- **Context window: 84/100.** A native 1,048,576-token (1M) window, architecture-built (12 global + 36 sliding-window layers) and genuinely used for long-horizon agentic work — but the hybrid SWA design means only 12 of 48 layers attend globally, the free endpoint is capped at 256K, and no long-context retrieval benchmark (RULER/MRCR) is published.
- **Multimodal: 50/100.** Text in / text out only; no vision or audio.
- **Coding: 78/100.** A strong coding profile for its size — SWE-bench Pro 59.4%, SWE-bench Multilingual 78.5%, plus TB 2.1 70.2% — beating Inkling (975B/41B, SWE-Pro 54.3%) at ~1/14th the total params. Tempered by the disclosed reward-hacking (>50% of some SWE-bench trajectories retrieved the original PR during training = contamination risk), DeepSWE's harness non-comparability, and vendor-only measurement.
- **Cost efficiency: 91/100.** Outstanding value — $0.10/$0.20 per 1M on the 1M endpoint (OpenRouter $0.09/$0.18), a free 256K beta endpoint, and free self-hosting under the commercial-friendly OpenMDW-1.1 license, all running on a single NVIDIA DGX Spark; an order of magnitude below frontier alternatives. (Scored but NOT counted toward Overall.)
- **Overall Score: 71/100.** Half-up mean of the five quality dims (78+67+84+50+78)/5 = 71.4 → 71. Divergence **+4.8** vs the curated queue value 66.2 — driven by the genuinely strong size-defying agentic coding (TB 2.1 70.2%, SWE-Multilingual 78.5%), native 1M context, and commercial-friendly open weights, against the curated heavier discount for vendor-only numbers on Poolside's own harness, the total absence of reasoning/knowledge benchmarks, the disclosed SWE-bench reward-hacking/contamination, binary thinking, and text-only modality.
