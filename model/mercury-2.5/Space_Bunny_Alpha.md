## Model card

- **Name:** Mercury 2.5
- **Short description:** Inception's flagship production **diffusion LLM (dLLM)** — the largest diffusion language model the company says it has trained. Instead of emitting tokens left-to-right, it denoises many token positions in parallel over multiple passes, which is where its headline speed comes from: **1,107 output tokens/second** on ordinary NVIDIA GPUs, sub-300 ms time-to-first-token. It is positioned explicitly against the *cost-optimized* frontier tier (GPT-5.6 Luna at low effort, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite, Claude Haiku 4.5), not against flagship reasoning models. **Variant note:** Mercury 2.5 and Mercury 2.5 Preview (2026-08-31) are the **same model eight days apart** — same engine, same speed claim, same price sheet; the preview page's 256K figure was corrected to 260K and "more than 10 points" intelligence gain was reframed as "40 percent" at launch. Call the production name.
- **Provider / access:** **Inception API** (`mercury-2.5`, OpenAI-compatible), plus **OpenRouter**, **Baseten**, **Vercel AI Gateway** and **Kilo Gateway**. Chat Completions style; `tools` / `tool_choice` function calling and JSON-schema `response_format` both supported. 100M free tokens to start. Enterprise deployments offer dedicated capacity, autoscaling, compliance controls and configurable data retention.
- **Release / knowledge:** **2026-09-08** (Inception blog and Business Wire release, same day); Preview channel opened 2026-08-31. Knowledge cutoff listed as **2025-11** by modelcompare.dev — the only published figure found, and not confirmed by Inception itself.
- **IDs:** `mercury-2.5` (Inception API); `inception/mercury-2.5` (OpenRouter / Vercel AI Gateway canonical IDs). No Zen ID, no open-weights identifier.
- **Context window:** **260,000 tokens**, doubled from Mercury 2's 128K (Inception models page; OpenRouter; Vercel AI Gateway; BenchLM; ModelCap). **Maximum output 65,536 tokens.** Per modelcompare.dev the 260K figure is the *combined* prompt + completion budget. Long-context capability is the one place where this model has a real independent measurement: **AA-LCR 68.0–71.7%** depending on the row's vintage.
- **Modalities:** **text in, text out — text-only, and structurally so.** A diffusion LM that generates text in parallel has no image or audio input path; modelcompare.dev lists attachments as unsupported and input/output modalities as text/text, and Venice's model page states "no vision or multimodal input" as a design boundary rather than an omission. Reasoning yes, with **tunable reasoning levels**. Tool calling, **parallel tool calls**, and schema-aligned JSON all supported. Web search available. No non-text output modality of any kind.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-02):** **$0.20 input / $0.75 output per 1M**, cached input $0.02 (Inception models page). **Launch discount 80% off → $0.04 / $0.15, cache read $0.004** (Business Wire, 2026-09-08; OpenRouter and Vercel both list the discounted rate as current). Inception has **published no end date for the discount** — BenchLM flags this explicitly — so the discounted rate must be treated as promotional rather than structural. OpenRouter reports 0.77 s median latency and 128 tps observed on its Inception route, a large gap below the vendor's 1,107 tok/s claim, which was not independently reproduced at launch.
- **Architecture:** **proprietary, API-only, no open weights** — self-hosting and fine-tuning are both impossible today. Diffusion (parallel denoising) rather than autoregressive decoding. **Parameter count and topology are not disclosed by Inception**; AI BENCHY carries a "~100B, low confidence" figure that it explicitly derives from existing Mercury family records rather than from any vendor statement, and that estimate is **not** treated as specification here. Inception's "largest diffusion LLM ever trained" claim is a vendor claim with no published parameter count behind it.

### Raw benchmarks found

> Provenance is mixed and is labelled per row. **Inception's own launch numbers are vendor-reported**; the Artificial Analysis and BenchLM rows are third-party aggregator figures whose internal sourcing is not always disclosed.

Agent / tool use:

- Terminal-Bench 2.1: **34.1%** *(BenchLM, "Terminal-Bench 2.1 (Vals)" row — the Vals harness, not xAI's own run)*
- τ³-bench results: **96.0%** *(BenchLM row — the strongest single agentic-tool number available for this model, and notably out of line with the Terminal-Bench figure; the harness for this row is not identified)*
- GDPval-AA: **0.0%** *(Artificial Analysis row, as published by OpenRouter)* — **flagged as suspect**: a hard zero on a graded professional-work benchmark more often indicates an incomplete or failed evaluation run than a model that solves nothing, and Inception has published no GDPval row of its own. Not treated as a capability measurement.
- CritPt: **0.0%** *(Artificial Analysis row)* — same caveat as GDPval-AA above.
- Tau3-Banking / Tau2-Bench: **no verified public score found.** Inception's launch materials name **Tau3Bench Telecom** as one of the ten benchmarks where Mercury 2.5 gained most over Mercury 2 (10-benchmark average 51% vs 38%, vendor-reported) but **publish no per-benchmark value**, so the delta is known and the level is not.
- Claw-Eval / ClawProBench: **no verified public score found**
- Toolathlon / MCP-Atlas / SWE Atlas Codebase QnA: **no verified public score found**
- Supporting capability evidence (not scored as a benchmark): native tool use, **parallel tool calls**, schema-aligned JSON, tunable reasoning, and web search are all documented on the serving endpoints; parallel tool calling is the specific capability that a diffusion decoder's batched generation makes structurally cheap.

Reasoning / knowledge:

- GPQA Diamond: **79.0%** *(BenchLM row; identical to Inception's own launch figure of 79% GPQA Diamond, which orcarouter.ai notes BenchLM carries as **provider-reported** — so treat 79% as a vendor number with an independent-looking label, not as an independent reproduction)*
- HLE: **11.8%** *(Artificial Analysis)*
- LCR / MLCR: **AA-LCR 71.7%** *(Artificial Analysis, current row)* / **68.0%** *(BenchLM row)* — both listed because the two aggregators disagree; either way long-context recall is the model's strongest measured dimension.
- CritPt: **0.0%** *(Artificial Analysis — see the suspect-zero caveat above)*
- Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index: **12.3** *(Artificial Analysis)*. Read this next to the index's own scale, where the current leader GPT-5.6 Sol sits at 58.9 and Claude Opus 5.5 at 57.6 — 12.3 is a **low** absolute placement, and it is pulled down hard by the two suspect zeros above (GDPval-AA and CritPt) plus HLE 11.8%. It should not be read as a clean measure of reasoning.
- BenchLM overall score: **none published — the model is unranked**, with **9 of 482** benchmarks covered and the overall marked "coming soon" (benchlm.ai).
- Omniscience Accuracy / Hallucination Rate: **AA-Omniscience Accuracy 22.7%** *(Artificial Analysis)* / **22.0%** *(BenchLM)*; **AA-Omniscience Non-Hallucination Rate 19.7%** *(Artificial Analysis)* / **Hallucination Rate 67.0%** *(BenchLM)*. These two are **the same measurement expressed inversely and they are not reconcilable from the published rows** — 19.7% non-hallucination would imply ~80% hallucination, which contradicts BenchLM's 67%. Both are recorded; the honest reading is that **this model's hallucination behaviour is poorly characterised** and the ~20% knowledge-accuracy figure is the more consistent of the two. Note also that ModelCap places Mercury 2.5 at Index 36.3 flagged **"Modeled · succession"** — a modelled estimate, not a measurement.
- IFBench (instruction following): **77.0%** *(BenchLM)*; identical to Inception's launch figure, so same provider-reported caveat as GPQA.

Coding:

- SciCode / AA-SciCode: **38.5%** *(Artificial Analysis)* / **38.0%** *(BenchLM)* — consistent across both aggregators, and the only coding row with two agreeing sources.
- Terminal-Bench 2.1: **34.1%** *(BenchLM, Vals harness)* — shared with the agentic section; for coding purposes this is a below-mid real-CLI task completion rate.
- SWE-bench Verified / SWE-Pro: **no verified public score found**
- LiveCodeBench: **no verified public score found**
- Vibe Code Bench: **no verified public score found**
- DeepSWE / AA Coding Index: **no verified public score found**
- Third-party functional testing: **AI BENCHY 7.1 overall, rank #138**, at the `high` reasoning setting — **62.1% attempt pass rate (12/22 tests fully passed), 9.7 reliability**, 174,547 output and 120,068 input tokens for **$0.031 total**, 4.32 s average response time, with **Puzzle Solving ranked #2 (8.7)** and **instruction following its weakest area at #15 (6.5)** *(aibenchy.com, tested 2026-09-08)*. AI BENCHY states explicitly that its ~100B parameter figure is a low-confidence estimate from family records, not a vendor count. This is the only genuinely independent end-to-end run found for this model.
- Note the **latency/quality tension** visible in independent testing: the $0.031 total for 22 tests and 4.32 s average response time (23.63 s max) confirm the speed claim directionally, while the 62.1% attempt pass rate and the 0.0% instruction-following figure reported by benchable.ai's independent suite ("a notable weakness is its 0.0% accuracy in Instruction Following (Baseline)") cut against the 77% IFBench row — the two instruction-following measurements disagree sharply and neither is reproduced.

Long context:

- **AA-LCR 68.0% / 71.7%** — the only long-context measurement available, and it is the one dimension where this model has genuine third-party evidence rather than a vendor claim. Inception names AA-LCR as one of the three benchmarks with the largest gains over Mercury 2 (vendor-reported, level not published by Inception).
- **No MRCR / RULER / GraphWalks figure exists.** At 260K the window sits in the methodology's 200K–500K tier, so no ≥512K retrieval requirement applies.

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 45/100.** The most genuinely mixed dimension in this report. Two real anchors point in opposite directions: **Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 34.1%** is *below* the methodology's mid band (TB2.1 45–60%) while **τ³-bench at 96.0%** would be frontier-class if its harness were identified. Set below the mid band's 50–70 floor because the one benchmark with a named harness is weak, because the two adjacent rows are hard zeros that look more like failed evaluations than measurements, and because the documented strength — parallel tool calling and schema-aligned JSON — is a *mechanism* advantage that no published harness has yet converted into an agentic score. **What caps it:** no GDPval-AA, Tau3, MCP-Atlas or Claw-Eval figure that can be trusted, and a 34.1% terminal completion rate is not what an agentic tool tier looks like.
- **Reasoning: 58/100.** Anchored on **GPQA Diamond 79.0%**, which the methodology places squarely in the 60–80% mid band mapping to 55–65. Not higher, and this is where the caveats bite hardest: that 79% is Inception's own number carrying an aggregator's label rather than an independent run; **HLE 11.8%** is weak; the **AA Intelligence Index at 12.3** is a low absolute placement; and the **AA-Omniscience knowledge accuracy of 22.7%** indicates substantial hallucination on open-domain knowledge questions, which is a reasoning-adjacent weakness the GPQA figure alone does not show. **What caps it:** the two suspect zeros (GDPval-AA, CritPt) make the composite index unreliable in both directions, and the BenchLM/Artificial Analysis disagreement on hallucination rate means this model's factual reliability is genuinely uncharacterised. A score above the mid band is not defensible on this evidence.
- **Context window: 74/100.** Tier mapping: 200K–500K = 65–84, with 200K as the 70 anchor. **260,000 tokens** sits above that anchor, and unlike most models in this tier it has a real long-context measurement behind it: **AA-LCR 68.0–71.7%**. That recall figure is respectable but not exceptional, which is what keeps it in the middle of the tier rather than at the top. **What caps it:** no MRCR / RULER / GraphWalks run, no retrieval figure at any specific window length, and a 260K ceiling that is **less than half** the 1M available from several peers at comparable or lower cost. Maximum output of 65,536 tokens is at the methodology's 64K caveat threshold and is noted, not separately scored.
- **Multimodal: 15/100.** **Text-only in, text-only out** — the methodology's explicit 15-if-text-only floor, and text-only here is architectural rather than an oversight: a diffusion decoder has no image or audio input path at all. Confirmed independently by modelcompare.dev (attachments unsupported, text/text modalities) and Venice ("no vision or multimodal input — text-only model"). **What caps it:** everything — there is no image, video, PDF, or audio input and no non-text output, so no score above the floor is available on any evidence.
- **Coding: 52/100.** The evidence base is thin and consistently mid-to-low: **SciCode 38.5%** (the one row two aggregators agree on) and **Terminal-Bench 2.1 34.1%**. The methodology's mid example (LiveCode 80% but Vibe <10% and SciCode <40% → 65–75) does not apply because there is no LiveCodeBench figure at all. **What caps it:** no SWE-bench Verified or Pro, no LiveCodeBench, no Vibe Code Bench, no DeepSWE and no AA Coding Index row exists — repository-scale bug fixing is entirely unmeasured — and the one independent end-to-end run (AI BENCHY) recorded a **62.1% attempt pass rate** with 10 of 22 tests wrong, which is consistent with a model that prototypes competently and does not hold up under sustained task execution. Coding subagents are an explicit Inception target use case, so this is the dimension where the marketing and the measurements diverge most.
- **Cost efficiency: 99/100.** The strongest dimension by a wide margin, and the one the model is actually built for. Launch rate **$0.04 input / $0.15 output per 1M** with **$0.004 cache read** is below the methodology's ~$0.10/$0.20 = 97–99 rung, and the list rate of $0.20/$0.75 would still sit in that same band. Independently corroborated by AI BENCHY, which ran 22 tests for **$0.031 total** and characterised the cost as "unusually low for its score range", and by OpenRouter listing the discounted rate as the live price across Inception, Vercel and Kilo routes. **What caps it:** Inception has **published no end date for the 80% launch discount**, so this is a promotional rate that may snap back to $0.20/$0.75; and no batch or provisioned-throughput discount is published. Even at list price the model would remain very cheap, which is why the score stays at 99 rather than being discounted for the promotion risk alone.
- **Overall Score: 49/100.** Half-up mean of the five quality dims (45 + 58 + 74 + 15 + 52) / 5 = 48.8 → **49**. The mean is dragged down almost entirely by the text-only Multimodal floor of 15; on the four text dimensions alone the model averages 57.3, which is the more useful way to read it. **Best fit:** a very cheap, very fast **text-only** reasoning model for high-volume, latency-bound inner-loop work — search reranking, query rewriting, classification, extraction, context compaction, tool selection and subagent steps, anywhere a 2,000-token answer landing in under two seconds changes the product. **Not** a fit for anything multimodal (no vision path at all), not a fit for long-context work above 260K, and not a fit for repository-scale autonomous coding on current evidence. Its real proposition is throughput per dollar, and the honest framing from launch coverage is that hard single-shot reasoning tasks still belong to a frontier reasoning model.
