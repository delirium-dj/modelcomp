## Model card

- **Name:** Ember-1
- **Short description:** Fireworks Research's first model in the Ember series — a **post-trained derivative of Moonshot AI's Kimi K3**, trained to emit shorter reasoning traces while keeping the deliberation that changes the answer. The target is not raw capability but reasoning *economics*: reasoning-heavy agentic workloads re-bill every prior turn's reasoning trace, so cutting wasted thinking cuts cost quadratically. **Variant note:** Ember-1 is not a Kimi-family SKU and not a new architecture — it is K3 retrained, and Fireworks markets and catalogs it as its own model. It launched as a **Research Preview with an initial two-week serverless window**, after which permanence was to be decided by community demand.
- **Provider / access:** Fireworks AI **Serverless** (`fireworks/ember-1`), served alongside base Kimi K3. Also reachable through **Vercel AI Gateway** (2026-09-27) and listed on **OpenRouter** and **Blackbox AI**. Fireworks Chat Completions (`https://api.fireworks.ai/inference/v1/...`). Enterprise customers can train their own token-efficient Ember variants on their own data via Fireworks Serverless Training. The endpoint advertises **Zero Data Retention and No Prompt Training**.
- **Release / knowledge:** announced **2026-09-23** (Fireworks Research blog and model page; Fireworks model page dates the serving entry 2026-09-22, Vercel Gateway changelog 2026-09-27). Knowledge cutoff not published separately; inherited from the K3 base.
- **IDs:** `fireworks/ember-1` (Fireworks API model page; ModelsAtlas API identifier). No Zen Free ID.
- **Context window:** **1,040,576 tokens** (~1.04M) per the Fireworks model page catalogue figure, cross-checked by BenchLM (1.04M) and ModelsAtlas (1.0M). Maximum output not separately published for Ember-1; Fireworks recommends reserving output budget for reasoning plus the final answer. **No MRCR / RULER / GraphWalks retrieval measurement exists at any window length** — this is a documented specification carried over from K3, not a measured capability of this checkpoint.
- **Modalities:** **text and image in, text out** (Fireworks model page, Vercel AI Gateway changelog, ModelsAtlas, Blackbox AI). Reasoning yes — traces are exposed and separately countable (this is the model's entire point). Tool calling / function calling supported; JSON mode supported. Implicit prompt caching supported on the Fireworks endpoint. **Video input is not advertised for Ember-1** even though the K3 base supports it — the narrower modality list is what the serving endpoints document, and that is what is scored.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-02):** **$3.00 / $0.30 cached / $15.00 per 1M input / cached input / output tokens** — identical to Kimi K3 on Fireworks (Fireworks model page; BenchLM comparison pages list $3.00 / $15.00; OpenRouter lists the same). The savings story is **entirely on the volume side**, not the rate side: fewer generated tokens at the same rate. Important counterweight from independent testing: other providers sell plain **Kimi K3 as low as $1 / $9 per 1M**, so at equal routing Ember-1 was *more expensive* than the cheapest available K3 route ($2.48 vs $1.96 across a 15-run test) — the correct comparison is Ember-1 against K3 *on Fireworks*, not against K3 everywhere.
- **Architecture:** **proprietary post-training of an open-weight MoE base.** Kimi K3 is open-weight (2.8T total parameters, MoE); Fireworks ran 50+ training experiments and 200+ evaluations across mathematics, coding, instruction following, conversation, search, tool use and software engineering. **Ember-1's own weights, training code and algorithms are not released** — self-hosting is not possible today, and the parameter count of the post-trained checkpoint is not published separately from its base.

### Raw benchmarks found

> Provenance matters for every row below: the five public-benchmark numbers are **Fireworks' own evaluations** (blog post, 2026-09-23), not independently reproduced at launch. The independent replication section that follows is a third-party run and is labelled as such.

Agent / tool use:

- Terminal-Bench 2.1 (n=89): **82.0%** for Ember-1 vs 80.9% K3-max / 77.6% K3-high / 76.4% K3-low — **-51.9% tokens, -$23.10 per task** against K3 max *(Fireworks Research evaluation, 2026-09-23)*. Ember-1 **beats** K3 at maximum effort on this row.
- SWE-Interact (n=75): **20.0%** vs 21.3% K3-max / 13.3% K3-high / 6.7% K3-low — **-32.5% tokens, -$60.80** *(Fireworks Research evaluation)*. Narrowly below K3 max, well above the lower effort tiers.
- τ-2 Bench Airline (n=50): **66%** vs 64% for all three K3 effort tiers — **-5.9% tokens, -$0.30** *(Fireworks Research evaluation)*. Best relative result in the table; note it is also the row with the **smallest token saving**, which is the clearest evidence that the headline "40%" is an average over a 6%–52% spread and not a uniform haircut.
- GDPval-AA: **no verified public score found**
- Claw-Eval / ClawProBench: **no verified public score found**
- Toolathon / MCP-Atlas / SWE Atlas Codebase QnA: **no verified public score found**
- **Doximity Bedside Bench** (500 physician-validated clinical cases across 10 categories), evaluated on Fireworks' new **Specialized Intelligence Index**: Ember-1 **set a new Pareto frontier on cost per task** across both open and closed models, compared against **GPT-5.6 Sol, GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5** *(Fireworks Research claim; a Pareto position, not a single score, and only as good as its cost assumptions — which are Kimi K3's public rate card, not an Ember-1 rate card)*.
- Pareto analysis across industry benchmarks with >50 samples: Ember-1 sits **on or near the quality-vs-cost frontier**, matching K3-max quality at a fraction of the cost and **strictly dominating K3-low**; the same analysis against **GPT-6 Astra, Claude Opus 5 and GLM 5.3** reportedly places Ember-1 as a frontier leader *(Fireworks Research claim)*.

Reasoning / knowledge:

- GPQA Diamond: **no verified public score found for Ember-1.** No third party has published a run.
- HLE: **no verified public score found for Ember-1.**
- LCR / MLCR: **no verified public score found for Ember-1.**
- CritPt: **no verified public score found for Ember-1.**
- Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index: **no verified public score found** — Ember-1 does not appear on the AA index leaderboard (top of table as of 2026-10-01: GPT-5.6 Sol 58.9, Claude Opus 5.5 57.6, Claude Sonnet 5.5 56.0, GPT-5.6 Terra 55.0, Claude Fable 5.1 53.4).
- BenchLM overall: **no public overall score — the model is unranked**, with only **5 of 486** benchmarks covered and the score marked "coming soon" (benchlm.ai, last updated 2026-09-28).
- Omniscience Accuracy / Hallucination Rate: **no verified public score found.**
- **Independent replication** (Jessica Wachtel, The New Stack, 2026-09-29) — 15 runs, 5 per test, both models via OpenRouter routed to Fireworks for a fair speed comparison, reasoning tokens logged separately:
  - *Logic puzzles* (4/5/7-engineer constraint sets): Ember-1 **5/5 perfect**, 3m46s, 13,630 reasoning tokens, $0.27 — K3 5/5 perfect, 12m26s, 16,679 reasoning tokens, $0.34.
  - *Deploy scheduling* (12 dependent services, one team at a time, two blackout windows, optimum 17h): Ember-1 **5/5 perfect**, 1m29s, 6,543 reasoning tokens, $0.10 — K3 5/5 perfect, 4m46s, 7,792 reasoning tokens, $0.13.
  - *Probability* (5 exact-fraction retry-system questions, checked against a 2M-request simulation): Ember-1 **4/5 perfect** — one run made an arithmetic slip (0.94619 vs 0.94629) that propagated into two other answers; K3 **5/5**. Ember-1 1m47s, 6,242 reasoning tokens, $0.13 — K3 6m48s, 9,682 reasoning tokens, $0.19.
  - **Totals: Ember-1 14 of 15 perfect runs vs K3 15 of 15**; **23% fewer reasoning tokens** (against the marketed ~40%); **3.4× faster** end-to-end (an effect Fireworks did not claim); $2.48 vs $3.26 on Fireworks (**24% cheaper**), but $1.96 for K3 at the cheapest available route.
- **Fireworks production A/B tests** (vendor-reported, 2 customers on live coding workloads, ~35% fewer tokens per task at comparable quality; task completion, success scores and failure rates held or improved): published run — **score 0.753 / 21.4 steps / 29.9K output tokens** for Ember-1 vs **0.751 / 23.8 steps / 49.3K output tokens** for K3, i.e. **71.3% reasoning-token reduction and 39% total-token reduction** at statistically indistinguishable quality. One customer moved to full production on the strength of it.

Coding:

- SWE-bench Verified (n=500): **92.2%** vs 93.2% K3-max / 86.0% K3-high / 80.4% K3-low — **-15.5% tokens, -$68.10** *(Fireworks Research evaluation)*. A ~1-point dip below K3 max is the cost of the token saving, and it is the row a high-stakes team should weigh most carefully.
- DeepSWE 1.1 (n=113): **75.2%** vs 66.4% K3-max / 62.8% K3-high / 55.8% K3-low — **-23.7% tokens, -$126.90** *(Fireworks Research evaluation)*. The largest gain in the table and the largest dollar saving: Ember-1 beats K3 at every effort tier here, which the vendor reads as evidence that forcing efficient reasoning improved reasoning quality rather than merely truncating it.
- LiveCodeBench: **no verified public score found**
- SciCode / AA-SciCode: **no verified public score found**
- Vibe Code Bench: **no verified public score found**
- AA Coding Index: **no verified public score found** (no AA index entry for this model).
- **Rejected as unverified:** ModelsAtlas publishes "capability signals" for Ember-1 (MMLU 97, GPQA proxy 95, HumanEval proxy 92, GSM8K proxy 87) that it explicitly labels **"estimated from metadata — not an official benchmark run."** These are metadata heuristics standing in for missing measurements and are not used here; LLM Reference likewise states that **no mapped benchmark peers are available** for the model.

Long context:

- **No long-context retrieval reported.** No MRCR / RULER / GraphWalks measurement exists for Ember-1 at any length. The 1,040,576-token window is inherited from the K3 base and documented by the serving endpoints. The vendor's own architectural argument is *about* context economics — reasoning traces are re-read and re-billed on every subsequent turn, growing roughly quadratically with turn count — which is a cost argument for short traces, not a retrieval-accuracy result.

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 88/100.** The strongest evidence in this report and the only dimension with three independent anchors. Terminal-Bench 2.1 **82.0%** sits just under the methodology's frontier reference of ~88%+, τ-2 Bench Airline **66%** is well into frontier territory against the ~50%+ reference, and SWE-Interact **20.0%** beats every K3 effort tier below max. Ember-1 also **beats K3 at maximum effort** on the terminal benchmark while using 51.9% fewer tokens — the clearest single demonstration that the token saving did not come out of agentic capability. **What caps it:** all three numbers are vendor-run on small samples (n=89 / n=50 / n=75), there is **no GDPval-AA, Tau3-Banking, MCP-Atlas or Claw-Eval row** anywhere, and no independent agentic-harness run exists.
- **Reasoning: 78/100.** The most inference-dependent score here, and it is flagged as such: **no GPQA Diamond, HLE, LCR, CritPt or Intelligence Index value exists for Ember-1**, and the AA index does not list it at all. The score rests on three things that *are* evidence rather than on a knowledge benchmark: DeepSWE 1.1 **75.2%** — a reasoning-heavy agentic suite where Ember-1 outperforms K3 at every effort tier, which is hard to explain if deliberative depth were simply lost; the independent 15-run replication at **14 of 15 perfect**, with the single miss a propagated arithmetic slip rather than a reasoning collapse; and the τ-2 Airline and Terminal-Bench rows above. **What caps it:** with the entire knowledge/reasoning anchor set missing, this cannot honestly be pushed into the 90–100 frontier band that the methodology reserves for GPQA 90%+ / HLE 40%+, and an argument-by-argument reproduction of K3's published reasoning scores is explicitly not attempted here.
- **Context window: 95/100.** Tier mapping: ≥1M = 95–100, and the documented ceiling is **1,040,576 tokens** (Fireworks model page, cross-checked at BenchLM and ModelsAtlas). Not 100 because that tier requires **≥98% retrieval measured at 512K+**, and no MRCR / RULER / GraphWalks run exists for this checkpoint. Max output is unpublished for Ember-1 specifically; noted as a caveat, not scored separately.
- **Multimodal: 68/100.** Methodology band: text + image in, text out = 60–70; placement is high in that band because image input is confirmed across four independent catalogues (Fireworks model page, Vercel AI Gateway changelog, ModelsAtlas, Blackbox AI) and tool calling plus implicit prompt caching are documented on the same endpoints. **What caps it:** **no video or PDF input is advertised for Ember-1** — narrower than the K3 base's documented image-and-video support — and output is text-only, so the 75–90 (video/PDF in) and 90–100 (audio in or non-text out) bands are both out of reach on current documentation.
- **Coding: 92/100.** All three methodology frontier markers are met or exceeded: SWE-bench Verified **92.2%** (frontier ref 90%+), DeepSWE 1.1 **75.2%** (frontier ref 74%+), and Terminal-Bench 2.1 **82.0%**. The n=500 SWE-bench Verified sample is the largest harness row published for this model by an order of magnitude, and the DeepSWE win over K3-max (75.2 vs 66.4) is a genuine capability result, not a token trade. **What caps it:** every number is Fireworks' own evaluation with no third-party reproduction of the harness rows; the independent replication covered logic, scheduling and probability rather than repository-scale bug fixing; and the ~1-point SWE-bench Verified dip below K3-max (92.2 vs 93.2) plus SWE-Interact at 20.0% are the price actually paid.
- **Cost efficiency: 66/100.** Rated against the **same route**, because that is the only comparison that isolates the model's actual contribution: $3.00 / $0.30 cached / $15.00 per 1M, which the ladder puts at **~60** for a $3/$15 model, adjusted **upward** for the Pareto lead — the independent replication measured **23–24% lower cost per completed task** at the same rate, and the vendor A/B measured **39% total-token and 71.3% reasoning-token reduction** at equal quality, which on reasoning-dominated agent loops is a structural rather than cosmetic saving. **What caps it:** the rate card is unchanged from Kimi K3's, so a reader shopping purely on price should note that **Kimi K3 is available from other providers at $1 / $9 per 1M** — cheaper than Ember-1 at the same token count, and in the replication K3 at that rate came out ahead on total spend ($1.96 vs $2.48). The saving is real only against K3-on-Fireworks, and the Research Preview two-week window means neither the price nor the endpoint is guaranteed to persist.
- **Overall Score: 84/100.** Half-up mean of the five quality dims (88 + 78 + 95 + 68 + 92) / 5 = 84.2 → **84**. **Best fit:** a drop-in reasoning-economics upgrade for teams already routing through Fireworks — specifically long agentic coding loops where reasoning tokens dominate the bill and where ~K3-max quality at ~60–75% of the reasoning cost is a better trade than paying K3's rate for K3's deliberation. **Not** a fit as a capability upgrade (it is at or slightly below its own base on SWE-bench Verified and SWE-Interact), as a reasoning-research model (no knowledge or reasoning anchor benchmark has ever been run on it), or for anyone who can buy Kimi K3 from a $1/$9 provider. Treat the Research Preview status as a live risk, not a footnote: Fireworks tied the model's permanence to community demand after a two-week window that opened 2026-09-23.
