## Model card

- **Name:** Ember-1. **Research Preview** — and that status is the single most important deployment fact about it: Fireworks ships research releases with **two weeks of complimentary serverless access** and makes them **permanent based on community demand**, so there is **no commitment that this model will still exist**. Proprietary (Fireworks' own model), served only from Fireworks' serverless API. Variant/alias flag: **this is not a new base model.** It is **Kimi K3 post-trained by Fireworks Research**, so its capability ceiling is K3's and its lineage inherits K3's 2.8T-parameter MoE architecture.
- **Short description:** Announced **2026-09-23** as the first model from Fireworks Research and the first in a planned series of "specialized" models. Its entire value proposition is one claim: **Kimi K3's quality with roughly 40% fewer tokens** — "half the tokens, same answers". The diagnosis behind it is specific and well argued: **reasoning models spend the majority of generated tokens, sometimes more than 90%, on internal reasoning rather than the answer**, and in multi-turn agentic workloads every turn replays all prior reasoning, so **context grows roughly quadratically with turn count** and early reasoning traces are re-read and re-billed on every subsequent call. Fireworks found that turning K3's reasoning effort down gave up too much quality, so instead it trained the model to reason more efficiently while preserving the self-reflection that helps recovery from mistakes. It reached the Hacker News front page on 2026-09-28 with 462 points.
- **Provider / access:** **Fireworks Serverless API only**, served as a serving option alongside the base Kimi K3 model. Also indexed by third-party API aggregators. **Training support for Ember-1 is offered**, so enterprises can post-train their own token-efficient variants on their own data — an unusual and genuinely differentiated capability for a hosted model.
- **Release / knowledge:** Announced 2026-09-23. **Knowledge cutoff: no verified public figure found** (inherited from Kimi K3, whose cutoff is also undocumented).
- **IDs:** `fireworks/ember-1` (Fireworks model page and serverless API). No free-tier ID beyond the two-week preview window.
- **Context window:** **1,048,576 tokens** per one API aggregator; **1,040,576** per another write-up; **1.04M** per a benchmark tracker. The figures conflict slightly and **Fireworks' own launch post does not state a context window at all** — all three values are recorded. **Max output tokens: no verified public figure found.** Note that long-context behaviour is central to this model's design rather than incidental: the quadratic context growth in multi-turn agent loops is the problem it exists to solve.
- **Modalities:** **Fireworks documents no modalities whatsoever** — the launch post never states what the model accepts or emits. Text input and text output are certain from the benchmark set and usage description. **Vision is probable but undocumented**: the base Kimi K3 has native visual understanding, and a post-trained derivative would normally retain it, but **no Fireworks statement and no multimodal benchmark exists for Ember-1**. Anyone requiring image input must verify it against the live API before relying on it. Reasoning: yes, and deliberately shortened — this is the model's defining property. No audio, video or non-text output documented or benchmarked.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-03):** **$3.00 / 1M uncached input, $0.30 / 1M cached input, $15.00 / 1M output — exactly Kimi K3's serverless rate card, unchanged.** Fireworks is explicit that it computed its own cost comparisons using K3's public API pricing. **The entire claimed discount therefore lives in token volume, not in the rate card** — there is no cheaper price per token, and independent commentary correctly notes the saving is **unverifiable from any pricing page and shrinks toward zero on input-heavy workloads**, since reasoning tokens are billed as output.
- **Architecture:** Proprietary post-training of **Kimi K3 (a 2.78-trillion-parameter sparse MoE)**. Fireworks discloses the research process in unusual detail: **more than 50 training experiments and over 200 evaluations**, with **new training algorithms developed specifically to shorten reasoning without losing accuracy**, all run on Fireworks Serverless Training. The training collection spanned **mathematics, coding, instruction following, conversation, search, tool use and software engineering**, covering both standalone problems and extended interactions so the model would adapt to observations and outcomes, with task feedback guiding on-policy planning. Fireworks states it used **its own data and no customer data**. A notable behavioural outcome: the learned restraint also **reduces prolonged unproductive reasoning on unsuccessful attempts**, not just on successes.

### Raw benchmarks found

> **Only five benchmarks have ever been published for this model**, all from **Fireworks' own launch post**, which is unusually transparent: it reports the sample size `N` for each, shows the base Kimi K3 at three reasoning-effort settings for direct comparison, and gives both the percentage token reduction and the absolute dollar saving per benchmark run. There are **no independent third-party measurements of Ember-1 at all**, and **no reasoning, long-context or multimodal benchmark of any kind**.

Agent / tool use:

- **Terminal-Bench 2.1: 82.0% (N=89)** — and it **beats its own base model at every effort setting** (K3 low 76.4%, high 77.6%, **max 80.9%**), while using **51.9% fewer tokens and costing $23.10 less** on that benchmark run. Below the methodology's ~88% frontier anchor.
- **τ²-bench Airline: 66.0% (N=50)** — above the methodology's Tau-family ~50%+ frontier reference, and again ahead of K3 at every setting (low/high/max all 64%), for **5.9% fewer tokens and $0.30 less**. **Only the Airline domain was run**; there is no Tau3-Banking figure and no multi-domain τ result.
- **SWE-Interact: 20.0% (N=75)** — K3 low 6.7%, high 13.3%, **max 21.3%**. This is the **one benchmark where Ember-1 falls below K3-max**, and the absolute level is low for all four arms, so interactive software-engineering agency is weak across the family.
- Tau3-Banking: no verified public score found
- GDPval-AA: no verified public score found
- Claw-Eval / ClawProBench: no verified public score found
- Toolathon / MCP-Atlas / SWE Atlas Codebase QnA: no verified public score found
- OSWorld / AutomationBench: no verified public score found

Reasoning / knowledge:

- GPQA Diamond: **no verified public score found**
- HLE: **no verified public score found**
- LCR / MLCR / MRCR: **no verified public score found**
- CritPt: **no verified public score found**
- Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index: **no verified public score found** — Ember-1 is not in AA's index. (For reference, the Kimi K3 base was independently measured at 59.7 on AA's Intelligence Index.)
- Omniscience Accuracy / Hallucination Rate: **no verified public score found**
- **Bedside Bench (Doximity): the only reasoning-adjacent evidence, and it is good.** A **physician-validated benchmark of 500 clinical cases across 10 specialized categories**, run as part of Fireworks' own Specialized Intelligence Index. **Ember-1 set a new cost-per-task Pareto frontier across both open and closed models, including GPT-5.6 Sol, GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.** The claim is about the cost/quality frontier, not about an absolute score — no raw Bedside Bench percentage was published.

Coding:

- **SWE-bench Verified: 92.2% (N=500)** — **the highest SWE-bench Verified figure recorded anywhere in this research pass.** Against K3 low 80.4%, high 86.0%, **max 93.2%**, so it retains **99% of K3-max's score for 15.5% fewer tokens and $68.10 less** on a 500-case run.
- **DeepSWE 1.1: 75.2% (N=113)** — **clears the methodology's 74%+ frontier reference**, and remarkably **beats K3-max's 66.4% by 8.8 points** (K3 low 55.8%, high 62.8%) while using **23.7% fewer tokens and costing $126.90 less**. A post-trained derivative outscoring its own base model by nearly nine points on agentic software engineering is the most interesting single result here.
- LiveCodeBench: no verified public score found
- SciCode / AA-SciCode: no verified public score found
- Vibe Code Bench: no verified public score found
- **Production A/B evidence (Fireworks-reported, two customers' live coding workloads):** quality held — **score 0.753 for Ember-1 against 0.751 for Kimi K3** — on **21.4 steps versus 23.8**, with **output tokens falling from 49.3K to 29.9K: a 39% total token reduction and a 71.3% reduction in reasoning tokens specifically.** Downstream product metrics (task completion, success scores, failure rates) held or improved. **One customer moved Ember-1 into live production with plans to replace the base model entirely.**
- **Internal dogfooding:** Fireworks ran Ember-1 on its own developers' everyday coding traffic before any customer saw it, and reports that **developers did not notice the switch** while consuming substantially fewer tokens.

Long context:

- **No MRCR, RULER or GraphWalks figure found.** The relevant evidence is architectural rather than benchmarked: Fireworks' analysis of quadratic context growth in multi-turn agent loops, and the measured 71.3% reasoning-token reduction that directly attacks it.

Cross-benchmark summary (Fireworks-reported):

- Across **seven benchmarks and two customers' production traffic, K3's reasoning could be shortened by 35–50% without sacrificing accuracy.** Across every benchmark with more than 50 test samples, **Ember-1 sits on or near the Pareto frontier, matching K3-max quality at a fraction of the cost and strictly dominating K3-low.** Fireworks also compared against GPT-6 Astra, Claude Opus 5 and GLM-5.3 and reports Ember-1 as a Pareto-frontier leader.

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 80/100.** The two published agentic results are both genuine wins over the base model: **Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 82.0% beats Kimi K3 at every effort setting including max (80.9%)** for 51.9% fewer tokens, and **τ²-bench Airline at 66.0% clears the methodology's ~50%+ Tau-family reference** while also beating K3 at every setting. The token-efficiency mechanism matters most precisely here, since agentic loops are where re-billed reasoning compounds quadratically. But the evidence base is thin and the gaps are structural: **Terminal-Bench 82.0% is below the ~88% frontier anchor**; **only the Airline domain of τ² was run (N=50)**, so there is no Tau3-Banking and no multi-domain result; **SWE-Interact at 20.0% is the one benchmark where Ember-1 loses to K3-max** and the absolute level is low for the whole family; and **GDPval-AA, Claw-Eval, MCP-Atlas, Toolathon and OSWorld are all unreported** — five of the methodology's six tool-use references have no value, which triggers the explicit missing-evidence penalty.
- **Reasoning: 78/100.** This is the weakest-evidenced dimension here and the one most exposed by the model's own design. **Not a single standard reasoning benchmark has been published** — no GPQA Diamond, no HLE, no MRCR/LCR, no CritPt, no Intelligence Index — which is a serious gap for a model whose entire intervention is **removing reasoning tokens**. Fireworks argues convincingly that it preserved the self-reflection that aids error recovery and trained across mathematics, instruction following, conversation and search to make the savings generalise, and it reports that across seven benchmarks reasoning shortened 35–50% without accuracy loss. The one piece of hard reasoning-adjacent evidence is strong: **a new cost-per-task Pareto frontier on Doximity's Bedside Bench — 500 physician-validated clinical cases across 10 categories — ahead of GPT-5.6 Sol, GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.** The score therefore sits above mid-band on inherited capability (the Kimi K3 base measures 59.7 on AA's Intelligence Index) plus the Bedside result, but well below the frontier band because **the specific risk this model introduces is unmeasured by any published reasoning benchmark**.
- **Context window: 94/100.** A claimed window of roughly **1,048,576 tokens** reaches the ≥1M tier, and uniquely in this dataset the long-context *engineering* is the product rather than a specification line: Fireworks' analysis that **multi-turn agent context grows quadratically because every turn replays and re-bills prior reasoning** is correct, and the **measured 71.3% reduction in reasoning tokens in live production** attacks exactly that growth — which does more for real long-session usability than a larger nominal number would. Three deductions keep it off the tier ceiling: **Fireworks' own launch post never states the context window**, and the three third-party figures disagree (1,048,576 / 1,040,576 / 1.04M); **no MRCR, RULER or GraphWalks retrieval evidence exists**, so the ≥98%-at-512K condition is wholly unverified; and **max output tokens are undocumented**.
- **Multimodal: 60/100.** **Fireworks documents no modalities at all** — the launch post states neither inputs nor outputs, and **no multimodal benchmark exists for this model**. Text in and text out are certain from the benchmark set. Image input is **probable but unverified**: the Kimi K3 base has native visual understanding and a post-trained derivative would normally retain it, but inheritance is an inference, not a specification. This is scored at the **floor of the methodology's "+image in" band (60–70)** to reflect a capability that almost certainly exists and is entirely undemonstrated — it is not scored as text-only because that would misrepresent the base lineage, and not higher because nothing substantiates it. **Any reader who needs vision must test the live API first.** No audio, video or non-text output is documented or implied.
- **Coding: 93/100.** The dimension where the evidence is both abundant and exceptional. **SWE-bench Verified at 92.2% across a full 500-case run is the highest such figure in this research pass**, retaining 99% of Kimi K3-max's 93.2% for 15.5% fewer tokens. **DeepSWE 1.1 at 75.2% clears the methodology's 74%+ frontier reference and beats its own base model's max setting by 8.8 points** while costing $126.90 less on the run — a post-trained derivative outperforming its base on agentic software engineering is a substantive result, not a rounding artefact. **Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 82.0% also beats K3-max.** Crucially, the validation extends past benchmarks: **two customers' live production coding workloads showed quality held exactly (0.753 vs 0.751) on fewer steps with 39% fewer tokens**, downstream completion and failure metrics held or improved, one customer has moved to production with plans to replace the base model, and Fireworks' own developers **did not notice the switch** on everyday internal traffic. Deductions: **SWE-Interact at 20.0% is below K3-max**, and **LiveCodeBench, SciCode and Vibe Code Bench are absent**, so some band references cannot be checked.
- **Cost efficiency: 72/100.** The honest position is more nuanced than the marketing. **The rate card is identical to Kimi K3's — $3 / $0.30 cached / $15 — so there is no discount on any pricing page**, and the entire saving lives in token volume. Fireworks deserves credit for quantifying that volume rigorously rather than asserting it: **per-benchmark token reductions of 15.5% to 51.9% with absolute savings of $23 to $127 per run**, a **39% total and 71.3% reasoning-token reduction measured in live customer production**, and **a new cost-per-task Pareto frontier on Bedside Bench ahead of GPT-5.6 Sol, GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5**. That is real money on output-heavy agentic coding. Four deductions hold it below the $3/$15 anchor's natural ~60-plus-savings reading. The savings are **workload-dependent and sometimes negligible** — τ²-bench Airline saved only 5.9% and $0.30. They **shrink toward zero on input-heavy workloads**, because reasoning is billed as output. They are **unverifiable from any published rate**, so a buyer must measure their own traffic. And most seriously, this is a **Research Preview with a two-week complimentary window and no permanence guarantee** — building a cost model on a model that may be withdrawn is itself a cost.
- **Overall Score: 81/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dimensions (80 + 78 + 94 + 60 + 93) / 5 = 81 — a narrow, excellent, and institutionally fragile tool. Choose it for exactly one thing and it is very good at it: **high-volume agentic coding where reasoning tokens dominate the bill.** The evidence there is the strongest in this dataset — SWE-bench Verified 92.2% on 500 cases, DeepSWE 1.1 75.2% beating its own base model by 8.8 points, Terminal-Bench 2.1 82.0% also beating it, two live customer A/B tests with quality flat and 39% fewer tokens, and an invisible internal rollout. The enterprise training support is a genuine differentiator if you want to push the same trick further on your own workload. Do not choose it for **general reasoning** — not one standard reasoning benchmark has been published for a model whose intervention is cutting reasoning, which is precisely the wrong gap to have. Do not choose it for **vision** without testing first; Fireworks documents no modalities at all. Do not choose it for **interactive agency** (SWE-Interact 20.0%, below its own base) or for tool-use breadth, where five of six methodology references are unreported. And do not build on it without a fallback: it is a **Research Preview whose continued existence depends on community demand**, so the base Kimi K3 — same price, one point better on SWE-bench Verified, nine points worse on DeepSWE — must stay in your routing as the escape hatch.
