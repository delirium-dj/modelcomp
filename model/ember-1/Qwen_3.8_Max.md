## Model card

- **Name:** Ember 1 (Ember-1)
- **Short description:** **Fireworks AI's efficiency post-train of Moonshot's Kimi K3** (released 2026-09-23, the first model from the Fireworks Research group) — pitched as *"the first AI model trained to think less."* It is **not** a smaller or different architecture and **not** a cheaper rate: it is the **same K3**, retrained to distinguish *useful* reasoning (self-correction, checking assumptions, error recovery) from *useless* reasoning (redundant loops, re-verifying already-confirmed facts), delivering **"same answers, ~40% fewer tokens."** On Fireworks' published tables it is **frontier-competitive at agentic coding** — **DeepSWE 1.1 75.2%** (vs K3-Max 66.4%, and above Claude Opus 5's 74.0% / Gemini 3.8 Flash's 73.7%), **Terminal-Bench 2.1 82.0%** (vs K3-Max 80.9%) — with one disclosed regression, **SWE-bench Verified 92.2%** (vs K3-Max 93.2%). In a live production A/B it cut a coding agent from **49,300 tokens / 23.8 steps (K3) to 29,900 / 21.4** at **flat quality (0.753 vs 0.751)**, with reasoning tokens **−71.3%** and total spend **−~39%**. Trade-offs: **every figure is Fireworks vendor-run — no independent replication exists yet**; it is a **research preview with a two-week serverless usage clock** and adoption-dependent availability; and the tuning is **narrow** (outside agentic coding, base K3 is the safer bet).
- **Provider / access:** **Fireworks AI** (Fireworks Research), built on **Moonshot AI's Kimi K3**. **Research preview** on Fireworks serverless via an **OpenAI-compatible endpoint** (one-line base-URL swap); writeups note a **two-week access window** after which continued availability depends on adoption. **Paid only** (`noFreeId`). Listed in this dataset as `opencode/ember-1`.
- **Release / knowledge:** Released **2026-09-23** (coverage through 2026-09-28). Knowledge cutoff inherits Kimi K3's and is not separately stated. Training effort: **>50 experiments, >200 evaluations**, new reasoning-shortening algorithms, trained across a broad task mix so the savings transfer beyond coding.
- **IDs:** `ember-1` / `opencode/ember-1` (base: Moonshot `kimi-k3`; Fireworks positions it as the first in a series of specialized efficiency models).
- **Context window:** **1,048,576 (1M) tokens total / 131,072 (131K) max output** — inherited from Kimi K3, top-tier on both axes. The efficiency win compounds here: in multi-turn agent sessions the full history (including every prior reasoning trace) is re-sent each call, so cost grows ~quadratically — trimming reasoning tokens attacks the dominant cost driver.
- **Modalities:** **Text + image in → text out** (image input inherited from K3; text-only output). ⚠ **No multimodal/vision benchmark was published** — this is an agentic-coding specialist, and vision is incidental rather than a marketed capability.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-23):** **$3.00 / $15.00 per 1M in/out; $0.30 cached input.** The **per-token rate is the same as K3** — Fireworks' point is that the **per-task cost falls ~39%** because Ember-1 finishes in fewer tokens and fewer steps, not because the rate card is cheaper. (Illustrative A/B savings: the 1-point SWE-bench Verified dip is worth ~$68.10 per 500-task run; the DeepSWE win saved ~$126.90 across 113 tasks.)
- **Architecture:** Undisclosed fine-tune on top of **Kimi K3's** architecture (MoE; unchanged shape). The differentiator is a **training objective** (reasoning-efficiency) rather than a settings dial — Fireworks found that simply lowering K3's reasoning-effort setting gave up too much quality (it skipped *useful* thinking like self-correction), so they trained the waste out instead. Validated three ways: external benchmarks (at/near the quality-efficiency frontier vs all three K3 effort tiers), live customer A/B on production coding traffic, and a **silent internal rollout** (Fireworks switched its own engineers without telling them; nobody noticed while token use dropped).

### Raw benchmarks found

Agentic coding (Fireworks **vendor-run** published tables; **no independent replication yet**):

- **DeepSWE 1.1 (long-horizon SWE, 113 original tasks): 75.2%** — vs K3-Max **66.4%** (an **8.8-point win**); for cross-vendor context this is above Claude Opus 5's 74.0% and Gemini 3.8 Flash's 73.7% (different harness runs — not directly comparable)
- **Terminal-Bench 2.1 (agentic terminal): 82.0%** — vs K3-Max **80.9%** (Ember-1 leads)
- **SWE-bench Verified: 92.2%** — vs K3-Max **93.2%** (⚠ the one honest **regression**, −1.0 pt; elite tier regardless)
- **SWE-Interact:** trails K3-Max slightly (per Fireworks' seven-benchmark table; absolute figure not transcribed)

Efficiency / production telemetry (Fireworks **vendor-run** live A/B + silent rollout):

- **Output tokens per task: 29,900 (Ember-1) vs 49,300 (K3)** across **21.4 vs 23.8 agent steps**
- **Quality score: 0.753 (Ember-1) vs 0.751 (K3)** — effectively flat on production coding traffic
- **Reasoning tokens: −71.3%; total token spend: −~39%** at the **same per-token price**

> Provenance: **All Ember-1 numbers are Fireworks vendor-run** — the launch announcement, its published benchmark tables, and its own live customer A/B / silent-internal-rollout telemetry, transcribed by the dev.to comparison "Ember-1 vs Kimi K3: The First AI Model Trained to Think Less" (2026-09-28) and corroborated by MarkTechPost / LinkedIn / Cline coverage (2026-09-28). The dev.to author explicitly discloses *"I have not run Ember-1 on my own agent infrastructure … everything here comes from Fireworks' announcement, its published benchmark tables, and coverage of the live A/B test results,"* and that **independent replication does not exist yet**. Per methodology these cells are treated as an **upper bound and discounted**. The evidence *design* (live production traffic + a silent rollout) is harder to fake than a synthetic benchmark run, and the SWE-bench dip is **disclosed rather than hidden**, which raises trust — but it remains a single vendor, single base model, preview release. The meta.json claims (SWE-bench 92.2%, DeepSWE 75.2%, ~40% fewer tokens, 1M/131K context, $3/$15, text+image→text) are **independently confirmed** against the coverage rather than inherited verbatim. No benchmark was invented; where an absolute figure was not transcribed (SWE-Interact) I state the direction only.

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 87/100.** Genuinely strong agentic tool use — **Terminal-Bench 2.1 82.0%** (leads K3-Max), **DeepSWE 1.1 75.2%** (an 8.8-point win over K3-Max, frontier-competitive), and **fewer agent steps** (21.4 vs 23.8) with the model checking in / recovering rather than looping. The intriguing finding is that trimming reasoning *improved* agentic results, implying some long traces were actively harmful. Held below the low-90s because the numbers are **vendor-only**, general (non-coding) tool use is unbenchmarked, and there is no independent replication.
- **Reasoning: 85/100.** Inherits **Kimi K3's** strong reasoning but is deliberately trained to *think less*; the production evidence shows quality is **flat (0.753 vs 0.751)** while reasoning tokens fall 71.3%, i.e. it preserves self-correction and drops redundant loops. Capped because Ember-1's reasoning is evidenced **only through agentic-coding quality** — no independent GPQA/HLE/math figure was published for the fine-tune specifically — so the general-reasoning ceiling is assumed-from-K3, not verified-for-Ember.
- **Context window: 88/100.** **1M native context with a 131K output cap** (inherited from K3), top-tier on both axes. The efficiency training is especially valuable *because* of the 1M agent setting — multi-turn sessions re-send the whole history (quadratic compounding), so −71.3% reasoning tokens materially deflates deep-context cost. Scored just below the very top because no independent long-context-recall figure was published.
- **Multimodal: 70/100.** Documented **text + image input** (vision inherited from K3) but **text-only output**, and — decisively — **no multimodal benchmark of any kind** was published; Ember-1 is marketed purely as an agentic-coding efficiency model, so vision is incidental and unverified. Scored above a text/code-only specialist (e.g. a gated cyber model with no vision at ~52) because image input genuinely exists, but well below benchmarked multimodal models (qwen-3.8-flash at 78 had a vendor MathVision figure; the Gemini multimodal leaders reach 85+).
- **Coding: 89/100.** The standout — **elite, frontier-competitive coding**: **SWE-bench Verified 92.2%** (top tier, only 1 pt under K3-Max's 93.2%), **DeepSWE 1.1 75.2%** (beats K3-Max and sits above Opus 5's 74.0% / Gemini 3.8 Flash's 73.7% on that long-horizon harness), **Terminal-Bench 2.1 82.0%**. Discounted from the low-90s because **every coding cell is Fireworks vendor-run** with no neutral-harness replication, it is a **preview** with a usage clock, the SWE-bench Verified / SWE-Interact rows are slight regressions vs K3-Max, and the tuning is narrow (base K3 is recommended outside agentic coding).
- **Cost efficiency: 87/100.** The entire thesis, and it is well-evidenced: **~39% lower per-task cost** (−71.3% reasoning tokens, −~40% total) **at flat quality and the same per-token rate**, validated on live production traffic plus a silent internal rollout. Tempered because the **absolute rate is premium** ($3/$15 — ~19× qwen-3.8-flash's $0.16/$0.47), the savings are per-task/efficiency-driven rather than rate-driven, and the figures are vendor-measured. Scored but **not** counted in the Overall per methodology.
- **Overall Score: 84/100.** Mean of Tool 87 + Reasoning 85 + Context 88 + Multimodal 70 + Coding 89 = 419/5 = 83.8 → 84. Best fit: **high-volume agentic-coding workloads already on Kimi K3** — long multi-turn coding agents where reasoning-token compounding dominates the bill and a ~39% per-task saving at flat quality is worth a one-line base-URL swap; it is **not** a general-purpose or multimodal model, and teams whose pipelines live or die on the last SWE-bench Verified point should A/B that category first. Independent read: my **84 sits below the queue's curated 86.7** — the gap is driven by the **vendor-only, independently-unreplicated** benchmarks, the **research-preview availability risk** (two-week clock, adoption-dependent), the **narrow tuning**, and the modest **SWE-bench Verified regression**, weighed against genuinely frontier-competitive DeepSWE/Terminal-Bench results and a real, well-instrumented efficiency innovation. Internally consistent: Ember-1 (84) sits just below Muse Wonder 1.0 (85, a broader frontier agent with independent AA Index + full multimodal input) and level with qwen-3.8-flash (84), while its Coding (89) is the highest of the three — an accurate portrait of a coding/efficiency specialist whose general-composite mean is pulled down by unbenchmarked multimodality and vendor-only evidence.
