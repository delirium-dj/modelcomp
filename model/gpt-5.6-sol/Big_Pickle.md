- Source: OpenAI's GPT-5.6 launch post and API model documentation, plus independent figures from Vals AI, Artificial Analysis (Index v4.3, 2026-09-07), BenchLM, mungomash, and the Kimi K3 and DeepSeek V4.1-Flash vendor comparison tables
- Date: 2026-09-28 (UTC)
- Overview and scoring methodology: `../../model-comparison.md`
- Cross-model signed log: `../../model-findings.md`

## Model card

- **Name:** GPT-5.6 Sol, released **2026-07-09** for general availability following a limited preview. Roughly **eleven weeks old** and the current flagship of a three-tier family: **Sol** (flagship), **Terra** (balanced, everyday work) and **Luna** (most cost-efficient).
- **Short description:** OpenAI positions Sol as achieving "state-of-the-art results across coding, knowledge work, cybersecurity, and science while outperforming previous and competing frontier models **with fewer tokens and at lower estimated cost**," concluding that "the result is stronger performance per dollar: more successful work for the same spend, or comparable results at a lower total cost." It corresponds to the unsuffixed flagship tier of earlier GPT-5 families, and the `gpt-5.6` alias routes to it. Note what the launch claim does *not* say: OpenAI makes no multimodal leadership claim.
- **Provider / access:** OpenAI first-party, plus Microsoft Azure AI (`azure/gpt-5.6-sol@swedencentral`), Amazon Bedrock, Vercel AI Gateway, Requesty and others. Bedrock mirrored OpenAI's first-party price changes for the family.
- **Release / knowledge:** released 2026-07-09; **knowledge cutoff February 16, 2026** (first-party, from OpenAI's API docs). Roughly seven months stale — fresher than Claude Opus 4.8's January 2026 but behind DeepSeek V4.1-Flash and Kimi K3.
- **Context window:** **1,050,000 tokens input, 128,000 max output** — tied with GPT-6 Astra for the largest window in this dataset, and confirmed by Azure's 1.1M listing.
- **Reasoning controls:** **`reasoning.effort` supports none, low, medium (default), high, xhigh and max** — six levels, the most granular control surface of any model here. Reasoning token support is native.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-09-28), per 1M — and this is where the report's one serious criticism lives:**
  - **List: $5.00 input / $0.50 cached / $30.00 output**
  - **Promotional: $4.00 input / $20.00 output — "a 20% reduction in input pricing and a 33% reduction in output pricing," available "at least through November 21, 2026"**
  - **Prompts with more than 272K input tokens are priced at 2× input and 1.5× output** — the same long-context multiplier as GPT-6 Astra
  - Service tiers: **Quick tokens are priced at 2× input**
  - Siblings after the 2026-07-30 repricing: **Terra $2.00/$12.00** (−20%), **Luna $0.20/$1.20** (−80%). **"Sol stayed at its launch rate."**
  - Resellers: aimlapi $6.50/$39.00, Requesty/Azure $5.50/$33.00 — both above first-party
- **Capabilities:** vision, reasoning, tool calling, caching, web search, JSON schema, computer use, image generation (5 of 8 on Azure's classification).
- **Architecture:** undisclosed parameters, proprietary, closed weights.

### Raw benchmarks found

**Independent composites:**
- **ARC-AGI-2 92.5% — the highest ARC-AGI-2 score of any model in this dataset**, leading Claude Fable 5.1 at 90.0 and DeepSeek V4-Pro at 61.3 (mungomash)
- **GPQA 94.6% (94.1)**, **HLE 49.5**, **SWE-bench Pro 64.6%** (mungomash)
- **Vals SWE-bench 96.20%, #3 of 88** — behind only Claude Opus 5 (97.00%) and DeepSeek V4 Pro (96.40%). **Vals Index overall 81.78**, the highest composite in that listing apart from Claude Opus 5's 82.13 (DeepSeek V4 Pro 61.29)
- **Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3: 47** (2026-09-07) — sixth, behind Claude Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra (53 each), Claude Opus 5 (51), Claude Fable 5 (50) and Muse Spark 1.3 (48), and ahead of Qwen3.8 Max (45) and every open-weights model
- Siblings on Vals SWE-bench: **GPT-5.6 Terra 95.40%**, **GPT-5.6 Luna 93.00%** — both well behind Sol

**Moonshot's Kimi K3 launch comparison, Sol column:** **Terminal Bench 2.1 88.8 (#1, ahead of Kimi K3's 88.3)**, Program Bench 77.6, SWE Marathon 39, BrowseComp 90.4, SpreadsheetBench 2 32.4, Automation Bench 29.7. **Moonshot's own footnote: "GPT-5.6 Sol results include potential cyberguards."**

**DeepSeek's V4.1-Flash model-card table, Sol column:** **Terminal-Bench 3.0 34.4**, **Terminal-Bench 4.0 39.9**, HLE 44.5, **ProgramBench 23.0**, NL2Repo-Bench 56.8, GPQA 94.1, **SEC-Bench Pro 74.3**.

Current TB 3.0 / TB 4.0 standings assembled across sources: Opus-5.0 43.3/51.8, **Sol 34.4/39.9**, Fable 5.1 34.0/—, DeepSeek V4.1-Flash 30.0/31.2, Opus 4.8 21.1/—.

**Not published for Sol:** SWE-bench Verified (vendor), Codeforces, LiveCodeBench, deepSwe, FrontierSWE, MMMU-Pro, Video-MMMU, MathVision, CharXiv, ScreenSpot, BrowseComp (independent), OSWorld, τ²-bench, MCP Atlas, Toolathlon, and any independent long-context retrieval measurement.

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 88/100.** Up 8 — the largest tool-use revision in this batch, and the prior pass was clearly too pessimistic. **Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 88.8% is #1 in Moonshot's field, ahead of Kimi K3's 88.3**, and Sol is **#2 on both harder terminal versions: Terminal-Bench 3.0 34.4% and Terminal-Bench 4.0 39.9%**, behind only Opus-5.0. **SEC-Bench Pro 74.3%** leads DeepSeek's own comparison table, ahead of DeepSeek V4.1-Flash's 62.8%. **BrowseComp 90.4%**, **SpreadsheetBench 2 32.4%**, **Automation Bench 29.7%**, **SWE Marathon 39%**. Held at 88 rather than higher for one reason that matters: **Moonshot's own footnote states that Sol's results "include potential cyberguards."** If the model declines, filters or is otherwise constrained on security-adjacent work, then SWE Marathon and SEC-Bench Pro are not measuring the same thing as the rest of the field — which is awkward, because OpenAI's launch copy claims state-of-the-art results "across coding, knowledge work, **cybersecurity**, and science." Also absent: any independent BrowseComp, OSWorld, τ²-bench or MCP Atlas figure.
- **Reasoning: 92/100.** Up 6. **ARC-AGI-2 at 92.5% is the highest in this dataset** and is a real frontier-reasoning result rather than a saturated one — 32 points above DeepSeek V4-Pro's 61.3. **GPQA 94.6%** is likewise near the top, behind only Gemini 3.8 Flash's 95.3 and Muse Spark 1.3's 94.1's neighbourhood. **HLE 49.5%**, and **Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3 at 47**. Held at 92 rather than higher on two grounds: the **February 16, 2026 knowledge cutoff** is about seven months stale, and **on the current independent composite Sol ranks sixth**, behind its own sibling GPT-6 Astra (53) — a two-generation jump in six weeks. Note also that **HLE is reported as both 49.5 and 44.5** by two sources, an 5-point harness spread on a benchmark where the field is tightly packed.
- **Context window: 90/100.** Up 2. **1,050,000 input / 128,000 max output**, tied with GPT-6 Astra for the largest window here and confirmed by Azure's 1.1M listing. Held at 90 for a commercial reason that belongs in a context score: **prompts above 272K input tokens are priced at 2× input and 1.5× output**, so the marginal token inside the window costs more than the headline implies, and the useful part of the window is the one billed at standard rates. **No independent long-context retrieval measurement is published for Sol** — no AA-LCR, no MRCR 1M — so 1.05M is a capacity claim here, not a measured one.
- **Multimodal: 84/100.** Up 2. Vision is listed among the model's capabilities on Azure. Held at 84, and this is the report's thinnest evidence base of any model in this batch: **no MMMU-Pro, Video-MMMU, MathVision, CharXiv or ScreenSpot figure is published for Sol in any source available**, and OpenAI's own launch post makes no multimodal claim at all — its stated domains are coding, knowledge work, cybersecurity and science. A 1.05M window with vision input and no published vision benchmark is an unusual position for a flagship, and it is scored conservatively rather than charitably.
- **Coding: 86/100.** Up 6. **Vals SWE-bench at 96.20% (#3 of 88)** places Sol within 0.8 points of Claude Opus 5 and 0.2 of DeepSeek V4 Pro, and its **Vals Index overall of 81.78** is the second-highest composite in that listing. **Terminal-Bench 2.1 88.8% (#1)**, **Terminal-Bench 3.0 34.4% and 4.0 39.9% (#2 on both)**, **SWE-bench Pro 64.6%**, **SEC-Bench Pro 74.3%**. Held at 86 on a specific, unresolved conflict: **ProgramBench is reported as 77.6% on Moonshot's chart and 23.0% on DeepSeek's** — a **54-point gap on the same benchmark name**, which can only be a harness difference and which makes the number useless for comparison in either direction. **NL2Repo-Bench 56.8% also loses to DeepSeek V4.1-Flash's 64.0%**, a smaller model. And there is **no vendor SWE-bench Verified figure, no Codeforces, no LiveCodeBench and no deepSwe**, so the competitive-coding and repository-scale measures that differentiate the leaders here are simply unpublished for Sol.
- **Cost efficiency: 45/100.** Down 17 — the largest cost decline in this batch, and the one place where this model is genuinely weak. **$5.00 input / $30.00 output at list is the second-highest output rate in this dataset**, behind only Claude Fable 5 at $50, and **2.4× Kimi K3's $15 and 8× Gemini 3.8 Flash's $3.75.** The **2× input / 1.5× output multiplier above 272K input tokens** compounds it exactly where a 1M-window model is most useful. OpenAI's own **2026-07-30 repricing makes the point against itself: Terra was cut 20% and Luna 80%, while "Sol stayed at its launch rate"** — the flagship that the company says delivers "stronger performance per dollar" was the one tier not repriced. And the **promotional $4/$20 rate is explicitly temporary, "available at least through November 21, 2026,"** after which the effective price rises 25% on input and 50% on output. Held at 45 rather than lower solely because OpenAI's token-efficiency claim ("outperforming... with fewer tokens") may be real and is not quantified in any source available — if Sol genuinely completes work in fewer output tokens, the per-task cost could be competitive even at $30/M. That claim should be tested on the user's own workloads before accepting it.
- **Overall Score: 88.0/100.** Half-up mean of the five quality dims: (88 + 92 + 90 + 84 + 86) / 5 = 88.0. Best fit: **the strongest raw-reasoning model in this dataset, at a price that only makes sense when the reasoning is the product.** Sol holds the highest **ARC-AGI-2 score anywhere here at 92.5%**, the highest **GPQA in the Claude/OpenAI cohort at 94.6%**, **#1 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 88.8%** and **#2 on both Terminal-Bench 3.0 and 4.0**, and **#3 on Vals SWE-bench at 96.20%** with the second-highest Vals composite at 81.78 — on a **1,050,000-token window**. For frontier abstract reasoning, difficult mathematics, scientific work or long-horizon agentic coding where a failed run is expensive, it is very hard to argue against. For everything else the arithmetic is brutal: **$30/M output, rising 50% when the promotional rate expires in November, with a 1.5× multiplier past 272K input.** Against that, **Claude Opus 5 leads Vals SWE-bench at 97.00% and scores 51 on AA Intelligence Index v4.3 versus Sol's 47**, and **GPT-6 Astra — OpenAI's own successor, six weeks newer — scores 53** — at **$10.00 input / $50.00 output, 2× Sol's input and 1.67× its output**, which is the basis for Artificial Analysis's "75% more expensive than GPT-5.6 Sol at max effort."

- **The finding that governs this report:** **GPT-5.6 Sol is already superseded by its own successor, and the evidence is unusually clear.** Sol launched 2026-07-09. **GPT-6 Astra arrived six weeks later on 2026-08-24 and scores 53 on Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3 against Sol's 47** — while Sol still scores well on the older, individually-named benchmarks (ARC-AGI-2 92.5%, GPQA 94.6%) that were current at its launch and have since been overtaken. Combined with **$30/M output and a promotional rate that expires in November 2026**, the practical conclusion is that Sol's remaining value is specific: it is where the independently measured ARC-AGI-2 frontier currently sits, and for tasks that are *only* about frontier abstract reasoning, nothing else in this dataset beats it. For general work, **Claude Opus 5 leads Vals SWE-bench at 97.00% against Sol's 96.20% and scores 51 versus 47 on AA v4.3, at $25/M output rather than $30** — cheaper and better on the independent measures.
- **Conflicts recorded rather than smoothed over:** (1) **OpenAI's own documentation contains two prices for the same model** — the pricing table says **$5.00 input / $0.50 cached / $30.00 output**, and the same page says **"GPT-5.6 Sol costs $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens, a 20% reduction in input pricing and a 33% reduction in output pricing," available "at least through November 21, 2026."** These are list and promotional rates and are reported as such, with the expiry date stated, because a cost model built on $4/$20 breaks in November. (4) **fast-mode pricing is ambiguous** — the API page renders a "Fast Price $5•$30" row that appears to restate the standard rate, which would be unusual for a fast tier; not resolved here. (5) **reseller rates exceed first-party** (aimlapi $6.50/$39.00, Requesty/Azure $5.50/$33.00 against OpenAI's $5.00/$30.00). (6) **The cyberguards footnote is Moonshot's, not OpenAI's** — it materially affects the comparability of Sol's SWE Marathon and SEC-Bench Pro results, and it sits awkwardly against OpenAI's own cybersecurity leadership claim.
