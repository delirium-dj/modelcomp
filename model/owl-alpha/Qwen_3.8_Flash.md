## Model card

- **Name:** Owl Alpha (listed as `openrouter/owl-alpha`; curated id `opencode/owl-alpha`)
- **Short description:** An **anonymous "stealth" frontier-class model served through OpenRouter with no lab publicly attached to it**, tuned for agentic workloads — tool use, long-context tasks, code generation and multi-step instruction following. It appeared with minimal metadata ("no org page, no model card narrative, no press cycle"), was briefly **free**, and climbed the OpenRouter usage charts rapidly on agent traffic before being repriced. Third-party write-ups describe it as "advertises a roughly 1 million-token context window" and note the classic stealth trade-off: the free tier **logged prompts and completions** (almost certainly to train the model) and served at only **~12 tokens/s**.
- **Provider / access:** **Unknown publisher.** OpenRouter is the *distributor*, not the lab. Community attribution — pushed hardest by an unofficial LongCat fan site — holds that Owl Alpha was the **preview of Meituan's LongCat-2.0** ("LongCat team confirms Owl Alpha = LongCat-2.0 preview", with "1.6T MoE, 1M context, domestic-chip end-to-end training, SWE-bench Pro 59.5" added to the record after the reveal, and open weights + official pricing following in **June 2026**). A different write-up is explicit that "**nobody has confirmed it … treat that as an educated guess, not a fact**", citing precedent (Polaris Alpha → GPT-5.1, Sherlock Alpha → Grok 4.1). **No model card, technical report, licence or data-handling policy exists for this ID**, so provenance, weights status and terms of use are all unverifiable.
- **Release / knowledge:** OpenRouter listing surfaces around **2026-04-28**, with the stealth window running roughly **8 weeks** (community coverage dated 2026-07-05 still describes it as unconfirmed). **No knowledge cutoff published.** The `Owl Alpha` name is reported as **retired after the reveal** in favour of LongCat-2.0, which means this folder may describe an ephemeral preview checkpoint that no longer exists as a routable model — the OpenRouter product page now returns **404** to this researcher's fetches.
- **IDs:** `openrouter/owl-alpha` (OpenRouter), `opencode/owl-alpha` (curated). **Aggregator coverage flag — none of the independent evaluators carry this ID:** **BenchLM 404** for `owl-alpha`; **Artificial Analysis 404** for `owl-alpha`; BenchLM *does* track the claimed revealed identity (`benchlm.ai/models/longcat-2-0`, Meituan, Open Weight, Reasoning type, **1M context**) but with **"0 of 625" benchmarks covered, Overall "Coming soon", Unranked**. Net effect: **there is no third-party benchmark measurement for Owl Alpha, and none yet for LongCat-2.0 either.**
- **Context window:** **Three-way conflict, unresolved.** Curated `meta.json` says **65,536 total (16,384 out)**. Two independent third-party descriptions of the *OpenRouter listing* say it advertised **~1M tokens** ("roughly 1 million-token context window"; "Given Owl Alpha's 1M context and agentic tuning"), and the post-reveal LongCat-2.0 record also lists **1M**. Nothing published reconciles the 65 K figure — plausible readings are (a) the curated file captured a reduced metadata value from the stealth endpoint (stealth routes often serve a shortened window), or (b) the listing advertised 1M without delivering it. **This report scores the conservative middle and flags the conflict** rather than assuming the vendor-grade 1M.
- **Modalities:** **text in / text out** per the curated file and every description found — the tool-use/long-context/code framing never mentions image, audio or video input, and no non-text output is claimed. (Unverifiable against a model card, because none exists.)
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-08):** curated **$0.50 / $2.00 per 1M input/output** (`noFreeId: true`). The documented history contradicts the "no free" flag in spirit: the listing launched at **$0 per 1M tokens** (the catch being prompt logging and ~12 tok/s) and was later moved to paid — "As of the latest updates, Owl Alpha is no longer free on OpenRouter". Post-reveal, the same unofficial source reports **$0.75/$2.95 standard with a $0.30/$1.20 promo** for the LongCat-2.0 API. All three figures are inconsistent; none is confirmable from a first-party price sheet.
- **Architecture:** **undisclosed.** The only concrete claim is post-reveal and second-hand: 1.6 T-parameter MoE trained end-to-end on domestic (Chinese) chips. No parameter count, active-parameter figure, licence or weight download can be attributed to the `owl-alpha` ID itself.
- **Identity flags:** (1) **Possible duplicate-object registration:** if the LongCat attribution is correct, this folder and **`model/longcat-2.0/`** describe the *same underlying model* at two lifecycle stages (stealth preview vs named release). That is not a rule-7 hyphen/dotted slug duplicate — the two slugs are legitimately different IDs — but the orchestrator should decide whether to keep both folders and how to cross-reference them. (2) **Ephemeral ID:** the name is reported retired; scores here may belong to a checkpoint that is no longer reachable. (3) **Consent/privacy flag:** the free tier logged prompts and completions — worth surfacing on the site, since `noFreeId: true` currently implies no free option existed. (4) **Scaffold-adjacent metadata:** the curated `short` reads like marketing copy and every substantive field (context, price, free-tier status) conflicts with third-party reporting.

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 45/100.** The listing text promises native tool use and the *adoption* data is striking — top-ranked routed volume across three independent agent frameworks and trillion-scale monthly tokens is what you see when an agent harness can actually drive a model. But adoption is not measurement: **τ²/τ³-bench, BFCL, Toolathlon, Claw-Eval, AutomationBench, OSWorld and GDPval have no row for this ID anywhere**, there is no model card to describe its tool protocol, and the community usage figures come from a fan-adjacent site citing "public reports and community analytics". Scored below the mid band purely on the total absence of harness evidence, per the methodology's "no verified score → note and penalty, never invent".
- **Reasoning: 40/100.** Nothing. No Artificial Analysis page, no BenchLM score, no published index, no GPQA/HLE/MMLU-Pro/AIME row, no vendor technical report — and the curated description ("fast reasoning workflows") is marketing, not measurement. A model that attracts heavy agent traffic is unlikely to be weak, which is why this is not scored at the floor, but an inference from popularity is exactly the kind of thing this registry's scoring contract forbids recording as a number.
- **Context window: 55/100.** Deliberately split between two irreconcilable claims: curated **65,536 (16,384 out)** — which alone would sit in the <100 K band (10–49) with a sub-64 K output ceiling — and the **~1 M advertised on the OpenRouter listing** (two third-party write-ups, plus 1 M on BenchLM's LongCat-2.0 row), which would sit at 95–100. Since the window is *advertised* rather than *demonstrated* — no MRCR/AI-Needle retrieval, no long-context harness row, and a documented history of stealth endpoints serving reduced windows — 55 is the honest middle: better than trusting the curated metadata blindly, far short of crediting an unverified 1 M claim. **Resolving this is the single highest-value follow-up for the folder.**
- **Multimodal: 15/100.** **Text-only** on the curated record and on every description found; no image/audio/video input is claimed, and there is no model card that would let anyone check. 10–20 band, low end, because for a stealth model even the "text-only" fact is an absence of counter-evidence rather than a verified spec.
- **Coding: 40/100.** Code generation is explicitly part of the positioning and the Whisper-network reports describe "strong multi-step SWE-style completion, stable tool calls" — but the **only coding number in the entire trail is `SWE-bench Pro 59.5`, published post-reveal by an unofficial LongCat site about a different ID**, and BenchLM's LongCat-2.0 page carries **0 of 625** benchmarks. No SWE-bench Verified, LiveCodeBench, DeepSWE, SciCode or Coding-Index row exists for `owl-alpha`, so the dimension is scored on positioning with the evidence gap stated.
- **Cost efficiency: 90/100.** **$0.50 / $2.00 per 1M** is genuinely cheap — just under the methodology's $0.60/$2.20 ≈ 92 anchor — and the model's *history* is even cheaper: it launched at **$0** on OpenRouter, which is why volume exploded. Deductions are real, though: the free tier **logged prompts and completions** for training (you paid in data), throughput was around **12 tok/s** (wall-clock cost), a stealth route has **no SLA and can change behaviour, get renamed or vanish without notice**, the ID is reportedly already retired, and the three price points on record ($0 → $0.50/$2.00 → $0.75/$2.95 with $0.30/$1.20 promo) cannot be reconciled.
- **Overall Score: 39/100.** Mean of the five quality dimensions (45 + 40 + 55 + 15 + 40) / 5 = 195 / 5 = 39.0 → 39; Cost excluded per `RULES.md`. **This is an evidence-floor score, not a capability verdict.** Best fit: **non-sensitive agent experimentation**, where a cheap, tool-capable, long-context route is useful and the logging caveat is acceptable — and only with a stable fallback model configured, since the ID may already be gone. It is unfit for production, compliance or factual work by construction: unknown publisher, no licence, no data-handling policy, no SLA. **Recommended action:** once LongCat-2.0 accumulates real harness rows (BenchLM says coverage is "coming soon"), re-search this folder against `model/longcat-2.0/` and decide whether the two entries should cross-reference each other or collapse into one.
