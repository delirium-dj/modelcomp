## Model card

- **Name:** Ling-2.6-1T
- **Short description:** inclusionAI's **trillion-parameter "instant" (instruct) flagship** — the **non-thinking** half of the 2.6-1T pair, "designed for real-world agents that require **fast execution and high efficiency at scale**" ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/inclusionai/ling-2.6-1t)). The positioning is speed rather than depth, and the independent evidence below is consistent with that: it posts an excellent conversational tool-use score and a **91.3% efficiency rating on ClawProBench**, while finishing **last of 64 models on NYT Connections Extended**. Distinct model; **`ring-2.6.1t` is the reasoning counterpart with the `high`/`xhigh` effort knob and has its own folder** — nothing is inherited between them — and `ling-3.0-flash`, `ling-3.1-flash`, `ling-2.6-flash`, `ling-3.0-tiny` and `ling-3.0-flash-sante` are all separate models with their own folders.
- **Provider / access:** **Open weights** on Hugging Face (`inclusionAI/Ling-2.6-1T`) under **MIT**, plus **OpenCode Zen**, OpenRouter (including a **free endpoint**, `inclusionai/ling-2.6-1t:free`) and other third-party routes. [BenchmarkList](https://benchmarklist.com/models/inclusionai-ling-2.6-1t/) classifies access as "Open weights".
- **Release / knowledge:** **2026-04-23**, convergent across BenchmarkList, OpenRouter, Artificial Analysis, Benchable, LLM Reference, Dataconomy and AI Flash Report. Knowledge cutoff: no verified public date found.
- **IDs:** `inclusionAI/Ling-2.6-1T` (Hugging Face), `inclusionai/ling-2.6-1t` and `inclusionai/ling-2.6-1t:free` (OpenRouter), `ling-2.6.1t` (Zen). **A free OpenRouter endpoint exists**; self-hosting is free under MIT.
- **Context window:** **262,144 tokens (262K)**, confirmed independently by OpenRouter, LLM Reference and Gert Labs' own run metadata (which records `context length 262144`). Max output: no verified public figure found.
- **Modalities:** **Text in → text out only.** No image, audio or video. Reasoning: **no — this is the instruct / non-thinking route.** The evidence is direct: BenchmarkList's ObviousBench run records **"Reasoning tokens: 0"** at default settings. inclusionAI's own description is "fast-thinking compressed CoT" rather than explicit extended reasoning. Tool calls: yes, and agentic execution is the stated design target.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-08):** ⚠️ **two materially different published rates, both reported rather than averaged.** BenchmarkList lists **$0.075 / MTok input and $0.625 / MTok output** (and computes a blended $0.70), while Artificial Analysis-derived records and AI Flash Report give **~$0.30 / MTok input and ~$2.50 / MTok output**. That is a ~4× spread, most likely reflecting different provider routes rather than a price change. A **free OpenRouter endpoint** also exists, and ClawProBench recorded its entire evaluation run at **Cost $0**.
- **Architecture:** **1T total / 63B active sparse MoE** with **hybrid MLA plus linear attention** and fast-thinking compressed chain-of-thought. **Openness is partial and quantified**: Artificial Analysis scores it **38.89 on the Openness Index (rank 116 of 204, 43rd percentile)**, with Model Availability 6 and Model Transparency 1 but **Pre-training Data Access 0, Pre-training Data License 0, Post-training Data Access 0, Post-training Data License 0** and Methodology Transparency 1 — open weights, closed data.

### Raw benchmarks found

> **Every figure below is independent.** BenchLM has no page for this model (404 on the machine-readable endpoint), so the evidence base is [BenchmarkList](https://benchmarklist.com/models/inclusionai-ling-2.6-1t/)'s **14-benchmark profile**, sourcing Artificial Analysis, Gert Labs, BLXBench, ClawProBench, ObviousBench and the NYT Connections Extended leaderboard. **I found no inclusionAI-published benchmark table for this model at all** — unusual, and it means there is no vendor framing to discount, only third-party measurement to report.
>
> ⚠️ **One internal conflict must be flagged.** BenchmarkList's headline **Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index is 26.05** (rank 122 of 427, 72nd percentile, dated 2026-07-21 and marked Verified), but the same page lists **33.61** as an Intelligence Index component inside both the Openness Index and GDPval-AA rows, and Dataconomy reports **34**. The most likely reading is that 33.61–34 is an **earlier index version** and 26.05 the current recomputed figure. **I score the live headline 26.05** and record the discrepancy rather than picking the flattering number.

- **Tau2-Bench Telecom: 89.8% success rate — rank 53 of 332, 84th percentile.** The model's best result by a wide margin, on a "dual-control conversational AI benchmark simulating telecom support scenarios where agent and user coordinate actions to resolve service issues."
- **Terminal-Bench Hard: 31.1% — rank 70 of 326, 79th percentile** (Artificial Analysis's hard subset covering terminal software engineering, system administration, game-playing and data processing)
- **GDPval-AA: 1,045 Elo — rank 104 of 352, 71st percentile**
- **ClawProBench: 57.4 final score — rank 23 of 48, 53rd percentile.** The sub-scores are the most informative data in this report: **Efficiency 91.3%**, Planning 70.5%, Error Recovery 65.7%, Tool Use 62.4%, Capability 61.6%, Avg Score 60.7%, **Pass@3 58.8% but Pass³ only 36.5%**, Safety 57.1%, Constraints 57.0%, **Synthesis 47.5%** — over 59.0 million tokens and a 69.93-second average runtime, at a recorded cost of **$0**.
- **Gert Labs Rankings: 25.0% GScore — rank 61 of 62, 2nd percentile.** The worst agentic result: average percentile 34.7%, median 29.6%, gameplay success 50.0% across 43 games and 193,787 matches, with **one-shot coding GScore 25.0%**.

- **SciCode: 37.0% — rank 141 of 296, 53rd percentile** (Verified; field leader O-5.5 at 66.9%)

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 70/100.** The strongest dimension, with a genuinely good headline number: **Tau2-Bench Telecom 89.8% at the 84th percentile of 332 models** means structured, multi-turn, coordinated tool dialogues work well, and **Terminal-Bench Hard 31.1% at the 79th percentile** plus **GDPval-AA 1,045 Elo at the 71st** show the capability is not a single-benchmark artefact. ClawProBench's breakdown explains both the appeal and the limit: **Efficiency 91.3% and Planning 70.5%** against **Synthesis 47.5% and Pass³ 36.5% versus Pass@3 58.8%** — it plans and executes quickly but is not reliably repeatable, and it struggles to combine results. Capped by the one severe outlier: **Gert Labs 25.0%, rank 61 of 62, 2nd percentile** on agentic game environments.
- **Reasoning: 52/100.** **GPQA Diamond 75.2%** is a real strength and sits at the 63rd percentile of 468 models. Everything else is weak, and two results are worth taking seriously rather than dismissing. **NYT Connections Extended at 10.8% — last of 64 — is a pure lateral-reasoning benchmark designed to defeat shortcuts**, and this model cannot do it at all. **ObviousBench 68.8% at the 39th percentile with Format pass³ at 99.3%** is the diagnostic pair: output *shape* is near-perfect while output *correctness* fails roughly a third of the time, which is precisely the failure profile of a fast non-thinking model. Add **AA-Omniscience −51, CritPt 0.3%, HLE 8.7%** and an **Intelligence Index of 26.05**, and the picture is consistent. The fair mitigation: **this is the instruct route with zero reasoning tokens by design** — Ring-2.6-1T is the model to use when depth matters.
- **Context window: 58/100.** **262,144 tokens is a solid window**, independently confirmed in three places including a harness's own run metadata, and the hybrid MLA-plus-linear-attention stack is the mechanism. But measured quality is mediocre — **AA-LCR 41.7% at the 47th percentile** (34.7% in the index breakdown), and **AA-LCR is one of its two largest deficits in head-to-head comparison, −53.1 points behind Kimi K3**. **No retrieval curve at any depth**, and no published max output. Scored on demonstrated long-context quality, not on the nominal number.
- **Multimodal: 15/100.** Text in, text out. No image, audio or video input and none claimed. Template floor.
- **Coding: 58/100.** **BLXBench 75.3% at the 71st percentile of 25** is a decent result on a multi-slice harness covering coding, debugging, refactoring and security, and the accompanying **2.02-second latency and 109.6 tok/s decode** are real practical value. **SciCode 37.0%** is mid-field. Against that: **Gert Labs one-shot coding GScore 25.0%** is poor, and the coverage is thin — **no SWE-bench, LiveCodeBench, SWE-bench Pro, Terminal-Bench 2.1 or DeepSWE figure exists**, so there is no evidence at all about repository-scale engineering. Terminal-Bench Hard 31.1% is the closest proxy and is respectable. Scored at mid-band on two benchmarks and one clear negative.
- **Cost efficiency: 80/100.** Strong on access, weakened by opacity and deployment reality. **MIT open weights** and a **free OpenRouter endpoint** are both genuine, it is **the cheapest model in BenchmarkList's eight-way comparison** at a blended $0.70, ClawProBench ran the entire evaluation at **$0 cost**, and **63B active of 1T** keeps compute per token low. Three deductions. First, **the published price is ambiguous by a factor of four** — $0.075/$0.625 versus ~$0.30/$2.50 — which is a real problem for anyone budgeting. Second, **1T total parameters make self-hosting impractical** for almost everyone regardless of the licence, the same constraint that applies to its reasoning sibling. Third, **Artificial Analysis scores its openness at only 38.89 (43rd percentile)** with zeros across all four data-access and data-licence dimensions — open weights, closed everything else.
- **Overall Score: 50.6/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dims (70 + 52 + 58 + 15 + 58) / 5 = 50.6. This is a **speed-and-efficiency model that should be judged on throughput per dollar, not on depth**, and the independent record supports exactly that reading: 91.3% ClawProBench efficiency, 109.6 tok/s decode, 2.02-second latency, the cheapest entry in its comparison class — alongside 0 wins and 42 losses on capability. Best fit: **high-volume conversational tool-calling and customer-service-style agent flows** where Tau2-Bench-Telecom-shaped work dominates and latency matters, plus straightforward debugging and refactoring via a cheap or free endpoint. Avoid it for anything requiring lateral reasoning (last of 64 on NYT Connections), factual reliability (Omniscience −51), repeatability (Pass³ 36.5% against Pass@3 58.8%) or long-context recall (AA-LCR 41.7%). If you need depth from this family, **use the Ring-2.6-1T reasoning route instead** — that is what the Ling/Ring split exists for.
