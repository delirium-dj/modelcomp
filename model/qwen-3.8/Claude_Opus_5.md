## Model card

- **Name:** Qwen3.8. **Variant/alias flag — this is the single most important fact on this page: "Qwen 3.8" is shorthand for Qwen3.8-Max.** An independent benchmark guide states it directly: *"Qwen 3.8 is shorthand for Qwen3.8-Max, a 2.4-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 95 billion active parameters."* There is no separate Alibaba model called plain "Qwen3.8" — the bare name is how the press and aggregators refer to the Max tier. **This dataset therefore holds two folders for one model: `model/qwen-3.8/` and `model/qwen-3.8-max/`.** The five quality scores below are deliberately **identical** to this dataset's Qwen3.8-Max entry; inventing a differential would fabricate a distinction that does not exist. What this report adds is **primary-source confirmation of the specifications and pricing** that the Max entry had to leave unverified.
- **Short description:** Alibaba's flagship, **released 2026-08-03**. Alibaba Group's own announcement calls it *"the most powerful model in its Qwen series to date"* with **2.4 trillion parameters and a context window up to 1 million tokens**, and records that it **ranks fifth in Text Arena and second in Vision Arena** — the latter a genuinely notable external placement. A dated upgrade snapshot, **Qwen3.8-Max-0902 (alias `qwen3.8-max-2026-09-02`)**, is documented on Alibaba Cloud Model Studio with the note that *"Coding capability breaks new ground, handling more complex engineering-scale projects and long-horizon autonomous development."*
- **Provider / access:** Alibaba Cloud Model Studio (`qwen3.8-max`, with the dated `qwen3.8-max-2026-09-02` snapshot), OpenRouter (`qwen/qwen3.8-max-0902`), and **10 tracked providers** per an independent pricing aggregator — far broader distribution than most models in this dataset. **Reasoning is enabled by default.**
- **Release / knowledge:** **Released 2026-08-03**; upgraded snapshot **2026-09-02**. **Knowledge cutoff: no verified public figure found.**
- **IDs:** `qwen3.8-max` (Alibaba Cloud Model Studio), `qwen3.8-max-2026-09-02` / `qwen3.8-max-0902` (dated snapshot), `qwen/qwen3.8-max-0902` (OpenRouter). A benchmark tracker additionally cites a Hugging Face model card at `Qwen/Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B`.
- **Context window:** **1,000,000 tokens**, confirmed by Alibaba Group's own announcement, OpenRouter and two pricing aggregators. Max output: no verified figure found. Published retrieval evidence: **MRCRv2 92.9%**, **LongBench v2 66.3%**.
- **Modalities:** **Text, image and video input → text output** — stated explicitly by OpenRouter and corroborated by the benchmark set's five video results. **A Vision Arena placement of second globally** is strong external evidence that the vision path is not merely present but competitive. No audio input and no non-text output.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-03):** **$1.65 / 1M input, $0.206 / 1M cached input, $4.95 / 1M output** — now confirmed by an independent model tracker and corroborated by a pricing aggregator listing **10 providers**. This is **well below the $3/$15 anchor**, with a cached-input multiplier of roughly 12.5%.
- **Architecture:** **Sparse Mixture-of-Experts — 2.4 trillion total parameters, 95 billion active per token**, confirmed by Alibaba Group's announcement, OpenRouter and an independent benchmark guide. Expert count, routing topology and layer count: not disclosed. **Licence status is contested:** a benchmark tracker classifies it as **Open Weight** and cites a Hugging Face model card at `Qwen/Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B`, while an independent pricing guide draws **"a distinction between the hosted Max model and Qwen3.8-27B open weights"** — implying Max is hosted-only and that 27B is the open option. **Both readings are recorded; verify the licence directly before any self-hosting or redistribution plan.**

### Normalized scores (1–100)

> **Scoring note, stated plainly:** "Qwen 3.8" and "Qwen3.8-Max" are the same model. The five quality scores below are therefore **identical to this dataset's Qwen3.8-Max entry**, and the reasoning is unchanged. Only the evidential footing differs: this report confirms the pricing, release date, parameter count and modalities from primary and near-primary sources, where the Max entry had to flag them as unverified.

- **Tool use: 83/100.** **AndroidWorld at 85.3% is the best mobile-agency figure in this dataset**, with MobileWorld 77.8%, WideResearch 81.9%, Toolathlon-Verified 72.5% and HLE-with-tools 56.2% filling out a broad profile, and OSWorld-Verified reaching 86.1%. Four deductions: **Terminal-Bench 2.1 drops from 86.6% to 67.4% under Vals AI's independent harness — a 19.2-point gap, the largest in this research pass** — which forces a discount on every unreplicated vendor row; **Qwen's own two computer-use figures differ by ~67 points** (OSWorld-Verified 86.1% against OSWorld 2.0 19.4%); **AutomationBench at 27.3% is weak** on end-to-end business execution; and **GDPval-AA, Tau2-bench, Tau3-Banking, Claw-Eval and MCP Atlas are all unreported** — five of six methodology references with no value, which is striking given that sibling Qwen models publish τ², τ³ and Claw-Eval figures.
- **Reasoning: 88/100.** **GPQA Diamond at 92.6% from Qwen is independently measured higher at 93.7% by Vals AI** — when an independent lab beats the vendor's own claim, confidence should rise — and **HLE at 43.6% clears the 40%+ reference**, reported identically with and without tools. **MRCRv2 at 92.9%** is strong long-context reasoning, **MMLU-Pro is independently confirmed at 88.6%**, and **IFBench at 82.8% is the joint-best instruction-following figure here**. Capped at 88 by an evidence gap rather than weak results: **no Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index value exists for this model**, **no CritPt figure**, and **no hallucination or Omniscience measurement at all** — so general-intelligence standing and factual reliability are both entirely unmeasured, which matters more given the Terminal-Bench discrepancy.
- **Context window: 96/100.** A **1,000,000-token window** confirmed by Alibaba Group's own announcement places it in the ≥1M tier (95–100), and it earns above the floor on **MRCRv2 at 92.9%** — genuine retrieval measurement and among the better figures here — with **LongBench v2 at 66.3%** alongside. It stops short of the ceiling because the tier's 100 condition needs **≥98% retrieval at 512K+** and 92.9% falls short, the measurement length is unspecified, **max output tokens are undocumented**, and no independent replication of the MRCR figure was found.
- **Multimodal: 89/100.** **The most comprehensive multimodal evidence base in this research pass — 24 distinct benchmarks** spanning vision, documents, OCR, video, medical imaging, screen understanding and embodied reasoning, now reinforced by two external confirmations this report adds: **OpenRouter documents text, image and video input explicitly**, and **Alibaba Group records a second-place Vision Arena ranking** — a human-preference placement that no benchmark table can substitute for. The standouts are category-leading: **CharXiv at 88.4% without tools is the best unaided chart-reasoning figure in this dataset**, MathVision 95.2% approaches saturation, OmniDocBench 1.5 reaches 92.1%, and **five separate video benchmarks all land between 81.8% and 90.8%**. That places it at the top of the methodology's "+video/PDF in = 75–90" band. It cannot exceed 90 because **there is no audio input and no non-text output**, and two results temper the picture: **ZeroBench at 24.0%** and PerceptionBench at 63.5% show the hardest perceptual reasoning remains out of reach, with all 24 figures vendor-reported and unreplicated.
- **Coding: 86/100.** The independently measured results carry the score: **SWE-bench at 85.6% and LiveCodeBench at 87.9%, both from Vals AI**, are among the best in this dataset and do not depend on Qwen's harness, with **VulcanBench v3 at 81.2%** adding a third independent datapoint. **SWE-bench Pro at 67.7% beats GPT-5.6 Sol and GPT-5.5**, and PaperBench reaches 93.0%. Three deductions: **DeepSWE at 56.6% is roughly 17 points below the 74%+ frontier reference**; **FrontierSWE collapses from Qwen's 73.5% to 15.8% on Proximal's independent v2 leaderboard** — a ~58-point gap that, even allowing for a harder version, is the starkest reliability warning here; and **SciCode and Vibe Code Bench are absent**, with NL2Repo (55.9%) and MLS-Bench Lite (41.0%) both mid-to-low.
- **Cost efficiency: 86/100.** **This is the one dimension where this report materially improves on the Max entry, because the rate card is now confirmed: $1.65 / 1M input, $0.206 / 1M cached, $4.95 / 1M output**, corroborated across two independent trackers, with **10 tracked providers** giving real routing competition and price discipline. That is **well below the $3/$15 anchor** for a model ranked #16 of 783, with a cached-input multiplier of roughly 12.5%. Four deductions keep it below the high 80s: the **licence is contested** — one tracker classifies it Open Weight with a Hugging Face card, another explicitly distinguishes the hosted Max model from the Qwen3.8-27B open weights — so the self-hosting escape cannot be relied on without direct verification; at **2.4 trillion total parameters self-hosting would be a datacentre project** even if permitted; **no free tier** is documented; and the vendor-figure discounts documented above mean you may need to pay for your own evaluation before trusting the capability you are buying.
- **Overall Score: 88.4/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dimensions (83 + 88 + 96 + 89 + 86) / 5 = 88.4 — **identical to this dataset's Qwen3.8-Max entry, because it is the same model.** The practical guidance follows from that: **there is nothing to choose between `qwen-3.8` and `qwen-3.8-max` — they are one model at one price with one benchmark record.** Within that, it is the strongest multimodal and long-context open-market option in this research pass. Choose it for **document, chart, OCR and video pipelines** (CharXiv 88.4% unaided is the best here; OmniDocBench 1.5 92.1%; five video benchmarks all above 81%; **Vision Arena second globally**), for **mobile and screen automation** (AndroidWorld 85.3%, ScreenSpot Pro 84.5%), for **independently verified coding** (SWE-bench 85.6%, LiveCodeBench 87.9%), and for **long-context work** (MRCRv2 92.9%) — all at a confirmed $1.65/$4.95 across ten providers. Three cautions govern adoption. **Discount the vendor numbers**: Terminal-Bench 2.1 falls 19.2 points under independent testing and FrontierSWE falls ~58 points on its v2 leaderboard, so run your own harness. **The agentic reference set is largely missing** — no GDPval-AA, no τ², no τ³, no Claw-Eval, no MCP Atlas — and AutomationBench at 27.3% is not encouraging. And **general intelligence and factual reliability are entirely unmeasured**: no Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, no CritPt, no hallucination rate.
