## Model card

- **Name:** Qwen3.5 397B. **Open weights.** **Reasoning type: Non-Reasoning** — a notable flag, because much of this model's evidence comes from a *comparison table* in which it is the cheaper baseline against Qwen's later reasoning-tier models. Variant: `base`, with a **Qwen3.5 397B (Reasoning)** sibling tracked but uncomputed. Model card: `Qwen/Qwen3.5-397B-A17B` on Hugging Face, which indicates a **397B total / 17B active** sparse MoE.
- **Short description:** Alibaba's prior-generation open-weight flagship, placed **#71 of 882 at 53.65/100** across a deep **49 of 613 benchmarks** — deeply superseded within its own family, with **eight newer Alibaba models scoring above it**, including Qwen3.8 Max (70.49), Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (64.11) and Qwen3.7 Max (62.61). Its genuine strengths are knowledge and mathematics: **MMLU-Pro at 87.8%**, **MMLU-Redux at 94.9%**, **C-Eval at 93%**, and a strong competition-mathematics battery (**AIME26 93.3%, HMMT Feb 2025 94.8%, HMMT Nov 2025 92.7%, HMMT Feb 2026 87.9%**). **Two findings should govern how it is read.** First, **its vendor-reported multimodal figures are excellent — MathVision 88.6%, VideoMMMU 84.7%, V\* 95.8% — while Artificial Analysis measures AA-MMMU-Pro at just 52.7%, a 26-point divergence that is the largest vendor-independent multimodal gap in this research pass.** Second, reliability is poor: **a −37.9% AA-Omniscience Index on a 24.5% accuracy and an 82.7% hallucination rate.**
- **Provider / access:** Open weights at `Qwen/Qwen3.5-397B-A17B` on Hugging Face. **No licence was stated in the sources read** — a gap worth closing before commercial use, since Alibaba's open-weight releases vary between Apache-2.0 and the more restrictive Qwen Community licence.
- **Release / knowledge:** **No verified release date or knowledge cutoff found.** As a Qwen3.5-generation model it precedes the 3.6, 3.7 and 3.8 generations tracked in this pass.
- **IDs:** `qwen3-5-397b`; sibling `qwen3-5-397b-reasoning`. **No free-tier ID**, though self-hosting is possible if the licence permits.
- **Context window:** **128,000 tokens** (tracker). **AA-LCR at 64.3% and LongBench v2 at 63.2%** are both weak, and **AI-Needle at 68.7%** — a retrieval probe — is mediocre. Max output: no verified figure found. This is modest context for a 397B flagship in the current field.
- **Modalities:** **Text, image and video input → text output.** Image and video comprehension are claimed via the vendor's multimodal comparison table, but **the vendor and independent measurements diverge sharply** (see below), so the modality claim is treated cautiously here. No audio path.
- **Pricing (as of 2026-10-03):** **No rate card was found** — neither a hosted price nor a stated licence permitting free self-hosting. **Cost efficiency below therefore rests on tier positioning and the open-weight status alone, with the licence unresolved.** Re-verify before budgeting.
- **Architecture:** **Open weights, 397B total parameters with 17B active** — a very sparse MoE, which makes self-hosting more tractable than the raw parameter count suggests.

### Normalized scores (1–100)

- **Tool use: 66/100.** Reasonably broad on paper, but built largely on vendor comparison tables and with the most important reference missing. On the credit side, **Claw-Eval at 56.8% is present** from the benchmark's own leaderboard, satisfying a requirement the methodology explicitly penalises models for lacking; **τ²-bench at 83.9% is well above the Tau-family reference**; and **τ³-bench 68.4%, MCP-Tasks 74.2% and WideResearch 74.0%** suggest competence across a range of tool orchestration tasks. Four deductions. **No GDPval-AA figure exists** — the methodology's principal measure of economically valuable real-world agentic work is simply absent, so the most consequential question about agentic value cannot be answered. **Terminal-Bench 2.0 at 52.5% is 35.5 points below the anchor**, with no newer version measured. **No AA Agentic Index, no OSWorld and no CyberGym** exist either. And **the great majority of rows come from Qwen's own comparison table in which this model serves as the cheaper baseline**, so they are vendor-framed rather than independently established — and **QwenClawBench at 51.8% is a vendor-proprietary benchmark with no external baseline**, so it is recorded but not credited.
- **Reasoning: 62/100.** The knowledge and mathematics results are genuinely strong; the frontier-reasoning and reliability results are poor, and the two halves do not reconcile. **MMLU-Pro at 87.8% and MMLU-Redux at 94.9%** are strong, **C-Eval at 93%** is strong, and the competition-mathematics battery — **AIME26 93.3%, HMMT Feb 2025 94.8%, HMMT Nov 2025 92.7%, HMMT Feb 2026 87.9%** — is impressive **for a model the tracker classifies as Non-Reasoning**. Against that: **HLE misses the 40%+ reference on both measurements (28.7% vendor, 19.8% independent)**; **GPQA misses the 90%+ reference on both** (88.4% vendor, 86.1% independent); **CritPt is 0.9%**, effectively zero; **the Intelligence Index is 21.4%**, 38.6 points below the 60+ marker; and **the AA-Omniscience Index is strongly negative at −37.9% on just 24.5% accuracy with an 82.7% hallucination rate**, so it is neither knowledgeable in the AA-Omniscience sense nor well-calibrated. **AA-IFBench at 51.6% against the vendor's IFEval of 92.6% is a 41-point divergence** between two instruction-following measures, which is difficult to reconcile and suggests the vendor figure is measuring something narrower. **Neither ARC-AGI nor FrontierMath is measured**, so abstract reasoning and research mathematics are uncheckable. The pattern — strong on saturated knowledge and competition benchmarks, weak on the hardest frontier references and on calibration — is consistent with a prior-generation non-reasoning model.
- **Context window: 72/100.** **128,000 tokens** places it in the methodology's 100K–200K band (50–64), and it is set at the top of that band because the depth evidence, while modest, is measured rather than asserted: **AA-LCR at 64.3%, LongBench v2 at 63.2% and AI-Needle at 68.7%** all point to usable but unremarkable long-context behaviour, and **AA-LCR at 64.3% matches Grok 4.3's figure exactly** at the same nominal capability level. It goes no higher because **128K is simply small for a 397B flagship in the current field** — every current-family model assessed in this pass offers at least 200K and most offer 1M — and because **all three long-context figures land in the low-to-mid 60s**, which is weak at absolute level. For a model whose selling point was long-context processing, that is a material limitation, and it is compounded by the absence of any MRCR, RULER or GraphWalks measurement.
- **Multimodal: 74/100.** **Text, image and video input → text output** places it in the methodology's "+video in = 75–90" band on the input surface alone, and video comprehension is claimed via VideoMMMU. It is scored **below the band floor of 75, and the reason is the most important finding in this dimension: the vendor and independent measurements diverge by more than 26 points.** Qwen reports **MMMU-Pro at 79%**, while Artificial Analysis measures **AA-MMMU-Pro at 52.7%** on the same benchmark — **the largest vendor-independent multimodal gap recorded anywhere in this research pass** — and the vendor's other visual figures are similarly high (**MathVision 88.6%, V\* 95.8%, VideoMMMU 84.7%, CharXiv 80.8%**) with no independent corroboration for any of them. When a vendor's own figure is 26 points above the only independent measurement on the same benchmark, the honest treatment is to discount heavily rather than to average, and the score is placed accordingly, just under the band floor. **No OCR, document or audio benchmark exists**, and no audio input path is documented. The score credits a verified multi-modal *input surface* while explicitly declining to credit the claimed level of *quality* — a distinction that matters more here than for any other model in this pass.
- **Coding: 70/100.** Mid-field on the figures that exist, with both composite measures missing. **SWE-bench Verified at 76.2% and LiveCodeBench v6 at 83.6%** are decent, though **both are the vendor's own numbers and neither has an independent counterpart** — unlike, for example, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite, where Vals AI independently measured SWE-bench at 75.0%. **SWE-bench Pro at 50.9%** is mid-field. Five deductions: **no AA Coding Index and no AA-SciCode exist**, so both of the methodology's composite coding measures are absent — every other model assessed in this pass with coding claims carries at least one; **no DeepSWE**, which is the frontier repository-engineering reference; **no Terminal-Bench 2.1**; and **no CursorBench, Codeforces, NL2Repo or ProgramBench**. The dimension rests on two uncorroborated vendor figures plus one mid-field one, which is a thin basis for a 397B model.
- **Cost efficiency: 62/100.** The case rests on open weights at **397B total / 17B active**, which is genuinely tractable to self-host — far more so than GLM-5.3's dense 753B or DeepSeek V4 Pro's undisclosed scale — and the sparsity means low per-token inference cost on owned hardware. That is the whole argument in favour, and it is a real one: free inference on infrastructure you already own is the cheapest form of access. Five deductions pull it well below the midpoint. **No licence was stated in any source read**, and Alibaba's Qwen open-weight releases vary between Apache-2.0 and the more restrictive Qwen Community licence — so the legal basis for commercial self-hosting is unresolved, which is a prerequisite rather than a detail. **No hosted rate card was found**, so there is no managed option to price against and no fallback if self-hosting proves impractical. **The model is deeply superseded within its own family**, with eight newer Alibaba models ranking above it, including **Qwen3.8-Flash-Next at 64.11 — 10.5 points higher — which is also open-weight, multimodal and listed at just $0.12/$0.40 with a *larger* 262K native context.** Paying the operational cost of standing up a 397B MoE to obtain weaker results than a cheaper, smaller, open-weight sibling is hard to justify. **The 128K context** is small for the workload class a 397B model targets. And **the unresolved multimodal divergence** means part of what you would be self-hosting may not perform as advertised.
- **Overall Score: 68.8/100.** Mean of the five non-cost dimensions (66 + 62 + 72 + 74 + 70) / 5 = 68.8 — **a prior-generation open-weight flagship whose strongest evidence is on saturated benchmarks and whose weakest is on the frontier references that now matter, with one unusual caveat attached.** What holds up: **MMLU-Pro at 87.8%, MMLU-Redux 94.9% and C-Eval 93%**; a strong competition-mathematics battery across four HMMT variants and AIME; **τ²-bench at 83.9%** with **Claw-Eval at 56.8%** and broad tool-orchestration coverage; and **397B/17B-active sparsity** that makes self-hosting tractable. Three findings should govern adoption. **The multimodal claim is unreliable in a specific, measurable way: Qwen reports MMMU-Pro at 79% while Artificial Analysis measures 52.7% — a 26.3-point divergence, the largest vendor-independent multimodal gap in this research pass**, with no independent corroboration for any of the vendor's other high visual figures. **Reliability is poor**: a −37.9% Omniscience Index on 24.5% accuracy and an 82.7% hallucination rate, with HLE, GPQA, CritPt and the Intelligence Index all well below reference. And **it is comprehensively superseded within its own vendor**: eight newer Alibaba models rank above it, and **Qwen3.8-Flash-Next scores 10.5 composite points higher, is open-weight and multimodal, lists at $0.12/$0.40, and offers a larger 262K native context.** Choose it only if you already run this generation and need the specific knowledge or mathematics profile, or as a self-hosting exercise where the sparse architecture appeals. **For new work, Alibaba's own newer and cheaper open-weight models are the better choice.**
